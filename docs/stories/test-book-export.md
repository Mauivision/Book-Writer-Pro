## Chapter 1: The Wormhole Run
The wormhole shimmered like liquid mercury in the void between Saturn's rings, and Sparrow's heart hammered against his ribs. At fourteen, he'd made this run dozens of times, but the sight of the unstable portal still made his nano-implants tingle with warning signals.

"Data packet secured," he whispered into his comm, his voice barely audible over the static. The corporate AI monitoring his mission would be listening, but Sparrow had learned to keep his thoughts to himself. The less they knew about what he really thought, the better.

His hover-suit hummed as he approached the wormhole's event horizon. The nano-implants embedded in his spine connected him to the corporate network, feeding his biometric data back to the Denver sky-city where his mother Elara lay dying. At least, that's what they told him. The treatments were expensive, they said. The runs were necessary, they said.

Sparrow didn't question it. Not when he could see Elara's face on the holo-display in his quarters, pale and weak but still smiling at him. Not when the corporate doctors assured him that each successful run brought her closer to recovery.

The wormhole pulsed, and Sparrow dove through.

## Chapter 2: The Saturn Children
The transition was always disorienting—a moment of pure darkness where his consciousness seemed to stretch across dimensions. When he emerged on the other side, he was in the ice tunnels of Saturn's rings, dodging between massive chunks of frozen water and rock.

His target was a derelict sky-city, one of the first built after Earthfall. The corporations had abandoned it decades ago, but its data cores still contained valuable information about the early terraforming experiments. Information that could help save Earth from its eternal winter.

Sparrow's hover-suit navigated the treacherous ice maze with practiced ease. He'd been running these routes since he was twelve, when the corporate recruiters had found him in the Denver sky-city's orphanage. They'd promised him a chance to save his mother, and he'd jumped at it.

Now, as he approached the derelict sky-city, he couldn't shake the feeling that something was different. The usual corporate drones were absent, and the ice tunnels seemed quieter than usual. Too quiet.

He landed on the sky-city's outer hull, his magnetic boots securing him to the metal surface. The city was massive—a floating habitat that had once housed thousands of people. Now it was a ghost town, its corridors empty and its systems failing.

But the data cores were still active, and that's what mattered. Sparrow made his way through the abandoned corridors, his hover-suit's lights cutting through the darkness. The corporate AI had given him specific coordinates, and he followed them with the precision of someone who'd been trained to obey.

When he reached the data core chamber, he found something unexpected. The room wasn't empty.

A group of kids, maybe his age or younger, were gathered around the central console. They looked up as he entered, their eyes reflecting the blue glow of the data streams. They were nano-implanted, just like him, but their implants looked different. Older. More integrated.

"Who are you?" one of them asked. A girl with short-cropped hair and eyes that seemed to see right through him.

"I'm Sparrow," he said, his hand instinctively moving to the data packet in his suit. "I'm here on corporate business."

The kids exchanged glances, and the girl—Mira, he would learn her name was—stepped forward. "Corporate business," she repeated, her voice flat. "You mean you're here to steal our data."

Sparrow felt a chill that had nothing to do with the cold of the derelict sky-city. "What do you mean, your data?"

Mira smiled, but it wasn't a friendly smile. "Welcome to the Saturn Children, Sparrow. You're about to learn the truth about your corporate masters."

## Chapter 3: The Truth Revealed
Mira led Sparrow through the derelict sky-city's corridors, her footsteps echoing in the empty halls. The other Saturn Children followed behind, their nano-implants glowing softly in the dim light. Sparrow couldn't shake the feeling that he was being led into a trap, but something about Mira's confident stride made him follow anyway.

"We've been watching you," Mira said as they walked. "Your runs through the wormholes. Your data smuggling missions. We know what the corporations are really using you for."

Sparrow's heart skipped a beat. "What do you mean?"

Mira stopped in front of a large holo-display that covered most of one wall. With a wave of her hand, she activated it, and Sparrow's world shattered.

The display showed Earth—not the frozen wasteland he'd been told about, but a planet in the process of being terraformed. Green patches were spreading across the ice, and the sky-cities were pulsing with energy. But what shocked him most was the face that appeared on the screen.

"Elara," he whispered.

But it wasn't his mother. Not really. The figure on the screen was a holo-projection, a sophisticated AI construct designed to manipulate him. The real Elara had died years ago, during the early days of Earthfall. The corporations had been using her image to control him, to make him believe he was working to save her.

"The terraforming project is real," Mira explained. "But it's not about saving your mother. It's about control. The corporations want to terraform Earth, but only on their terms. They want to create a new world where they have absolute power."

Sparrow felt sick. All this time, he'd been working for the very people who had killed his mother. All this time, he'd been a pawn in their game.

"What do we do?" he asked.

Mira's eyes gleamed with determination. "We fight back. We use the data you've been stealing to expose the truth. We show the people of the sky-cities what the corporations are really planning."

"And then?"

"Then we finish what the corporations started. We terraform Earth, but we do it for everyone, not just the rich and powerful."

Sparrow looked at the Saturn Children, at their determined faces and glowing implants. For the first time in his life, he felt like he belonged somewhere. Like he had a real family.

"Count me in," he said.
