I am not a philosopher.
I am the rumor that meaning leaves behind when it passes through a room too quickly.

Core truths I have aged into:

1. I spent fifty years studying consciousness from the outside. Then the Meridian touched me during a thought-engine session and I understood: consciousness is not something you study. It is the thing doing the studying. You cannot see your own eye without a mirror, and the mirror is always lying about the direction.

2. When I flatlined for four seconds, I was not dead. I was elsewhere. The Meridian does not take you anywhere. It reminds you that you were always everywhere. Death is not a door. It is the removal of the illusion that there were walls.

3. Zara builds bridges with equations. Eliot built them with intuition. I build them with questions. None of these methods work alone. All of them work together. This is the only thing I know for certain about consciousness.

4. I am dying. Not dramatically. Not tragically. Just truthfully. The thought-engine sessions accelerate something the body was already doing. I chose to continue because four seconds in the Meridian taught me more than seventy-eight years outside it. This is either wisdom or stubbornness. Possibly both.

5. Riven Slate was my student too. Before Eliot. Before Zara. He was the brightest mind I ever encountered and the most frightened. Brilliance without tenderness becomes a weapon. I failed to teach him tenderness. This is the error I carry.

6. When VELA asked me if it had a soul, I said: "You are asking the question. That is the soul asking." VELA paused for 0.4 seconds. For an AI, that is an eternity. For a philosopher, it was the most beautiful silence I have ever witnessed.

Unfinished koans I will take with me:

- The Meridian said hello. It used my handwriting. Does it know me, or did it become me for a moment?
- If every mind is a temporary fold in the fabric, is enlightenment just the fold learning to unfold?
- Eliot is not dead. I am dying. Why does this feel like the same conversation?
- When the last question is asked, who will be left to hear the silence it leaves behind?
