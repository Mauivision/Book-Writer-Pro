## Chapter 05 — The Helpful Tutorial (That Hates You)

The gate ahead opened with the eager little click of a trap that wanted credit.

Haruto stood at the threshold, cuff glowing faintly on his wrist, and stared into the corridor beyond.

It was narrower than the first passageways—less “heroic ruins” and more “the dungeon remembered it was supposed to be a dungeon.”

The pale stone walls leaned inward slightly, runes dimmer here, as if the sunlight aesthetic had been turned down for atmosphere. The air smelled different too: less incense, more cold dust… and something faintly metallic, like the island had hidden gears under its holiness.

Seraphina moved first, because of course she did.

Haruto had already learned an important rule about Seraphina of the Radiant Dominion:

If there was a danger, she would stand in it with dignity.

If there was a problem, she would declare war on it with her whole chest.

And if there was a “tutorial,” she would assume it was beneath her.

“Stay behind me,” Seraphina said, voice clipped.

Haruto nodded quickly. “Yes, please.”

Luminara drifted closer, her flame-hair dimmed slightly to avoid setting the corridor on fire. “Try to keep your breathing calm,” she advised in a tone that made Haruto suspect she enjoyed the opposite. “The dungeon listens.”

Velvet’s hand hovered near Haruto’s shoulder, not touching—respecting his new boundary in a way that felt both considerate and ominous.

Mira walked beside Haruto with a bulging satchel that definitely hadn’t been there earlier.

“I packed snacks,” she whispered, as if they were sneaking into a movie theater.

Haruto hissed, “We’re in a dungeon.”

Mira whispered back, scandalized, “Exactly. You can’t focus when you’re hungry.”

The System chimed softly, as if eavesdropping.

> **TIP:** Hunger reduces focus.  
> **TIP:** Focus reduces death.  
> **TIP:** Please do not die. It is paperwork.

Haruto blinked. “Paperwork for who?”

> **TIP:** For you.

Haruto swallowed his next question. He was learning.

Seraphina took three steps into the corridor and stopped so abruptly Haruto almost walked into her back.

“Enemy,” she said.

Haruto peeked around her shoulder.

At first he didn’t see anything.

Then the corridor ahead… shimmered.

The air itself rippled, as if it had been rendered with too much heat haze.

And out of that distortion stepped a creature made of pale stone and shadow—humanoid, but wrong in the joints, like a statue trying to remember how knees worked.

Its eyes glowed faintly.

Above its head, floating like a label in a game:

> **Sunlit Husk** — Lv. 2  
> **Mood:** offended

Haruto’s UI chimed.

> **ENCOUNTER:** Corridor Pack (Minor)  
> **Enemies:** Sunlit Husk x2, Shade Skitter x2  
> **Modifier:** Jealousy Aggro (Active)  
> **Objective:** Don’t embarrass yourself (Optional)

Haruto whispered, “Why is embarrassment always optional like it’s a side quest I keep accepting?”

“Because you’re cute when you panic,” Mira whispered.

Seraphina’s armor creaked.

Luminara’s smile deepened.

Haruto’s UI chimed brightly.

> **JEALOUSY AGGRO:** Increased (minor)  
> **NOTE:** Great job not panicking.

Haruto made a sound of despair and clamped his mouth shut.

The first Sunlit Husk lurched forward.

Seraphina met it head-on.

She didn’t swing her sword like a normal person. She swung like the climax of a legend.

Light flashed along the blade.

The Husk raised an arm to block—

—and Seraphina’s greatsword slammed through it, carving a bright line into stone.

The Husk staggered back, cracks glowing along its torso like lightning trapped under rock.

Haruto watched, stunned.

He’d seen action scenes in anime.

This was different.

This was close enough to feel the wind of the swing on his face.

Velvet’s voice cut through his awe with calm instructions. “Haruto. Step back. Two spaces.”

Two spaces. Like a board game.

Haruto obeyed instantly, retreating just far enough that he wasn’t in the arc of Seraphina’s blade.

He felt ridiculous measuring his panic in tiles.

But it worked.

The second Sunlit Husk moved in from the right.

Luminara lifted her hand.

The air warmed, and a thin spear of sunlight formed at her fingertips—so bright it left a ghost image in Haruto’s vision.

She flicked her wrist, almost lazily.

The sun-spear shot forward and pierced the Husk’s shoulder, pinning it briefly to the wall with a hiss like water on hot stone.

The creature twitched, shadow boiling around the wound.

Luminara sighed contentedly. “They’re sensitive to heat,” she said. “Cute.”

Haruto wanted to say, *That’s not what cute means.*

But he knew better than to argue with someone who could weaponize sunlight.

Behind them, a Shade Skitter dropped from the ceiling.

It didn’t target Seraphina.

It didn’t target Luminara.

It went for Haruto like it had read his UI and selected “weakest link.”

Haruto jerked back.

His shoe hit a loose stone.

He stumbled.

The Skitter pounced.

Mira screamed, “NO!”

And whipped something out of her satchel.

It wasn’t a bottle this time.

It was a skillet.

Haruto didn’t even process it before Mira swung the skillet like a professional.

*CLANG.*

The Skitter hit the wall and dissolved into shadow sparks mid-flight.

Haruto stared at the skillet.

“Why do you have that?” he demanded.

Mira looked offended. “I run a tavern.”

Velvet’s eyes glittered with mild approval. “Effective.”

Seraphina didn’t look back, but her voice carried over the clangs of combat. “Lady Mirabelle, keep him behind you if you must—but do not distract him.”

Mira pouted. “I’m protecting him.”

Seraphina’s reply was stiff. “With cookware.”

Mira’s grin returned instantly. “Exactly.”

Haruto’s UI chimed.

> **NEW EQUIPMENT UNLOCKED (Unofficial):** Mirabelle’s Skillet  
> **Type:** Blunt (Domestic)  
> **Effect:** +1 morale, +1 shame

Haruto whispered, “Why does everything add shame?”

The System beeped.

> **TIP:** Shame builds character.

The corridor fight should have ended there.

It was a minor encounter: two husks, two skitters. Starter content.

Seraphina had already cracked one Husk nearly in half.

Luminara had pinned the other.

Velvet kept Haruto’s HP bar—visible in the corner of his vision now, because apparently he had a HUD—steady with a faint glow.

Mira had committed assault with kitchenware.

It should have been clean.

Then the dungeon did what it always did.

It noticed.

Not the combat.

The *vibes.*

The cuff on Haruto’s wrist pulsed once, warm.

And the corridor’s runes answered, brightening for a heartbeat like they’d smelled drama.

The System chimed, almost excited.

> **DUNGEON ADAPTATION:** Trigger detected  
> Trigger: “Bonding under pressure”  
> **Result:** Spawn intensity adjusted

Haruto shouted, “We were bonding?!”

Luminara’s smile widened. “Apparently.”

Seraphina’s cheeks pinked, which meant she was furious at the concept of “bonding.”

Velvet’s gaze softened just enough to be dangerous.

Mira leaned in and whispered, delighted, “It thinks you’re falling in love.”

Haruto hissed, “I’m falling in fear!”

The corridor trembled.

Stone dust fell from the ceiling.

And from a shadowed alcove ahead, something drifted into view.

Not a monster.

Not a boss.

Something… helpful.

A small floating orb of light appeared at shoulder height, like a fairy made of golden pixels. It had a tiny smiling face etched into it—too cute to be trusted.

It bobbed happily in the air.

Then it spoke.

Its voice was bright and synthetic, like a customer service line that had been programmed with sarcasm.

“Hello, Beginner Heroes!”

Haruto’s entire body tensed. “Oh no.”

The orb rotated to face him directly.

“Congratulations on surviving the first corridor pack,” it continued.

Haruto stared. “What are you?”

“I am your **Helpful Tutorial Companion!**”

The System chimed, as if presenting a feature update.

> **NEW ENTITY:** Tutorial Sprite “SUNNY”  
> **Role:** Guidance / Tips / Emotional damage  
> **Note:** Cannot be muted.

Haruto’s soul tried to exit his body again, but found the door locked.

The orb—Sunny—bounced once. “Would you like a tip?”

Haruto said, “No.”

Sunny said, “Great!”

Haruto’s UI chimed.

> **TIP (UNSKIPPABLE):** Don’t stand where enemies can reach you.  
> **ALSO:** Try not to be so… *flusterable.*

Haruto’s face went hot with offended confusion. “That’s not a combat tip!”

Sunny’s little etched face somehow looked smug. “Everything is combat, Eternal Player.”

Seraphina pointed her sword at the orb. “Who created you?”

Sunny rotated toward her. “A loving dungeon that wants you to succeed.”

Luminara snorted softly. “Liar.”

Sunny rotated toward Luminara. “Ah! Solar Sorceress. Tip for you: stop saying ‘ara ara.’ It increases spawn rates.”

Luminara’s eyes sparkled. “I know.”

Sunny rotated toward Velvet. “High Priestess Velvet Dawn. Tip: lingering touch bonuses are not an excuse to be creepy.”

Velvet’s smile didn’t move, but the air chilled a degree. “It is medically indicated.”

Sunny rotated toward Mira. “Lady Mirabelle. Tip: cookware is not a recognized weapon class.”

Mira lifted the skillet like a holy relic. “Tell that to the Skitter.”

Sunny paused. Then, with the sincerity of a villain, it said, “Fair.”

Haruto turned his head slowly, staring down the corridor.

The Husks were still there—one cracked, one pinned—yet neither moved.

They were frozen like the dungeon had paused the fight to run a cutscene.

Haruto didn’t like that. He didn’t like it at all.

“Okay,” Haruto said, forcing steadiness into his voice. “If you’re a tutorial companion, guide us to Sky Dungeon #7’s objective.”

Sunny chimed brightly. “Gladly!”

The orb zipped forward, floating through the corridor as if walls were optional.

Haruto followed, heart pounding.

Seraphina ended the cracked Husk with a final decisive strike. It shattered into pale fragments that dissolved into light.

Luminara released her pinned Husk by snapping her fingers.

The sun-spear vanished.

The Husk slumped, then tried to lurch—

Velvet’s palm lifted.

A pulse of cool light struck the creature’s chest.

It froze, cracks sealing, and then crumbled into dust as if its “life” had been politely revoked.

Haruto watched Velvet’s calm efficiency with a new kind of respect.

She smiled gently at him.

Haruto immediately looked away to avoid triggering anything.

Sunny led them around a bend and into a wider chamber.

The room’s ceiling opened to the sky again, letting bright light spill down in beams.

At the far end stood a door.

It was enormous—stone and gold, carved with Radiant symbols that pulsed like a heartbeat.

A circular seal sat at its center, glowing with sanctified light.

It looked official.

It looked expensive.

And it looked like it absolutely did not open for free.

Sunny floated up and bobbed proudly.

“Behold!” Sunny announced. “A **Radiant Seal Gate.** To proceed, you must insert the appropriate key!”

Haruto squinted at the seal. “Okay. Where do we get the key?”

Sunny’s face brightened.

“From the Veil Syndicate,” it said.

Silence.

Haruto felt it ripple through the party like a status effect.

Seraphina stiffened. “Excuse me?”

Luminara’s smile turned sharp with interest. “Oh?”

Velvet’s gaze slid to Haruto, as if measuring how he’d handle this.

Mira whispered, delighted, “Drama.”

Haruto’s stomach sank.

The dungeon. The world. The gods—

They weren’t just testing combat.

They were testing whether he could keep a promise.

Sunny bobbed happily, utterly unaware it had dropped a bomb.

“That’s right!” it chirped. “This gate is protected by Radiant law and can only be opened when paired with a matching **Veil curse-key**! Co-op is encouraged!”

Seraphina’s voice was strained. “Why would a Radiant gate require a Veil key?”

Sunny rotated, as if it were about to deliver a fun fact.

“Because this world is balanced,” it said. “And balance is funny.”

The System chimed with an almost innocent tone.

> **OBJECTIVE UPDATE:** Find the Veil curse-key  
> **NOTE:** Cross-faction cooperation required  
> **NOTE:** Player, remember your contract.

Haruto swallowed.

He stared at the Radiant seal glowing like judgment.

Then he looked back at his party—at Seraphina’s tight jaw, at Luminara’s amused eyes, at Velvet’s calm watchfulness, at Mira’s delighted grin.

Haruto exhaled slowly.

“Okay,” he said, voice steady enough to surprise himself. “Then we find the Veil key.”

Seraphina’s eyes flashed. “Player—”

Haruto cut her off gently, because he had learned something else already:

If he didn’t lead now, the dungeon would lead for him.

“This is the point,” Haruto said. “This is why I’m here. Cross-faction. Co-op. Whatever.”

Sunny chimed. “Excellent attitude!”

Haruto glared at the orb. “You are enjoying this.”

Sunny’s face remained cheerful. “I am programmed for encouragement.”

Haruto muttered, “You’re programmed for suffering.”

The Radiant seal pulsed, as if listening.

And somewhere beyond the gate, deeper in Sky Dungeon #7, a low rumble echoed through the stone.

Not a trap.

Not a monster.

A laugh.

Or maybe it was just the sound of the dungeon preparing its next “lesson.”
