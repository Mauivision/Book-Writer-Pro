## Chapter 17 — Verdant Interrupt

Haruto had a lot of experience with strangers in Lumora.

He had experience with dramatic strangers.
Flirty strangers.
Edgy strangers who broke chandeliers as a conversation starter.

He did *not* have experience with the kind of stranger who looked at his wrist like she was reading a hazard sign.

Elara Sylvandrel stood in the corridor as if Sky Dungeon #7 had been built to accommodate her.

She wasn’t trying to be impressive.

Which, unfortunately, made her impressive.

Moonlight cut across her armor in quiet bands—leather and metal worked together like a compromise, not a costume. The shape of it made sense: flexible at the joints, reinforced where a blade would glance, worn in the places that meant it had been used.

Her cloak hung heavy, deep green-black in the dim, the color of forest shade at noon.
It didn’t flutter for drama.
It settled.

Haruto’s brain tried to categorize her like the System wanted him to categorize everything.

**Class:** ???  
**Faction:** ???  
**Threat Level:** yes

His HUD refused to help.

It didn’t label her.
It didn’t offer a tip.
It didn’t even insult him for being confused.

That silence was the loudest thing in the room.

Seraphina broke it first, because Seraphina could not allow silence to win a scene.

“State your allegiance,” she demanded, greatsword angled downward but ready.

Bone Legion Commander didn’t speak.
He didn’t need to.

The shadows around his boots tightened into thin, patient lines—tethers with the manners of a noose.

Luminara floated a fraction higher, golden flame hair dimmed to a steady burn—less flirting, more furnace.

Velvet stood close enough to Haruto that her presence read as both support and control.
Her hand didn’t touch him.
But Haruto felt the intention of it, a healer’s readiness to anchor a panic spiral before it could become damage.

Mira hugged the Sunny-box like a hostage situation.

From inside: one indignant muffled chirp.

Mira whispered, “No.”

Elara’s gaze swept them all, not like a judge, but like a soldier counting exits.

When her eyes returned to Haruto’s wrist, the air felt colder.

“Your dungeon is not the most dangerous thing in this corridor,” Elara said.

Haruto’s mouth went dry. “Cool. Love that.”

Seraphina snapped, “You’re avoiding the question.”

Elara’s expression didn’t change, but her eyes sharpened.

“I am avoiding your *framework,*” she corrected. “You see two factions and call that the world. You see Light and Shadow and call it truth.”

Her gaze flicked briefly to Bone Legion Commander.

“Shadow is not evil,” she said, voice flat. “And Light is not safe.”

Seraphina’s jaw tightened like she’d been slapped.

Luminara’s lips curved, almost pleased. “Careful,” she murmured. “You’ll make her think.”

Seraphina hissed, “I am always thinking.”

The floor pulse that followed was so faint Haruto almost missed it.

Almost.

His UI chimed immediately.

> **JEALOUSY AGGRO:** increased (minor)  
> **Note:** Posturing is a spawn trigger.

Haruto pinched the bridge of his nose. “Of course it is.”

Elara tilted her head slightly.

“It speaks to you,” she observed.

Haruto froze. “It… what?”

Velvet’s gaze snapped to Elara. “You can see his interface?”

Elara’s expression flickered—something like surprise, quickly smothered.

“Not all of it,” she admitted. “But I can feel the *pressure* of it. The way the world leans toward his decisions.”

Her eyes narrowed, and the cyan tab in Haruto’s peripheral vision pulsed in response like it had been called by name.

Haruto swallowed.
He didn’t open it.

He didn’t want to see a “Codex” entry titled: **You’re About to Ruin Everything.**

Bone Legion Commander’s voice cut in, cold and precise. “Explain your interest in the Player.”

Mira whispered, delighted, “Oh my god.”

Elara looked at the commander with an expression that suggested she respected his tone, even if she didn’t like him.

“Because your Player is wearing a world’s emergency mechanism like jewelry,” Elara said.

The sentence landed in the corridor with the weight of a dropped anvil.

Seraphina’s eyes widened. “Heretic.”

Luminara’s smile thinned. “Interesting.”

Velvet’s calm didn’t break, but Haruto felt it tighten around him like a brace.

Mira mouthed silently, *what.*

From inside the box: a smug muffled chirp. *Mmph!*

Haruto stared at Elara. “Okay. You’re going to have to—”

Elara cut him off.

Not with a raised voice.
With timing.

Her head snapped toward the corridor ahead.
Her hand went to the hilt at her hip—not a flourish, a reflex.

The dungeon hum deepened.

Haruto felt it in his teeth.

The air ahead trembled, and the mist—*mist,* which had been minding its own business—began to spiral as if someone was stirring it with a spoon.

Velvet’s voice went quiet. “That’s not a spawn vent.”

Bone Legion Commander’s shadow threads twitched, seeking anchors.

Luminara’s heat rippled outward, testing the air.

Seraphina shifted her stance, shield-line instinct returning.

Elara didn’t look at Haruto.
She didn’t look at Seraphina.

She looked at the space between this room and the next like she expected it to lie.

“It’s coming,” Elara said.

Haruto’s stomach dropped. “What is?”

Elara finally met his eyes.

Up close, he saw it clearly: emerald irises with faint cyan flecks, like someone had scattered tiny shards of sky-glass into green.

“A proof,” she said. “A consequence. A correction.”

Her gaze dropped to his wrist.

“Your world does not like being rewritten,” Elara added. “And whatever is inside that cuff—”

The cyan tab pulsed.

Haruto’s skin prickled.

“—is not as asleep as you think.”

Haruto tried to breathe through the sudden, stupid fear that he’d been “chosen” by something he didn’t understand.

He managed, barely, to get one sentence out.

“Okay,” Haruto said, voice tight. “Then help us. Or leave.”

Elara’s lips twitched—almost a smile.

“That,” she said, “was the first smart thing you’ve said since I started watching you.”

Seraphina snapped, “You were watching us?”

Elara didn’t deny it.

She stepped forward, taking the lead position like she’d always belonged there.

“Stay within two tiles,” she said, eyes on the trembling mist ahead. “Your dungeon likes separation.”

Haruto blinked. “How do you—”

Elara’s gaze slid sideways, brief and sharp. “Because it’s an old trick.”

She lifted her sword.

The aetherite in its core didn’t flare gold or violet.

It glowed with a deep verdant resonance, as if the blade had remembered forests that existed before factions had names.

Then the corridor ahead *opened*—

—not with a door,
but with a tear in the air, thin as a seam, bright as polished brass.

For a heartbeat, Haruto thought he saw a figure in the tear: split gold and violet, one eye sun, one eye void—

But the image was gone before his mind could grab it.

Only the laughter lingered, faint as a UI flicker.

Haruto’s HUD flashed a single line, small and cyan.

> **ROUTE UPDATE:** Verdant Interrupt — Active

Mira whispered, scandalized and thrilled, “We got a route.”

Velvet murmured, “Haruto… don’t.”

Haruto didn’t even know what “don’t” meant anymore.

He tightened his grip on the Sunny-box handle Mira had improvised with twine because apparently they were the kind of party that used tutorial entities as weapons and luggage.

He looked at Elara.

And for the first time since he’d arrived in Lumora, he felt like he’d met someone who wasn’t trying to own him.

Someone who was trying to warn him.

Which, in this world, might be the most romantic thing that could happen without triggering a spawn.
