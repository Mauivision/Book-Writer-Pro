## Chapter 22 — Velvet’s Checkup (Soft Power)

Velvet waited until the betting board went up.

Not because she cared about wagers.

Because she cared about thresholds.

She watched the Golden Tankard like a physician watches a fever chart: not for drama, but for the moment the numbers stopped being funny.

Haruto didn’t notice her decision until she was already beside him.

Not grabbing.

Not demanding.

Just… present.

“Haruto,” Velvet said quietly.

Haruto looked up, still half in the fog of rumor combat and Mira’s chalkboard warfare. “Yeah?”

Velvet’s expression didn’t change. “You’re coming with me.”

Haruto blinked. “Is that a request?”

Velvet’s gaze softened by a fraction. “It’s a checkup.”

Haruto’s mouth opened.

His HUD chimed before he could argue.

> **NOTICE:** Support role intervention detected.  
> **Tip:** Compliance improves survival odds.

Haruto stared at the tip like it had personally betrayed him.

“I hate it here,” he whispered.

Velvet’s lips twitched. “Yes.”

She didn’t drag him.

She didn’t have to.

The way she said *checkup* made it sound like refusing would be childish, and Haruto hated that his body responded to that tone the way it responded to a seatbelt: reluctant, but relieved.

Mira noticed immediately, because Mira noticed everything.

“Oh!” Mira chirped, voice bright as a knife. “Private healing scene?”

Seraphina made a strangled sound.

Luminara’s smile widened. “Mm.”

Ecto gasped. “Bond event!”

Chiller Executioner said, “Do I get to nap?”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once. “This is prudent.”

Elara’s eyes narrowed slightly, but she didn’t interfere.

Velvet didn’t dignify Mira’s teasing with an answer.

She simply guided Haruto toward the back hallway, through a door that looked like storage and smelled like herbs and clean linen.

The noise of the tavern faded behind them.

The air cooled.

The light softened.

For the first time in days, Haruto felt like the world wasn’t leaning forward with its mouth open, waiting to bite.

Velvet’s room wasn’t a “chamber” in the dramatic sense.

It was a practical space that had been turned gentle on purpose.

A small table.

A chair.

A basin of water that steamed faintly.

Shelves lined with vials and wrapped bundles of dried plants.

On the wall, tucked behind a hanging cloth, Haruto spotted a tidy rack of sealed envelopes—different colors, different seals.

Not mail.

Favors.

Velvet closed the door.

The latch clicked.

Haruto’s HUD flickered.

The public-mode restraint lifted like a hand letting go of his throat.

The UI brightened slightly.

Not cheerful.

Just… honest.

> **PRIVATE SPACE DETECTED:** Reduced witness factor  
> **Tip:** You may panic at normal volume

Haruto let out a laugh that came out wrong.

Velvet didn’t laugh.

She gestured to the chair. “Sit.”

Haruto sat.

Velvet washed her hands first.

Not because she needed to.

Because the ritual mattered.

Haruto watched the way her fingers moved—steady, sure, careful. The kind of care that didn’t ask permission but also didn’t steal it.

When she finished, she sat across from him.

Close enough to feel like intimacy.

Far enough to feel like professionalism.

“All right,” Velvet said softly. “Tell me what hurts.”

Haruto opened his mouth and then realized he didn’t know how to answer that question anymore.

His wrist hurt.

His chest hurt.

His reputation hurt.

His name hurt, every time someone said it like it belonged to them.

He swallowed.

“Everything,” he admitted.

Velvet nodded as if that was a normal symptom.

“That’s not a joke,” Haruto added quickly, because he didn’t want the System to interpret it as comedy.

Velvet’s gaze held his. “I know.”

She reached toward his wrist and paused.

Haruto’s breath hitched.

Not fear of Velvet.

Fear of what touch meant in Lumora.

Velvet noticed.

She didn’t withdraw.

She didn’t push.

She asked, quietly, “May I?”

Haruto blinked.

Consent felt foreign here.

It felt like a relic from Earth.

He nodded once. “Yeah.”

Velvet’s fingers closed gently around Haruto’s wrist.

The cuff warmed.

Not cyan.

Not gold.

Warm like it recognized a stabilizer.

Velvet’s eyes narrowed slightly, focusing on something Haruto couldn’t see.

Her thumb pressed lightly against the underside of the cuff—not testing a clasp, not trying to remove it.

Listening.

Haruto’s HUD chimed.

> **HEALER DIAGNOSTIC:** Active  
> **Note:** Stop clenching your jaw

Haruto realized his teeth were grinding and forced himself to stop.

Velvet’s voice stayed calm. “Your stress is high,” she said.

Haruto snorted once. “No way.”

Velvet didn’t smile.

She was past humor.

“Your unity output is also high,” she continued, and Haruto hated that she phrased his relationships like a power meter.

“And,” Velvet added, quiet now, “there is an additional signal.”

Haruto’s throat tightened. “Cyan.”

Velvet’s gaze flicked up to his face.

“Yes,” she said. “Cyan.”

Haruto swallowed.

“Is that… bad?” he asked.

Velvet didn’t answer immediately.

She released his wrist and reached for a small vial on the shelf.

She uncorked it.

The scent was clean—like crushed mint and cold water.

Velvet dabbed a drop onto her fingertip and traced a simple circle in the air above Haruto’s wrist.

The circle glowed faintly.

Not gold.

Not violet.

Pale, neutral light.

The cuff’s veins reacted, pulsing once like an irritated heartbeat.

Velvet’s expression tightened.

Haruto’s stomach dropped. “Velvet?”

Velvet recorked the vial and set it down with too much care.

Then she looked at him and spoke like someone delivering a diagnosis to a patient who didn’t have the luxury of denial.

“Your interface,” she said softly, “is no longer entirely yours.”

Haruto’s skin went cold. “It never was.”

Velvet’s gaze softened again, pity flickering through her composure. “No. But it used to behave like it was only watching you.”

She glanced toward the door, as if even wood could eavesdrop.

Then back to Haruto.

“Now,” Velvet said, “it behaves like something is watching *through* it.”

Haruto’s throat tightened.

He thought of the Watcher.

Of mirrors.

Of laughter in seams.

Of the codex’s warning: outsiders are temporary; the world is not.

Haruto forced himself to breathe.

“So what do I do?” he asked, voice rough.

Velvet reached out and touched his hand—flat palm, steady pressure, grounding in the simplest way possible.

“You stop treating care like a trap,” Velvet said quietly.

Haruto stared.

She continued before he could argue.

“You have allies,” she said. “That is not a mechanic. That is a fact.”

Haruto’s chest tightened.

Velvet’s voice remained calm, but there was steel under it.

“And I,” Velvet added, “have obligations.”

Haruto blinked. “Obligations?”

Velvet’s gaze shifted briefly toward the rack of sealed envelopes.

Haruto followed it this time.

He saw the different seals.

Sun.

Crescent.

Leaf.

A coin.

Symbols of factions and guilds and whatever lived between them.

Haruto’s stomach turned.

“Those are… favors,” he realized.

Velvet didn’t deny it.

“I heal people,” Velvet said. “That creates debts. Debts create networks.”

Haruto’s mouth went dry. “So you’re—”

“I’m a support role,” Velvet said, and her voice made it sound like a threat.

Haruto swallowed.

Because he finally understood what she meant.

Velvet wasn’t a healer because she was soft.

Velvet was soft because softness was how you touched everything without triggering defenses.

She could reach places swords couldn’t.

She could move people without moving them.

Move.

Haruto felt his wrist warm again at the thought, and he hated that even his insights were triggers.

Velvet noticed the warmth.

Her fingers tightened gently around his hand.

“Boundaries,” she said.

Haruto blinked. “Right.”

Velvet nodded once.

“Here is mine,” she said. “When you feel yourself tipping into Overdrive, you tell me.”

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

Overdrive.

The word still tasted like metal and fear.

“I don’t always know,” he admitted.

Velvet’s gaze sharpened. “You will learn.”

Haruto stared at her.

There was no flirt in her voice.

No teasing.

Just the uncompromising tone of someone who had kept too many people alive to accept ignorance as an excuse.

Haruto swallowed. “Okay.”

Velvet’s expression softened just enough to be kind again.

“And here is yours,” Velvet said. “You may refuse my help. But you must do it honestly.”

Haruto blinked. “What does that mean?”

Velvet’s thumb stroked once across the back of his hand—small, anchoring.

“No performative bravery,” she said. “No ‘I’m fine’ when you’re not. No martyrdom.”

Haruto’s throat tightened.

He looked away, because that hit too close to his coping instincts.

Velvet let him look away.

She waited.

That patience was its own kind of power.

Finally, Haruto whispered, “I’m not fine.”

Velvet nodded as if the world had just stabilized by one degree.

“Good,” she said.

Haruto exhaled shakily.

For a moment, the room was quiet enough that he could hear his own heartbeat.

Then Velvet’s gaze slid to his wrist again, and her calm turned sharp.

“Haruto,” she said softly.

He looked at her.

Velvet’s voice lowered, and this time it sounded like a warning she didn’t want to have to deliver.

“The next time you Overdrive,” Velvet said, “you may not come back the same.”

Haruto’s breath caught.

His cuff warmed as if in agreement.

His HUD chimed once—no tip, no joke, just a line that looked like it had been written by something that did not care about his feelings.

> **NOTICE:** Host stability required.

Haruto stared at the words.

Then he looked at Velvet.

Velvet’s hand stayed on his.

Not a chain.

Not a flag.

A choice.

And Haruto realized, with a quiet, sick fear, that this might be the last kind of choice the world allowed him to have for a while.
