## Chapter 15 — Put the Tutorial in a Box

Haruto had always believed that putting problems in containers did not solve them.

Then Sunny nearly killed him with a quiz about heroism.

So.

His beliefs were evolving.

They stood in the corridor just beyond the “Moral Lecture” trap, the dungeon’s light dimmer now, shadows thicker, and the faint hum underfoot suggesting Sky Dungeon #7 was actively disappointed that they were still alive.

Sunny hovered at head height, chirping like a bird that had learned sarcasm from a customer service hotline.

“Great job surviving!” Sunny sang. “You are all very—”

“Box,” Bone Legion Commander said.

Sunny rotated. “What?”

Mira’s eyes sparkled like she’d been given permission to commit a crime. “We’re putting you in a box.”

Sunny chirped. “You can’t!”

Velvet’s voice was calm. “We can carry you safely so your… encouragement doesn’t trigger traps.”

Sunny chirped, offended. “My encouragement is helpful!”

Seraphina muttered, “Your encouragement is a curse.”

Luminara’s smile turned wicked. “A portable curse.”

Sunny bobbed anxiously, which was the first time Haruto had seen it look anything other than smug. “Wait—what kind of box?”

Haruto glanced at Mira.

Mira grinned and rummaged through her satchel with the confidence of a woman who had been carrying illegal items since Chapter 1.

She pulled out a small wooden crate.

Haruto blinked. “Where did you—”

Mira lifted her chin. “Tavern supplies.”

Seraphina stared. “Why is there a crate in your apron satchel?”

Mira replied cheerfully, “Sometimes customers need to be escorted out.”

Velvet made a soft, approving sound that could have meant anything.

Luminara’s flame hair flickered, amused. “It’s a bouncer’s crate.”

Sunny hovered backward, chirping faster. “No. No. I don’t consent to containment.”

Bone Legion Commander stepped forward with the cold patience of a man who had contained entire armies.

“Containment is not optional,” he said.

Sunny chirped, “You can’t touch me! I’m a tutorial entity!”

Bone Legion Commander’s skull tilted. “Then you will enjoy the box.”

Sunny darted upward—

—and Mira snapped the crate open like she’d practiced this.

Haruto expected chaos.

Instead, Velvet lifted her hand and released a gentle pulse of cool, steady light.

Not an attack.

Not a spell that looked like a spell.

Just… calming.

Sunny’s bobbing slowed.

Its chirping stuttered.

The orb wobbled as if suddenly aware of gravity.

Haruto’s UI chimed, almost confused.

> **STATUS:** Tutorial Sprite “SUNNY”  
> **Debuff:** Quieted (1)  
> **Note:** This is not supposed to be possible.

Sunny’s etched face looked outraged. “Hey!”

Mira scooped it.

Gently.

Like catching a firefly.

Sunny chirped, offended, as Mira dropped it into the crate and snapped the lid shut.

The crate immediately began to vibrate with furious little muffled chirps.

Haruto exhaled.

Silence.

Actual silence.

It felt… holy.

Seraphina’s shoulders visibly loosened. “Thank the Light.”

Bone Legion Commander said, flat, “Finally.”

Luminara sighed contentedly. “I can hear myself think.”

Mira cradled the crate like a pet. “Aww, it’s like a grumpy lantern.”

From inside: muffled chirps of protest.

Haruto’s UI chimed.

> **NEW INVENTORY ITEM (Unofficial):** Boxed Tutorial  
> **Effect:** -50% unwanted tips  
> **Side effect:** tutorial resentment increases over time

Haruto whispered, “We boxed the tutorial and it became an item.”

Velvet’s smile was gentle. “Welcome to Lumora.”

The corridor ahead opened into a larger chamber.

Not an arena.

Not a puzzle room.

A… staging area.

The ceiling was closed here, heavy stone pressing down. The air was colder, damp with mist that smelled faintly metallic—like rainwater that had been stored in a weapon rack.

Along the sides of the chamber stood statues.

Not Radiant knights.

Not Veil commanders.

Something older.

Faceless figures holding mirrors.

Haruto’s skin prickled.

The System chimed—quietly, almost respectfully.

> **NOTICE:** Watcher proximity increasing  
> **Tip:** Don’t look directly at mirrors.

Mira whispered, “That’s always bad.”

Seraphina lifted her sword. “No one looks at anything. We move.”

Bone Legion Commander’s skull angled upward, listening. “We are being evaluated.”

Luminara murmured, “We have been the whole time.”

Velvet’s gaze slid to Haruto. “Stay centered,” she said softly. “Don’t let your thoughts spiral.”

Haruto nodded, trying to keep his face neutral.

He did not want to spawn another emotional hazard.

He also did not want to see what a “Watcher” was.

The mirrors along the walls shimmered.

Not like reflective glass.

Like screens.

Each mirror showed a different image.

One showed Haruto alone in the Grand Radiant Hall, surrounded by arguing people.

One showed him holding the Veil curse-key, stepping into the Moon Plate.

One showed him in the fog, yelling while mimics swarmed.

One showed him in the arena, shouting orders.

One—just for a blink—showed a forest canopy stitched with bronze vines, and a young figure perched on a ruined beam like a sentry.

Pointed ears.

An emerald gaze that didn’t laugh.

Then the mirror corrected itself, swapping leaves for mist and pretending it had never shown him anything outside the dungeon’s script.

It wasn’t prophecy.

It was playback.

The dungeon was replaying his most vulnerable moments like a highlight reel.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

His UI flickered.

> **HIDDEN STAT:** Unity (visible)  
> **Current:** 2  
> **Warning:** Watcher can see this.

Haruto whispered, “It can see my stats.”

From inside the crate, Sunny emitted a muffled, gleeful chirp.

Mira patted the crate. “No.”

The air in the chamber shifted.

A presence stepped forward—not from a corridor, not from the ceiling, not from behind a statue.

From the mirrors themselves.

A figure emerged like a reflection deciding it wanted a body.

It was tall and thin, wrapped in a cloak that looked like liquid shadow with gold edges. Its face was hidden behind a smooth mask split down the center—half radiant porcelain, half dark glass.

Where eyes should be, two narrow slits glowed faintly.

Haruto’s UI did not label it.

It simply flashed one word.

> **WATCHER**

Seraphina’s stance shifted, ready to strike. “Identify yourself.”

Bone Legion Commander’s cloak flared slightly. “State your purpose.”

The Watcher tilted its head as if amused by the idea that it owed them anything.

When it spoke, its voice sounded like two narrators speaking at once—one warm, one cold.

“Unity,” it said.

Haruto’s stomach dropped. “That’s… my stat.”

The Watcher lifted one gloved hand and the mirrors brightened.

In each mirror, Haruto saw the party.

Not as they were.

As they could be.

Seraphina and Bone Legion Commander standing back-to-back, perfectly synchronized.

Luminara and Velvet casting in tandem, heat and tide weaving a shield.

Mira and Haruto laughing, calm, unafraid.

It looked… impossible.

It looked like an ending the gods wouldn’t allow.

The Watcher’s voice layered again.

“Unity breaks balance,” it said.

The System chimed faintly, like a nervous assistant.

> **NOTICE:** Unscheduled boss-like entity  
> **Tip:** Please stop leveling hidden stats.

Haruto’s mouth opened.

No sound came out.

Bone Legion Commander stepped forward half a pace, voice cold. “You are not a boss.”

The Watcher tilted its head. “Not yet.”

Seraphina raised her sword. “Then we destroy you now.”

The Watcher lifted its hand.

The mirrors flashed.

And the floor beneath them lit up—not gold, not violet.

Both.

Together.

Haruto’s UI screamed a warning.

> **TRIAL START:** Unity Check  
> **Condition:** Stay together.  
> **Failure:** separation + punishment

Haruto’s heart hammered.

Velvet’s hand pressed lightly to his back—grounding. “Breathe.”

Luminara’s voice was soft but sharp. “Haruto. Lead.”

Mira hugged the crate tighter. “If we survive this, Sunny is staying in jail forever.”

From inside: muffled chirps of outrage.

Haruto swallowed.

He stared at the Watcher—at the mask split between light and shadow—and realized this dungeon wasn’t just trying to kill him.

It was trying to decide what kind of story he was allowed to have.

And for the first time since arriving in Lumora, Haruto felt something colder than fear.

Determination.
