## Chapter 16 — Unity Trial (Mirror Lies)

The Watcher raised its hand.

The room responded like it had been waiting for permission.

The mirrors along the walls brightened at once, their surfaces no longer reflective but *active*—screens showing angles Haruto knew were impossible. Views from above. Views from behind. Views from inside his own ribcage, it felt like.

The floor tiles lit in patterns that didn’t match any path. Gold. Violet. Gold-violet together in sickening harmony.

Haruto’s UI flashed so hard it made his vision stutter.

> **TRIAL ACTIVE:** Unity Check  
> **Rule:** Stay within 2 tiles of an ally  
> **Rule:** If separated, “punishment” triggers  
> **Tip:** Do not blink.

Haruto blinked anyway.

The world shifted.

For half a second, Haruto saw a different room—brighter, cleaner—Castle Lumora’s Grand Radiant Hall, the choir holding that ridiculous note.

Seraphina stood there, alone, sword planted, looking at him like he’d abandoned her.

Then the image snapped away.

Haruto’s stomach turned.

“Illusion,” Bone Legion Commander said sharply, voice cutting through the haze. “Do not chase it.”

Seraphina snapped back, “I know what an illusion is.”

The floor pulsed.

Haruto’s UI chimed.

> **WARNING:** Argument detected  
> **Rivalry:** attempting to re-enable

Haruto threw his hands out. “NO. Not now. Save it for when we’re not being psychologically murdered by mirrors.”

Velvet’s hand touched Haruto’s shoulder—one steady, grounding press. “Breathe,” she murmured. “Anchor yourself in the present.”

Luminara’s voice was soft, unusually serious. “The Watcher is testing whether your unity is real… or only convenient.”

Mira hugged the crate tighter, muffled chirps rattling inside it like a trapped mosquito with opinions.

“If we die,” Mira whispered, “Sunny is not getting out of jail.”

From inside the crate: *MMPH! MMPH!*

Haruto almost laughed.

He didn’t.

He could feel the dungeon watching his face, waiting for the emotion spike that would turn into damage.

The Watcher stepped forward.

It didn’t walk.

It *glided,* like a reflection moving across glass.

“Unity,” it said again, voice doubled. “Show me.”

The mirrors flashed.

And the room split.

Not with walls this time.

With *distance.*

Haruto’s eyes widened as the floor stretched like rubber.

One moment Seraphina was two steps away.

The next moment she was ten.

Velvet’s fingers slipped from Haruto’s shoulder like the world had pulled them apart.

Mira stumbled, clutching the crate, suddenly across a gap of glowing tiles.

Luminara drifted upward instinctively, but the air itself thickened like syrup.

Bone Legion Commander’s cloak flared, and the violet lines in the floor answered, trying to tether him—

—and yet the room kept pulling.

Haruto’s UI screamed.

> **SEPARATION EVENT:** Triggered  
> **Punishment in:** 3… 2…

Haruto’s heart slammed.

He couldn’t let them separate.

He couldn’t let the dungeon decide.

He did the only thing he could do.

He called them by name.

“Seraphina!” Haruto shouted. “Don’t chase mirrors—chase my voice!”

Seraphina’s head snapped toward him, eyes sharp. “I’m here!”

Her boots dug into the floor, and she lunged—using brute will and muscle to cross tiles that seemed to resist her like deep water.

Haruto’s UI flickered, showing her distance shrinking by one tile.

“Velvet!” Haruto shouted. “Anchor the center!”

Velvet’s calm didn’t crack. She stepped forward, palms lifting, and her aura spilled outward—cool water-light spreading across the tiles like a calm sea.

Where her aura touched, the floor’s stretching slowed, as if stability itself was a counterspell.

“Luminara!” Haruto barked. “Heat the air—make it move again!”

Luminara’s eyes narrowed. “You want wind.”

“I want friction,” Haruto corrected, voice tight. “Anything that breaks the syrup.”

Luminara snapped her fingers and a thin distortion of heat rippled out—just enough to make the thick air shimmer and loosen.

Mira yelled from her gap, “What do I do?!”

Haruto’s brain tried to answer “stop existing,” because that would solve half the dungeon’s social mechanics, but he didn’t say it.

He shouted instead, “Throw the box!”

Mira stared at him. “WHAT?”

Haruto shouted back, “It’s loud and annoying! Use it as a beacon!”

From inside the crate: frantic muffled chirps.

Mira hesitated for half a heartbeat—then, with the confidence of a tavern mistress ejecting a problem, she hurled the crate across the stretching tiles.

The box sailed, spinning.

It landed near Haruto with a *THUMP.*

The muffled chirps exploded into furious vibration.

Haruto’s UI chimed.

> **OBJECT:** Loud Tutorial Detected  
> **Effect:** Dungeon irritation increased  
> **Note:** Irritation can disrupt illusions.

Haruto’s eyes widened.

The floor’s stretching wavered, as if the room itself had momentarily lost focus.

Bone Legion Commander stepped into that wobble like a soldier stepping into a gap in a shield wall.

He lifted one hand and snapped two fingers together.

Violet mist poured out, not as a blanket, but as a set of *lines*—thin, precise threads that shot across the floor and attached to allies’ shadows.

Haruto felt one wrap around his own feet, cold and steady, like an invisible rope.

“Hold,” Bone Legion Commander ordered.

Haruto held.

Seraphina held.

Velvet held.

Luminara held.

Mira held, scrambling toward them while the room’s resistance weakened.

Haruto’s UI flashed again.

> **MECHANIC COUNTERED:** Separation slowed by “Shadow Tether”  
> **Bonus:** Unity check progress +1

The Watcher tilted its head.

For the first time, it seemed… interested.

The mirrors changed.

New images flashed across them—images designed to hurt.

Haruto saw Seraphina standing beside a Radiant hero who wasn’t him, her smile soft and proud.

He saw Luminara leaning close to a different Player, laughing.

He saw Velvet in a quiet chamber, hand on someone else’s chest, whispering devotion.

He saw Mira behind a tavern bar, flirting with a knight who wasn’t flustered—who laughed easily, who didn’t bring monsters when he blushed.

And in each mirror, there was a version of Haruto in the background, small and forgotten.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

The guilt hazard from earlier tried to crawl back into his chest.

His face heated.

The floor pulsed, hungry for that emotion.

Velvet’s voice cut through it—close, firm, grounding.

“Haruto,” she said softly. “Those are not real.”

Haruto swallowed.

“I know,” he whispered.

Velvet’s hand brushed his cuff—brief, practical.

“Then look at what is real,” Velvet murmured.

Haruto forced his gaze away from the mirrors.

He looked at Seraphina, teeth clenched, still moving toward him like a shield that refused to break.

He looked at Luminara, eyes sharp, keeping the air moving.

He looked at Mira, hauling herself forward, face determined despite her usual chaos.

He looked at Bone Legion Commander, shadow threads taut, holding the party together through sheer will.

He looked at Velvet, calm in the center of it all, stabilizing everything without demanding attention.

This—this was real.

And it mattered.

Haruto’s UI chimed softly.

> **HIDDEN STAT:** Unity +1  
> **Current:** 3  
> **Note:** You stopped believing the dungeon’s story.

The Watcher’s voice layered again, colder now.

“Good,” it said. “Now suffer.”

The floor lit up with a new pattern—tiles flashing in alternating gold and violet, like a warning siren.

From the mirrors, enemies emerged.

Not creatures this time.

Not skitters or husks.

Reflections.

Copies shaped like the party members themselves, but wrong—eyes empty, movements too smooth, smiles too sharp.

Haruto’s UI labeled them with brutal simplicity.

> **ENEMIES:** Mirror Seraphina, Mirror Luminara, Mirror Velvet, Mirror Mira, Mirror Commander  
> **Trait:** attacks target “weakest bond”

Mira shrieked, “IT MADE EVIL ME?!”

Seraphina snapped, “Ignore it!”

Mirror Seraphina raised a sword and declared, voice perfect and hollow, “By the Radiant Dominion, I will—”

Haruto shouted, “NO DRAMA! MOVE!”

Seraphina lunged.

Bone Legion Commander’s shadow tethers snapped tighter, pulling the party into a tighter formation.

Velvet’s aura surged, a cool ring stabilizing their “two-tile” rule.

Luminara’s heat-line cracked across Mirror Luminara’s shoulder, disrupting the copy’s perfect posture.

Mira grabbed the boxed Sunny like a weapon and swung it at Mirror Mira.

The crate connected with a loud *THUNK.*

Inside: furious muffled chirps.

Mirror Mira staggered, offended.

Haruto’s UI chimed.

> **EFFECTIVE:** Tutorial resentment damage

Haruto didn’t have time to question that.

The mirrors’ copies attacked in sync, targeting moments where the party’s bonds were weakest.

Mirror Commander aimed for Seraphina’s blind spot.

Mirror Seraphina aimed for the commander’s shadow threads.

Mirror Velvet tried to pull Haruto away with “gentle” hands.

Mirror Luminara tried to distract him with a smile too sharp to be kind.

Haruto’s chest tightened.

The dungeon was trying to separate them again—this time using their own faces.

Haruto clenched his fists.

He felt the cuff on his wrist pulse.

He felt the Split Sigil hum.

And he chose.

“Everyone,” Haruto said, voice low but carrying, “ignore your mirror. Protect the real person next to you.”

Seraphina didn’t hesitate.

She stepped toward Bone Legion Commander—not to compete, not to argue—just to cover him.

Bone Legion Commander’s skull angled toward her for a fraction of a second.

Then he shifted his shadow threads to reinforce her footing.

Velvet’s aura wrapped around both of them, a calm ring in the center.

Luminara’s heat-line snapped across Mirror Seraphina’s blade, cracking it.

Mira bonked Mirror Velvet with the Sunny-box.

Haruto didn’t ask why.

He only knew it worked.

The Mirror copies flickered.

The Watcher’s mask tilted.

And the mirrors—one by one—went dark, as if the room had lost its source.

The copies dissolved into mist, sucked back into the glass like a bad idea being unmade.

Silence slammed down.

Haruto stood panting, chest heaving, the boxed Sunny vibrating like it had opinions about being used as a blunt instrument.

The Watcher lowered its hand.

For a long moment, it said nothing.

Then, slowly, it spoke.

“Unity persists,” it said. “Against design.”

Haruto swallowed, voice rough. “Yeah.”

The Watcher’s voice layered, almost… pleased.

“Good,” it said again. “Now the gods will care.”

The mirrors dimmed.

The floor’s glow faded.

And the Watcher began to sink backward into the glass, as if returning to where it belonged.

Before it vanished completely, it left one final line on Haruto’s HUD—small, cold, and sharp.

> **NOTICE:** Your party has been marked.  
> **NEXT:** Boss route unlocked.

Haruto exhaled shakily.

Mira slumped against the wall, still clutching the crate. “Sunny, if you ever chirp again, I’m throwing you into the nearest void.”

From inside: a muffled, indignant *MMPH!*

Seraphina wiped her blade, breathing hard. “We passed.”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once. “Barely.”

Luminara drifted closer to Haruto, eyes bright. “You did well,” she said softly.

Velvet’s hand touched Haruto’s sleeve—brief, grounding. “You stayed present,” she murmured. “That’s how you win.”

Haruto nodded.

He looked ahead into the corridor the Watcher had revealed—a path darker than any they’d taken so far, where the dungeon’s hum felt like a larger heartbeat waiting.

He swallowed.

“Okay,” Haruto said, voice steady despite everything. “Next part.”

The moment the words left his mouth, his HUD flickered.

Not a warning.
Not a tip.

An unlocked tab—small, cyan, pulsing at the edge of his vision like a heartbeat trying to pretend it wasn’t attached to him.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

He didn’t open it.

He didn’t want to know what the dungeon thought he *needed* to know.

He wanted to keep living in the simple world where problems were: traps, doors, enemies, and whether or not Mira would weaponize cookware.

But the cyan tab pulsed again.

And for the briefest instant, the cuff on his wrist—under his sleeve, under his armor straps, under the whole ridiculous premise of his new life—felt warm.
Like it had noticed him noticing it.

Velvet’s gaze slid to Haruto’s wrist.
Not obvious.
Not dramatic.

But sharp in the way healers got when they saw something internal manifest externally.

“Haruto,” she murmured, too quiet for the dungeon to monetize. “Your… interface changed.”

Haruto swallowed. “Yeah.”

Mira leaned in, still hugging the Sunny-box like a grudge. “Is it a romance flag?”

Seraphina snapped, “This is not the time.”

Luminara’s smile flickered—interested, not teasing. “It’s not a heart. It’s a color.”

Bone Legion Commander’s skull angled slightly. “Cyan is not Radiant.”

“Cyan is not Veil either,” Velvet added, voice calm, which somehow made it worse.

From inside the crate: a smug, muffled chirp. *Mmph!*

Mira squeezed the box. “No.”

Haruto forced himself to look away from the cyan pulse and down the corridor.

The path ahead narrowed, then opened into a sliver of night-sky visible through a broken ceiling.
Moonlight spilled across stone.
Mist rose in slow sheets like breath.

And there—right at the edge of the light—something moved.

Not a spawn.
Not a skitter.
Not a husk.

Haruto’s UI did not label it.

That alone made his stomach drop.

A figure stood on a broken beam above the corridor, balanced like she belonged to height and silence.
A cloak the color of deep forest shadowed her frame.
Long hair—pale in the moonlight—fell in a braid that didn’t move like a costume piece. It moved like it had weight.

Two eyes caught the light.

Emerald.

And in the center of the emerald, just for a flicker, Haruto saw cyan flecks—like tiny chips of the same strange glow now pulsing on his HUD.

Haruto’s breath hitched.

He remembered the sap-scent that didn’t belong.
The leaf-after-rain note in the air.
The mirror blink that had shown pointed ears and a gaze that didn’t laugh.

Seraphina’s sword rose an inch.
Bone Legion Commander’s shadow threads tensed without being told.
Luminara’s heat shifted the air.
Velvet’s aura cooled the floor under Haruto’s feet.
Mira braced the Sunny-box like it was suddenly a mace again.

The figure didn’t attack.

She watched.

Then she spoke—voice low, controlled, and sharp with the kind of discipline that didn’t need a dungeon to enforce it.

“That glow,” she said, eyes never leaving Haruto’s wrist, “is not yours.”

Haruto swallowed. “It sure feels like it is.”

The elf’s gaze narrowed.

Pointed ears became visible as she shifted slightly, letting moonlight outline her profile with infuriating clarity.

“No,” she said, and the word landed like a verdict. “It’s *awake*.”

The dungeon’s hum deepened.

The cyan tab pulsed harder.

And somewhere behind Haruto’s eyes, a presence like a quiet workshop exhaled—as if it had been waiting a long time to be recognized by someone who could name it.

Haruto’s UI chimed once.

Not cheerful.

Not snarky.

Almost… formal.

> **NOTICE:** Resonance detected.  
> **Source:** Unknown (Verdant signature).  
> **Tip:** Please do not trigger a new route while under-leveled.

Luminara let out a single, delighted breath. “Oh.”

Seraphina snapped, “Explain yourself.”

The elf’s gaze flicked to Seraphina, then to Bone Legion Commander—measuring them the way a veteran measured threats and liabilities.

“Later,” she said. “If you live.”

Then she stepped off the beam—

—and instead of falling, she landed light as a leaf on stone.

Close enough now that Haruto could see the faint patterning on her armor: leather and metal worked together, not as fashion, but as something made for moving through ruins without waking them.

She turned back toward the broken ceiling, as if listening to something Haruto couldn’t hear.

And smiled.

Not kindly.

Not cruelly.

Like someone amused by a cosmic joke that hadn’t finished yet.

High above, where moonlight met mist, a seam opened in the air—thin as a blade edge, bright as polished brass.

For a heartbeat, Haruto saw a silhouette in it: a figure split down the middle between gold radiance and violet shadow.

One eye a sun.
One eye a starless night.

Equinox.

The seam closed.

But the laughter lingered—not in the air, not in sound.

In the way Haruto’s UI flickered like a screen being watched from the other side.

The elf’s voice came soft, almost private.

“You’ve been marked,” she said. “By more than your dungeon.”

Haruto stared at her. “Who are you?”

She met his eyes without flinching.

“Elara Sylvandrel,” she said. “And if you keep walking that route—”

Her gaze dropped to his wrist again, emerald reflecting cyan.

“—you’re going to break your world.”
