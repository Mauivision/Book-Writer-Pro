## Chapter 09 — Loot Binds (And So Do People)

Haruto stared at the chest like it might bite him.

It sat in the center of the arena, ornate and smug, shimmering faintly with that “guaranteed reward” glow that had already betrayed him once.

Sunny hovered above it, bobbing so enthusiastically it looked like the dungeon had installed a cheerleader.

“Open it! Open it!” Sunny sang.

Haruto glanced at his wrist.

The Radiant Party Cuff glowed there like a reminder of his first mistake: *picking things up.*

Seraphina stood to his left, sword lowered but posture still tense, as if the mini-boss might respawn out of spite.

Luminara floated to his right, calm again, hair-flame flickering gently. Her smile had returned—dangerously soft.

Velvet watched from behind him, expression serene, hands folded like she was attending a ceremony.

Mira leaned over Haruto’s shoulder, eyes bright, practically vibrating. “If it’s food, it’s mine.”

Haruto swallowed. “If it binds to me again, I’m filing a complaint.”

The System chimed.

> **TIP:** Complaints are recorded.  
> **TIP:** Recorded complaints increase difficulty.

Haruto’s eye twitched. “Why.”

> **TIP:** Because it’s funny.

Haruto crouched and reached for the lid.

He did it slowly, because in games, “slowly” made you feel like you had control even when you absolutely did not.

The chest opened with a *click* that sounded too satisfied.

Light spilled out.

Not warm Radiant light.

Not cold Veil glow.

A weird in-between shimmer—like dawn and midnight arguing in a blender.

Inside, two items rested on velvet cloth:

1) A small charm shaped like a broken coin—half gold, half blackened silver, edges jagged as if snapped from a larger whole.

2) A folded piece of paper with a wax seal stamped into it.

Haruto stared at the paper.

“That looks like a contract,” he whispered.

Velvet’s voice was gentle. “It is.”

Mira’s voice was delighted. “It is.”

Seraphina’s voice was horrified. “It is.”

Luminara’s smile widened. “It is.”

Sunny chimed brightly.

“Congratulations!” it sang. “You have received **Social Loot!**”

Haruto turned his head slowly to glare at the orb.

“Social loot,” Haruto repeated, tone flat with dread.

Sunny bobbed. “Yes! The most dangerous kind!”

The System chimed with unhelpful clarity.

> **LOOT ACQUIRED (PENDING):**  
> **Item A:** Split Sigil (Dual-Faction)  
> **Item B:** Recruitment Contract (Veil)  
> **Warning:** Reading contracts may cause plot.

Haruto whispered, “Everything causes plot.”

> **TIP:** Correct.

Seraphina stepped closer, eyes narrowing at the broken coin charm. “That sigil…”

Luminara’s gaze sharpened. “Dual-Faction crafting,” she murmured. “Old.”

Velvet’s voice stayed calm. “Rare.”

Mira leaned in, whispering loudly, “Sexy.”

Seraphina snapped, “Nothing about coins is—”

Mira giggled. “Not the coin. The tension.”

Seraphina flushed, which was unfair, because she was in full armor and yet still somehow expressive enough to be bullied by a tavern mistress.

Haruto picked up the broken coin charm first—because he’d learned that touching paper in a fantasy world was basically signing your soul away.

The charm was cool against his fingers.

It didn’t snap onto him.

It didn’t bind.

It just… hummed faintly, like it recognized him.

Haruto’s UI updated.

> **ITEM ACQUIRED:** Split Sigil (Dual-Faction)  
> **Effect:** +1 “Co-op Compatibility” (hidden)  
> **Unlock:** enables mixed-faction doors to acknowledge the Player  
> **Note:** The gods will notice this.

Haruto swallowed. “Hidden stats?”

Sunny chirped, delighted. “Yes! The best kind!”

Haruto set the charm carefully into his inventory—wherever that was. It vanished from his hand in a shimmer, which would’ve been cool if it wasn’t happening to him.

Now only the paper remained.

Haruto stared at it like it was a bomb.

The wax seal on the fold was stamped with a skull and a neat little banner beneath it that read:

**VEIL SYNDICATE**

Mira’s voice softened into something almost respectful. “That’s official.”

Seraphina’s grip tightened on her sword. “Do not touch it.”

Luminara’s tone was light. “Touch it.”

Velvet said nothing.

Which, for Velvet, was loud.

Haruto looked at the parchment.

He remembered the message from Bone Legion Commander.

*You owe me.*

He remembered the System recording his promise like a lawyer with a god complex.

*Contract registered.*

Haruto reached out.

The moment his fingers touched the paper, the wax seal warmed.

It didn’t break like normal wax.

It dissolved into mist.

The parchment unfolded by itself, floating up into the air as if the dungeon were presenting it on stage.

Words appeared across it in glowing ink—half in Radiant script, half in Veil runes.

Haruto’s UI chimed, too pleased.

> **CONTRACT EVENT TRIGGERED**

Seraphina took a step forward. “Player—”

The parchment spoke.

Not with a voice.

With text that scrolled like a quest window.

> **VEIL SYNDICATE RECRUITMENT NOTICE**  
> By prior promise and System verification, the Eternal Player shall add one Veil member to the party roster.  
> **Eligible candidates:**  
> - Bone Legion Commander  
> - Chiller Executioner  
> - Ectoplasm Elemental  
> **Deadline:** Before next major gate.  
> **Penalty for failure:** Title: *Oathbreaker (Smelly)*  
> **Note:** Yes, smelly.

Haruto’s face went pale.

“Smelly?” he whispered.

Sunny chirped, thrilled. “Smelly!”

Mira covered her mouth, trying not to laugh. “Oh no. Not smelly.”

Seraphina’s face went stormy. “This is extortion.”

Velvet’s voice was mild. “It is enforcement.”

Luminara leaned in, eyes gleaming. “It is balance.”

Haruto’s brain tried to compute the options.

Bone Legion Commander: tactical, serious, would absolutely treat Haruto like a mission objective.

Chiller Executioner: strong, dangerous, might cut furniture again.

Ectoplasm Elemental: unpredictable, sincere, hugging hazard.

Haruto swallowed.

He had promised the commander already.

But this contract said “eligible candidates,” like it wanted to tempt him into chaos.

The System chimed, as if narrating his indecision.

> **TIP:** Choosing the obvious option is boring.  
> **TIP:** The gods prefer entertainment.

Haruto whispered, “I don’t care what the gods prefer.”

Sunny chirped, “They do!”

Haruto’s cuff warmed.

The Split Sigil in his inventory hummed faintly.

And for a heartbeat, Haruto felt that same internal *click* he’d felt during the fight—authority, not as power, but as permission to decide.

He lifted his chin.

“Okay,” Haruto said, voice steadier than he felt. “We’ll recruit one Veil member.”

Seraphina snapped, “We will not.”

Haruto looked at her. “We will.”

Seraphina’s eyes widened slightly.

She wasn’t used to being contradicted by someone who looked like he’d lose a fight to a stiff breeze.

Haruto continued quickly, before his courage could despawn.

“This dungeon already forced a Veil key. It forced a shadow detour. It’s going to keep doing it. If we refuse, we lose,” Haruto said.

Velvet’s gaze softened with approval.

Luminara’s smile turned pleased. “Good.”

Mira beamed. “He’s growing up.”

Seraphina’s jaw tightened. “He is being manipulated.”

“Both can be true,” Velvet said gently.

Seraphina’s nostrils flared.

Then she took a breath and—painfully—nodded once.

“If this is necessary,” Seraphina said, voice stiff, “then it will be done properly.”

Sunny chimed. “Aww! Character development!”

Haruto glared at the orb. “I’m going to throw you off a sky-island.”

Sunny chirped, delighted. “You can’t!”

Haruto whispered, “Watch me.”

The parchment floated lower, waiting like a menu screen.

The three eligible names hovered in the air like buttons.

Haruto’s eyes flicked toward the deepest option:

**Bone Legion Commander**

The one he’d promised.

The one who was watching.

The one who would absolutely show up the moment Haruto tried to dodge responsibility.

Haruto swallowed.

Then a new message appeared in his HUD, sharp and dark like bone script.

> **MESSAGE RECEIVED (VEIL):**  
> *Choose.*  
> *I’m patient.*  
> *The dungeon is not.*  
> — **Bone Legion Commander**

Haruto closed his eyes for one second.

When he opened them, he knew the next part wasn’t just about combat.

It was about the kind of leader he’d become in this absurd world.

And about the kind of party he was going to build—whether the gods liked it or not.
