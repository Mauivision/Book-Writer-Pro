## Chapter 19 — Radiant Hearing (Anomaly)

Haruto spent the rest of the night at the Golden Tankard pretending his life wasn’t a pair of leashes dressed up as invitations.

It went… poorly.

People tried to congratulate him.
People tried to flirt with him.
People tried to sell him “special recovery drinks” that looked suspiciously like colored water with ambition.

Mira intercepted most of it with the kind of smile that could disarm a knife and then sell the knife back at triple price.

Velvet monitored Haruto’s pulse with her eyes like she could see statistics through skin.

Luminara laughed too loudly at jokes that weren’t funny, as if volume could keep dread out of the room.

Seraphina stared at anyone who looked at Haruto for longer than three seconds like she was collecting names for a holy vendetta.

Bone Legion Commander sat at the edge of the table and watched the crowd like a tactician forced into a social raid.

Chiller Executioner fell asleep standing up at least twice.

Ecto hugged strangers with a cheer that made grown men cry and then immediately apologize for crying.

Elara stayed quiet.
Not distant.
Quiet like a blade she hadn’t decided to draw.

And above it all, the System behaved itself.

No new slime spawns.
No “micro-incidents.”
No cheerful chimes.

Just a subtle, polite pressure at the edge of Haruto’s vision—like a reminder that the world didn’t have to scream to control you.

When Mira finally declared last call, the Radiant courier was still waiting.

The Veil messenger was still waiting too, hood tilted, patient as a trap.

Haruto did not choose.

He did not sign.

He did not smile.

He went to sleep for approximately three hours and woke up with the sensation that his entire existence had been added to someone’s calendar.

His HUD chimed once.

Soft.

Respectful.

Corporate.

> **REMINDER:** Radiant Hearing — Today  
> **Dress code:** presentable  
> **Penalty for failure:** unpleasantness

Haruto stared at the word *unpleasantness* until he hated the person who had written it.

“I miss the slime,” he whispered.

Mira, already awake because tavern owners did not possess the biological function known as “rest,” shoved a plate at him.

“Eat,” she ordered. “You’re about to be judged by people who think sunlight is a personality.”

Seraphina was already in armor.

Of course she was.

She looked like she’d spent the night polishing her moral certainty.

“They cannot judge you unfairly,” she declared, voice tight.

Haruto blinked at her. “That’s… an optimistic sentence.”

Seraphina bristled. “The Radiant Dominion is just.”

Bone Legion Commander clicked his jaw. “The Radiant Dominion is *consistent.*”

Seraphina’s glare snapped to him.

“That is not the compliment you think it is,” Haruto muttered.

Luminara appeared in a shimmer of heat, hair perfect in a way that made Haruto resent physics.

“Try to be charming,” she advised. “It confuses them.”

Velvet adjusted Haruto’s collar with calm hands, not because he needed it, but because she needed something physical to anchor.

“Breathe,” she murmured. “And don’t let them rush you into words.”

Elara watched from the doorway, cloak settled, eyes unreadable.

“They will try to name you,” she said.

Haruto swallowed. “Yeah. That’s what hearings are.”

Elara’s gaze slid to his wrist. “And if they name you incorrectly, the world will treat it as a permission.”

Haruto’s stomach tightened.

He hated how casually she said it.

He hated more that he believed her.

The walk to Castle Lumora was shorter than Haruto wanted and longer than his nerves could afford.

The city was bright today.

Radiant bright.

The kind of bright that didn’t just illuminate—it *insisted.*

Sunlight bounced off white stone until Haruto’s eyes watered. Gold banners hung from balconies like the city was constantly celebrating itself.

People watched them as they passed.

Not hostile.

Not friendly.

Interested.

Haruto felt like a new feature release walking through a user base.

His HUD stayed quiet.

So did Sunny.

The Sunny-box, carried by Mira like a promise of violence, didn’t chirp once.

That silence felt wrong.

Like a tutorial holding its breath.

Castle Lumora’s doors opened before they reached them.

Not because someone was being polite.

Because someone had been waiting.

The tribunal chamber looked like a cathedral that had gone to law school.

White marble.
Gold inlays.
Sun motifs everywhere like a brand that had discovered religion.

The ceiling arched high enough to make Haruto feel small on purpose.

Sound carried strangely. Every footstep echoed like a confession.

At the far end was a semicircle of seats, elevated, filled with figures in radiant robes and armor—councilors, judges, priests, and whatever else you called people who decided what you were allowed to be.

Seraphina straightened like she was meeting her gods.

Luminara smiled like she was meeting her rivals.

Velvet’s expression smoothed into professional calm.

Bone Legion Commander’s shadow tightened under his boots.

Chiller Executioner looked around and immediately hated the lighting.

Ecto waved.

No one waved back.

Elara’s gaze swept the room once, then returned to Haruto’s wrist like it was the only honest thing here.

Haruto stepped forward.

The moment his shoe touched the center of the chamber, the air changed.

Not magically.

Administratively.

His HUD flickered and then settled into a new mode—smaller font, muted colors, as if the System had decided to be polite in court.

> **NOTICE:** Public Proceedings Detected  
> **UI behavior:** restricted  
> **Tip:** Do not embarrass yourself

Haruto exhaled through his nose. “Wow. It can do manners.”

Seraphina hissed, “Haruto.”

Haruto kept walking.

At the center of the chamber, a sun-shaped seal was carved into the floor.

It was beautiful in the way traps were beautiful.

One of the councilors stood.

He was old in the way power made people old: not frail, not tired, just… layered. His robe was white and gold, and the sunlight that poured in through the high windows seemed to bend slightly toward him, like it respected seniority.

“Haruto Takahashi,” he said, voice amplified by architecture and ego.

Haruto flinched again.

His name didn’t belong here.

His name was a relic from a world that didn’t have quest windows.

The councilor spread his arms.

It looked welcoming.

It felt like a net.

“By grace of the Radiant Dominion, you were summoned,” he said.

Haruto’s HUD chimed softly.

> **FACT CHECK:** You were summoned.  
> **Note:** Grace not guaranteed.

Haruto kept his face neutral with the skill of a man who had survived group projects.

The councilor continued.

“You have performed beyond expectation,” he said, and Haruto hated that it sounded like a performance review.

“You have cleared a Sky Dungeon,” the councilor went on, “with… unconventional companions.”

His gaze flicked, briefly, to Bone Legion Commander.

The chamber cooled by half a degree.

Haruto felt Seraphina’s tension spike.

He also felt the city outside holding its breath.

The councilor smiled.

“We are grateful,” he said.

And Haruto, because his life was now a genre, waited for the hidden clause.

It arrived immediately.

“Therefore,” the councilor said, “it is our duty to ensure you are protected.”

Haruto swallowed.

Protected was the polite word for contained.

The councilor’s voice softened.

“You are… an anomaly,” he said, and the chamber made the word sound like a diagnosis.

“Not a condemnation,” he added quickly, like someone reading the fine print out loud. “A classification.”

Haruto’s HUD chimed again.

> **NEW TAG:** Anomaly  
> **Description:** does not fit approved frameworks  
> **Note:** frameworks will be enforced anyway

Haruto’s stomach tightened.

Another councilor spoke—woman this time, younger, sharper, eyes like polished coins.

“An asset,” she corrected smoothly. “One we must not lose.”

Seraphina’s sword hand twitched.

Velvet’s hand moved slightly toward Haruto’s elbow, stopping short.

Mira’s fingers tightened on the Sunny-box handle.

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once, amused in the way only enemies got when your allies said the quiet part out loud.

Luminara’s smile didn’t move.

Elara’s eyes narrowed.

Haruto’s chest went cold.

The first councilor raised a hand. “Words,” he admonished gently. “We are here to honor the Player.”

Honor.

Another polite word for ownership.

He turned his gaze back to Haruto.

“The Radiant Dominion offers you sanctuary,” he said. “Status. Support. A place among us.”

He let it hang there like a gift.

Then he added, softly, “In exchange for clarity.”

There it was.

Clarity was the word you used when you wanted someone to pick a side and then pretend they chose it freely.

Seraphina took a step forward before Haruto could speak.

“He has already proven himself,” she declared. “He does not need to—”

“Seraphina,” the councilor said, voice gentle in the way a knife could be gentle. “You may speak when addressed.”

Seraphina froze.

Her cheeks flushed.

Haruto felt something twist in his chest—not romance, not jealousy, just the ugly awareness of watching someone you cared about be put in their place by the institution they believed in.

He stepped forward.

Not dramatically.

Just enough to put himself between Seraphina and the councilor’s calm cruelty.

“You want me to swear exclusivity,” Haruto said.

The chamber’s silence sharpened.

The councilor’s smile widened.

“We want you safe,” he corrected.

Haruto nodded once. “You want me safe *with you.*”

The second councilor’s eyes glittered. “You are safer in the light.”

Bone Legion Commander clicked his jaw. “He is safer where he is not owned.”

A ripple went through the chamber.

Not because Bone Legion Commander had spoken.

Because a Veil voice had been allowed to.

Haruto’s HUD chimed, polite as a bureaucrat.

> **NOTICE:** Unauthorized speaker detected.  
> **Penalty:** pending

Seraphina whirled. “You have no right—”

Haruto raised a hand.

Seraphina stopped.

Not because she obeyed the council.

Because she had started obeying *him*.

Haruto hated the responsibility that came with that.

He also… needed it.

He looked back at the council.

“I’m not refusing protection,” he said carefully. “I’m refusing ownership.”

The councilor’s expression didn’t change.

But the air did.

It got colder.

“Ownership,” the councilor repeated, as if tasting an insult.

“Allegiance,” Haruto corrected. “Exclusivity. Being… assigned.”

The second councilor leaned forward.

“The Radiant Dominion cannot permit an unbound Player to destabilize our city,” she said smoothly.

Haruto’s stomach dropped. “Destabilize.”

Velvet murmured, barely audible, “Words as weapons.”

Haruto nodded slightly, acknowledging her.

“I haven’t destabilized anything,” he said.

The councilor smiled again.

“Not intentionally,” he agreed. “Which is why you are not accused. You are *managed.*”

Haruto felt heat rise in his face.

Managed.

The word made him want to throw something.

Preferably a chandelier.

Chiller Executioner perked up, as if sensing his calling.

Haruto forced his hands to unclench.

“What do you want,” Haruto said, voice flatter than he felt, “specifically?”

The councilor spread his hands.

“A pledge,” he said. “A vow of cooperation. A public statement that you stand with Radiant.”

Haruto’s HUD chimed.

> **QUEST UPDATE:** Radiant Hearing (Mandatory)  
> **Objective:** declare allegiance (recommended)  
> **Alternative objective:** refuse gracefully

Haruto stared.

Refuse gracefully.

He hated that the System framed his dignity like a difficulty setting.

He took a slow breath.

Velvet’s earlier advice echoed: don’t let them rush you into words.

He spoke anyway.

“I will cooperate,” Haruto said, “with anyone who treats my party as people.”

The councilor’s smile faltered for the first time.

The chamber made a tiny, offended noise—an intake of breath, a shift of fabric, the collective discomfort of bureaucracy being asked to acknowledge humanity.

Haruto continued before they could interrupt.

“I will fight threats to the city,” he said. “I will not swear that the Veil are monsters. I will not swear that you own me because you summoned me.”

The second councilor’s eyes narrowed. “We *saved* you.”

Haruto’s pulse jumped.

Saved.

Another word that came with a bill.

Elara’s voice cut in, quiet, but sharp enough to slice marble.

“Summoning is not saving,” she said.

The chamber snapped toward her like a single organism discovering an infection.

The first councilor’s voice hardened, just a fraction.

“You are not recognized,” he said.

Elara met his gaze without blinking. “Then your recognition is not required.”

Seraphina made a shocked sound.

Luminara smiled like she was watching a good play.

Velvet exhaled slowly, as if bracing.

Haruto felt his cuff warm.

Not cyan.

Not gold.

Warm like a warning.

The councilor raised his hand.

“Enough,” he said softly.

Haruto’s HUD flickered.

> **NOTICE:** Containment language detected.  
> **Tip:** Be careful.

The councilor turned back to Haruto.

“If you will not pledge exclusivity,” he said, “then we must restrict your movement.”

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

“You can’t—”

The councilor’s smile returned, patient and terrible.

“We can,” he said. “We *will*, if you force us.”

Seraphina stepped forward again, voice shaking with fury. “You would cage him?”

The councilor’s gaze softened toward her, and Haruto hated how paternal it was.

“Seraphina,” he said gently. “You of all people should understand duty.”

Seraphina’s breath hitched.

Haruto saw it then—the hook behind her loyalty.

Duty was the chain she called holy.

Haruto stepped forward once more.

He didn’t raise his voice.

He didn’t insult their faith.

He just refused the frame.

“If you cage me,” Haruto said, “you’ll prove the Veil right. You’ll prove that Radiant ‘protection’ is ownership.”

Silence.

The council chamber did not like dilemmas that couldn’t be solved by declaring something evil.

Haruto felt the city outside listening.

He felt the weight of public perception like a hand on his throat.

Mira’s earlier whisper returned: we control the story, or the story controls us.

Haruto looked at the councilor.

“I won’t be your weapon,” he said. “But I will be your ally.”

The second councilor scoffed softly. “Ally is a word without leverage.”

Bone Legion Commander clicked his jaw. “That is the point.”

The councilor’s eyes flashed.

Then, like a person choosing the least damaging option, he exhaled.

“Very well,” he said.

Haruto’s stomach tightened. “Very well?”

“A temporary compromise,” the councilor said, and Haruto hated those two words more than “managed.”

He lifted his hand.

A golden seal formed in the air like a UI window in physical space—beautiful, glowing, suffocating.

“You will remain in the city,” the councilor said.

Haruto’s jaw clenched.

“You will accept Radiant escort when requested,” the councilor continued.

Seraphina’s eyes widened—half pride, half horror.

“And,” the councilor finished, “you will refrain from recruiting additional Veil personnel without council approval.”

Bone Legion Commander’s shadows tightened.

Luminara’s flames flared a fraction.

Velvet went still, calm turning sharp.

Elara’s gaze cooled.

Haruto felt his cuff warm again, like the world was taking notes.

His HUD chimed, polite as poison.

> **SYSTEM UPDATE:** Reputation systems enabled in town.  
> **Note:** Your choices now have witnesses.

Haruto swallowed.

He looked up at the councilor.

“And if I refuse that too?” he asked.

The councilor’s smile was almost kind.

Almost.

“Then we will decide you are a threat,” he said.

Haruto exhaled slowly.

So this was the compromise.

Not freedom.

Not prison.

A narrowing corridor with bright walls.

He glanced at his party.

Seraphina looked ready to die for him in a courtroom.

Luminara looked ready to burn the courtroom.

Velvet looked ready to hold Haruto together while it happened.

Mira looked ready to turn the courtroom into a rumor.

Bone Legion Commander looked ready to file a grievance with violence.

Chiller looked ready to hit something for the emotional catharsis.

Ecto looked ready to hug the council into compliance.

Elara looked like she’d already seen how this ended in other worlds.

Haruto swallowed.

“Fine,” he said, and the word tasted like swallowing a hook.

The councilor nodded, satisfied.

His voice softened again, honey over iron.

“This is for your protection,” he said.

Haruto smiled without humor.

“Yeah,” he said. “I’m sure it is.”

As they turned to leave, Haruto felt eyes on his back.

Not the council’s.

Different.

From the side gallery.

He glanced up and caught the edge of a hood in shadow.

Veil.

Watching.

Waiting.

And as if to make sure Haruto didn’t forget he was still in a game, his HUD chimed one more time—quietly, politely, and with the vicious glee of a system that had just unlocked a new menu.
