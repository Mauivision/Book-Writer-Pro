## Chapter 20 — Veil Offer (Real Contract)

If Radiant tried to own you with light, Veil tried to own you with shadows.

Haruto learned that on the walk back from the hearing, when the city’s sunshine felt less like warmth and more like surveillance.

The Radiant escort was polite.

That was the worst part.

Two knights flanked him at a respectful distance as if guarding him was an honor and not a restriction. They didn’t grab him. They didn’t threaten him. They didn’t even look at him directly for too long, as if eye contact might count as harassment and generate paperwork.

Seraphina looked like she wanted to bite through her own helmet straps.

Luminara drifted close enough to make the air shimmer around the escorts’ shoulders—friendly heat, the kind that reminded people she could stop being friendly at any moment.

Velvet stayed at Haruto’s side, calm as a sedative.

Mira walked ahead, smiling at anyone who stared, collecting information like coins.

Chiller Executioner… technically complied by walking in the same direction.

Ecto waved at the escort twice and got ignored both times, which offended it so deeply that it began hugging lampposts out of spite.

Elara kept to the edge again, eyes scanning rooftops and corners the way you scanned a dungeon hallway after learning traps could spawn in taverns.

Bone Legion Commander didn’t seem bothered by the escort.

That also felt like a threat.

Haruto’s HUD stayed in its “public mode.”

Quiet.

Muted.

Polite.

It was like the System had decided court was over, but witnesses remained.

> **NOTICE:** Reputation systems active.  
> **Tip:** Smile (optional)  
> **Tip:** Choices are permanent (mandatory)

Haruto wanted to ask what “permanent” meant in a world where chandeliers respawned.

He didn’t.

He was learning.

When the escorts finally peeled away near the Golden Tankard—because even holy bureaucracy needed plausible deniability in a tavern district—Haruto exhaled slowly.

Seraphina exhaled like she’d been holding in a scream.

Mira flicked her eyes toward the windows of nearby buildings and smiled brighter.

Velvet touched Haruto’s sleeve, a brief, grounding press.

“You did well,” she murmured.

Haruto let out a humorless laugh. “They put me on a leash and called it a compromise.”

“Yes,” Velvet said, like agreeing with him would keep him sane. “But they didn’t close the collar.”

Haruto’s stomach tightened.

He could still hear the councilor’s voice: *temporary compromise.*

Temporary was how people described cages they planned to lock later.

The Golden Tankard’s door was half open, as if Mira’s building didn’t understand how to fully close against the world’s nonsense.

Mira paused in the doorway, hand on the frame, and glanced back at Haruto.

“Before we go in,” she said brightly, “we’re going to do a quick vibe check.”

Haruto blinked. “A what?”

Mira pointed at his face. “Your face right now is the face of a man who is about to start a political war because he’s mad.”

Haruto opened his mouth.

Mira kept going.

“Which is fair,” she added, “but we do not start political wars *without snacks*.”

Seraphina bristled. “This is not a joke.”

Mira smiled at her. “Sweetie, everything is a joke. The only question is whether we laugh first or the world laughs at us.”

Elara’s gaze slid toward a side street.

Haruto followed it.

A hooded figure stood in shadow, motionless, like a quest marker that had decided to cosplay as a person.

It wasn’t Radiant.

The light didn’t touch it right.

Haruto’s cuff warmed.

Not cyan.

Not gold.

Warm like recognition.

The hooded figure lifted two fingers in a small, precise gesture.

Bone Legion Commander clicked his jaw once.

“There,” he said.

Seraphina’s hand moved toward her sword.

“No,” Haruto said immediately, because his life had become a constant emergency veto.

Seraphina froze, glaring.

Haruto didn’t look away from the hooded figure.

The hooded figure turned and walked into the side street without urgency.

Like it knew Haruto would follow.

Haruto’s HUD chimed—different from public mode. Sharper. Less polite.

> **OPTIONAL QUEST:** Veil Offer — Active  
> **Difficulty:** Social  
> **Warning:** Optional quests are never optional.

Haruto exhaled. “Yeah. I know.”

Mira leaned close, voice low. “You want me to come?”

Haruto hesitated.

This was a negotiation.

Mira’s presence would shift it.

So would Velvet’s.

So would Luminara’s.

Seraphina’s would turn it into a holy incident.

Elara’s would turn it into something… older.

Haruto looked at Bone Legion Commander.

“You?” Haruto asked.

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once. “It’s my offer.”

Haruto nodded. “Then you and me.”

Seraphina’s eyes widened. “Absolutely not.”

Haruto held up a hand. “Seraphina—”

“They will trap you,” she snapped. “They will—”

“They already did,” Haruto said, quietly.

Seraphina’s mouth closed.

Velvet’s gaze softened, but it didn’t relax.

Luminara’s smile thinned into something dangerous. “If he doesn’t come back,” she said lightly, “I’ll set their negotiation space on fire.”

Bone Legion Commander looked at her. “Understood.”

Chiller Executioner yawned. “That’s fair.”

Ecto raised both hands. “Group hug if he gets kidnapped!”

Mira whispered, “Please don’t say kidnapped out loud. It makes it more likely.”

Haruto didn’t know if she was joking.

His HUD didn’t clarify.

It just chimed again, impatient.

> **TIP:** Proceed.

Haruto followed the hooded figure into the side street.

The city changed in three steps.

The bright stone and gold banners faded behind him. The air got cooler. The noise softened. Shadows thickened, not ominously, but practically—like this part of the city had learned not to waste light where it didn’t help.

There was no dramatic sewer entrance.

No secret door that slid open with a cinematic click.

Just a plain building with a plain door and a plain guard whose eyes missed nothing.

The hooded messenger tapped twice.

The door opened.

Haruto stepped inside.

The light dropped.

Not into darkness.

Into controlled visibility.

Lanterns burned low. Walls were clean and undecorated. The space was functional the way Radiant was performative.

Haruto’s HUD flickered.

The font changed.

The colors deepened.

The System stopped pretending it was polite.

> **NOTICE:** Veil jurisdiction detected.  
> **UI behavior:** candid  
> **Tip:** Numbers don’t lie.

Haruto’s jaw tightened. “I hate this place already.”

Bone Legion Commander walked ahead of him like he belonged here.

Which, unfortunately, he did.

They entered a room with a single table.

Not a grand tribunal.

Not a stage.

A table.

Two chairs.

On the table lay a sheet of dark paper, perfectly flat, with violet ink that looked like it had been coaxed out of shadow itself.

Bone Legion Commander sat.

Haruto sat across from him.

The chair was uncomfortable in a way that felt intentional.

“You came,” Bone Legion Commander said.

Haruto stared at the paper. “You made it a quest.”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked. “The System made it a quest.”

Haruto looked up. “Did you ask it to?”

Bone Legion Commander’s silence was answer enough.

He slid the paper forward.

“Read,” he said.

Haruto didn’t touch it immediately.

He’d learned what “touch” meant here.

“Tell me the lie first,” Haruto said.

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked again—once, crisp.

“It’s not a lie,” he said. “It’s leverage.”

Haruto exhaled slowly.

“Okay,” Haruto said. “Let’s play.”

He picked up the contract.

It was heavier than paper should be, as if the words had weight.

The ink was neat. The terms were clean. The language was reasonable enough to be dangerous.

Protection.

Mutual defense.

Access to Veil resources.

Non-interference clauses that sounded fair until Haruto realized they were fences.

And then the quiet sentence near the middle that made his stomach drop:

**Exclusive operational priority.**

Haruto looked up, voice flat. “There it is.”

Bone Legion Commander didn’t pretend. “There it is.”

Haruto tapped the line. “You want exclusivity.”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked. “We want predictability.”

Haruto snorted once. “Same word, different marketing.”

Bone Legion Commander leaned forward slightly.

In this light, with no theatrical cloak flare, he looked less like a warlord and more like a commander who had been forced to turn people into resources because resources were how you kept them alive.

“Radiant wants you as a symbol,” he said. “Veil wants you as a tool.”

Haruto’s chest tightened. “And what do you think I am?”

Bone Legion Commander’s empty eye sockets fixed on Haruto in a way that made Haruto feel seen despite the lack of eyes.

“A hazard,” he said simply.

Haruto laughed, sharp. “Okay. At least you’re honest.”

“Honesty is efficient,” Bone Legion Commander replied.

Haruto’s fingers tightened on the contract.

“Why,” Haruto asked, voice tight, “can’t you let me be unclaimed?”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once. Slower.

“Because you make other people unclaimed,” he said.

Haruto froze.

The sentence landed differently than “asset.”

It wasn’t flattery.

It was fear dressed as logic.

Bone Legion Commander continued, voice calm.

“Your party is mixed,” he said. “That alone destabilizes established control. Every time you refuse ownership, you teach others it’s possible.”

Haruto swallowed.

He thought of Ecto hugging strangers.

He thought of Seraphina stepping in front of him in a tribunal.

He thought of Mira turning rumors into shields.

He thought of Velvet anchoring him with quiet hands.

He thought of Luminara laughing too loud because she could smell gods.

He thought of Elara’s calm warning: if they name you incorrectly, the world will treat it as permission.

Haruto’s HUD chimed, candid and cruel.

> **INSIGHT:** You are contagious.

Haruto’s mouth went dry. “Contagious.”

> **TIP:** Social contagion is difficult to contain.

Haruto stared at the words until he wanted to tear his own HUD out of the air.

Bone Legion Commander’s voice didn’t change.

“Factions cannot afford you unclaimed,” he said. “Not because you are special. Because you are *unsettling.*”

Haruto’s wrist warmed at the word.

Unsettling.

Settling.

The gauntlet was settling.

And Haruto was… not.

Haruto set the contract down carefully.

“No,” he said.

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once. Not surprised.

“Expected,” he said.

Haruto’s chest tightened. “Then why offer it?”

Bone Legion Commander leaned back slightly.

“Because now the Veil can say we tried,” he said.

Haruto’s skin prickled. “And because—”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked.

“Because if you refuse the clean contract,” he said, “you will be offered a dirty one.”

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

Hardliners.

The Veil had them too.

“Is that a threat?” Haruto asked.

Bone Legion Commander’s voice stayed steady. “It’s a forecast.”

Haruto swallowed.

Then he did the only thing that made sense in a world where everything was a negotiation disguised as fate.

He offered his own terms.

Haruto leaned forward and slid the contract back across the table.

“Here’s what I can give you,” Haruto said.

Bone Legion Commander waited.

Haruto spoke slowly, choosing words like tools.

“Transparency,” Haruto said. “No secret clauses. No exclusivity. No ‘priority.’”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once, disapproving.

Haruto continued anyway.

“Cooperation,” he said. “I’ll take missions that protect people—Radiant or Veil—if the mission doesn’t require me to call the other side monsters.”

Bone Legion Commander’s shadows twitched under the table.

Haruto pressed on.

“And boundaries,” Haruto said. “You don’t get to tell me who I can recruit. You don’t get to control my party. You don’t get to own my wrist.”

The cuff warmed.

Not cyan.

Just warm.

Like a device appreciating the attempt.

Bone Legion Commander was silent for a long moment.

Then his jaw clicked.

Once.

Twice.

Not anger.

Calculation.

“You’re proposing a third faction behavior,” he said.

Haruto’s mouth tightened. “I’m proposing basic human dignity.”

Bone Legion Commander tilted his skull slightly.

“Those are synonyms in your world,” he said. “Not in this one.”

Haruto felt his stomach twist.

He looked down at the contract again.

The words were still neat.

The cage was still clean.

He pushed it away.

“Then learn,” Haruto said.

The room went quiet.

Even the lanterns seemed to hold their light.

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once.

Then he did something Haruto didn’t expect.

He laughed.

It wasn’t warm.

It wasn’t cruel.

It was the brief, startled laugh of someone hearing a sound that didn’t belong in their system.

“You are unreasonable,” he said.

Haruto met his gaze. “Thank you.”

Bone Legion Commander’s voice went flat again.

“I can’t accept your terms,” he said.

Haruto’s chest tightened. “Then we’re done.”

“We are not done,” Bone Legion Commander replied.

Haruto frowned. “Why not?”

Bone Legion Commander tapped the contract once with one bony finger.

“Because the offer is not the point,” he said. “The point is that you now understand the shape of the cage.”

Haruto’s pulse kicked.

He hated that the commander was right.

Haruto’s HUD chimed again, candid as a knife.

> **QUEST UPDATE:** Veil Offer  
> **Result:** Refused  
> **Reward:** Knowledge (uncomfortable)

Haruto exhaled slowly. “Great. My favorite kind of reward.”

Bone Legion Commander slid something else across the table.

Not the contract.

A smaller slip of paper.

No ink.

Just a pressed violet thread tied in a loop.

“What is that?” Haruto asked.

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once. “A contact.”

Haruto stared. “A person?”

“A route,” Bone Legion Commander corrected.

Haruto’s skin prickled.

Routes.

Verdant route.

Cyan route.

Now Veil route.

His HUD chimed—quietly delighted at his misery.

> **NOTICE:** New connection unlocked.  
> **Tip:** Don’t tell Radiant.

Haruto swallowed.

He took the slip of paper, because refusing knowledge in this world was how you died.

Bone Legion Commander stood.

Haruto stood too.

At the door, the commander paused.

He didn’t look back dramatically.

He just said, quietly, “The Radiant hearing gave you a leash.”

Haruto’s jaw tightened. “Yeah.”

Bone Legion Commander’s jaw clicked once.

“The Veil offer gives you a map,” he said. “Use it.”

Haruto swallowed.

“And if I don’t?” Haruto asked.

Bone Legion Commander’s voice stayed calm.

“Then the hardliners will,” he said.

The door opened.

Haruto stepped out into the alley where the city’s light didn’t reach.

He could breathe again.

Barely.

When he returned to the Golden Tankard, Mira was waiting in the doorway like a bouncer guarding his fate.

She took one look at his face and clicked her tongue.

“You didn’t sign,” she said.

Haruto shook his head.

Mira’s smile flashed. “Good. I hate paperwork.”

Then her eyes narrowed, sharp as knives.

“But,” she added softly, “now everyone’s going to hear about it.”

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

He looked down at his HUD.

The System had pinned something to the corner of his vision without asking.

A new icon.

A folded-paper symbol.

Violet thread.

And a label in crisp text.

> **PINNED:** “Veil Contract (Refused)”  
> **Note:** Rumors require no signature.

Haruto stared.

“Oh,” he whispered.

Mira patted his cheek gently, like he was a child who had just discovered how stories spread.

“Welcome to politics,” she said.
