## Codex 01 — The First Equinox

The System did not *announce* the unlock.

It did not chime brightly.
It did not throw confetti.
It did not let Sunny narrate it.

Haruto noticed it the way you notice a new icon on your desktop after a crash—quietly, suspiciously, and with the certainty that whatever it was, it wasn’t there for his comfort.

A small tab had appeared at the edge of his vision.

Not gold.
Not violet.

**Cyan.**

It pulsed once, like something breathing through glass.

> **OPTIONAL CONTENT UNLOCKED:** Architect Codex Fragment (1)  
> **WARNING:** This information may alter your interpretation of events.  
> **Tip:** You asked to know how the world works.

Haruto did not remember asking.

But he remembered the Watcher.
He remembered the mirrors replaying him like a highlight reel.
He remembered the way the dungeon had reacted—less like an obstacle, more like a mind with rules.

So he swallowed and, with the same cautious resignation he used when clicking “I Agree” on a terms-of-service he couldn’t possibly read, he opened the codex.

The world blinked.

The stone ceiling above him was gone.
The party around him was gone.
Even the dungeon’s hum was gone.

He stood in a place that felt older than the word *old*.

There was no sky.
There was only a vast, curved vault of darkness filled with moving lights—twin suns dragging their brilliance across an ocean of black, and moons that did not reflect but *absorbed*, swallowing edges until even light seemed tired.

Somewhere far below, a planet turned.

Lumora.

But not the Lumora he’d met.

This Lumora had no marble castles.
No neon crypt arcades.
No elegant faction branding.

It was raw.

Continents glowed faintly like heated metal.
Rivers ran not with water but with pale aether-luminescence, weaving through valleys like veins.
Mountains rose in shapes too clean to be natural—angles that implied intent, not erosion.

Haruto’s stomach lurched with the sudden, impossible certainty that he was not watching a story.

He was watching an *instructional replay*.

And something—someone—was watching it with him.

Not Sunny.

Not the gods with their patch notes and laughter.

A presence like a workshop: quiet, vast, patient.

The Codex spoke without a voice.
Words formed in his mind like blueprints.

> **THE ARCHITECTS**  
> The first builders did not worship Light or Shadow.  
> They did not treat reality as sacred.  
> They treated it as material.

Haruto’s breath caught.

Figures moved across the planet’s surface—too small at first to see clearly, then suddenly close, as if the replay decided distance was optional.

They were not human.

They were tall silhouettes made of faintly glowing lines, as if someone had drawn living bodies using the concept of geometry.
Their hands were wrong—too many joints, too many angles—built for precision rather than comfort.

Where they walked, the land changed.

Not violently.
Not like an earthquake.

Like a designer adjusting a map.

Ridges smoothed into roads.
Canyons bridged themselves.
Walls rose in elegant curves of brass-colored stone threaded with cyan light.

Haruto’s gamer brain translated what his eyes couldn’t.

*They are editing the world.*

The Codex continued.

> They built balance as a mechanism.  
> They called creation **Build**.  
> They called renewal **Destroy**.  
> They called mercy **Move**.

Haruto’s throat tightened at the last one.

Mercy.

Not Heal.
Not Bless.
Not “support class.”

*Move.*

Relocation.
Reposition.
Take the thing that would break and put it somewhere it could survive.

He felt the cuff on his wrist in the real world—felt it even here, in the replay.
A phantom pressure, like the memory of a hand.

The scene widened again.

Above the planet, the twin suns aligned on opposite sides like eyes.
Between them, the moons slid into place like shutters.

Light and shadow overlapped in a way that made Haruto’s teeth ache.

The planet shivered.

The Codex put a name to it.

> **THE FIRST EQUINOX**  
> A rare alignment.  
> A stress test.  
> A question asked of the World Core:  
> **Can you hold both?**

Haruto watched the surface of Lumora crack—not with craters, but with thin luminous seams.
Cyan lines spread under the continents like fractures in tempered glass.

And then he saw it.

Deep beneath everything, a colossal structure pulsing slowly.

Not a dungeon.
Not a castle.
Not a god.

A heart.

The World Core.

It beat once.

The beat was too strong.

The cyan seams flared—

—and the World Core fractured.

Haruto flinched as if the crack happened inside his own chest.

From the heart’s break, something leapt free.

A shard.

Pure cyan aetherite, bright enough to hurt, spinning through void like a comet made of intention.

The Architects moved, all at once, hands rising as if catching a falling tool.

They didn’t panic.

They didn’t pray.

They did what builders did:

They *made* a solution.

The shard slowed.
Not because space resisted it—
but because the Architects said it should.

Threads of light wrapped around it.
Brass filigree formed in the air like a cage being grown rather than forged.

And the shard became a shape that was unmistakably, achingly familiar.

A cuff.

A single gauntlet.

Not metal.
Not leather.

Crystal, held in place by geometry and will.

Haruto felt his wrist sting in sympathy.

The Codex set the purpose like a stamp pressed into wax.

> **THE CRYSTAL GAUNTLET**  
> Emergency balance mechanism.  
> Designed to bind to **one** wearer.  
> Designed to select an **unbiased** host.  
> Designed to awaken only when the world tilts too far.

The replay shifted again, showing the Architects placing the finished cuff against a pillar of light that descended into the planet—feeding it into the World Core’s lattice like returning a tool to its rack.

As the gauntlet sank into the cyan glow, Haruto felt a sudden, chilling understanding.

This wasn’t a “legendary item drop.”

This was a circuit breaker.

And he—

He was the hand it had chosen when the breaker flipped.

The final line of the Codex appeared, small and merciless.

> **Protocol:** If balance fails again, the Gauntlet will select an outsider.  
> **Cost:** Outsiders are temporary.  
> The world is not.

The vision snapped shut.

Stone ceiling returned.
Dungeon hum returned.
The weight of his body returned.

Haruto’s party was still there somewhere, still fighting their way through a story the world insisted on enforcing.

But Haruto stood very still, staring at nothing, because his wrist felt too warm.

And the cyan tab at the edge of his vision pulsed once—

—like a heartbeat that had finally remembered it had hands.
