## Codex 09 — Two Hands (Margin Note)

Haruto didn’t go looking for the margin note.

He went looking for proof it wasn’t real.

Because the idea of a loophole was hope, and hope—Haruto had learned—was the fastest way to make the world demand payment.

So he tried to treat it like a bug report.

Like a weird line of text that slipped into the system log by accident.

Like something you could ignore until a later patch.

The cyan tab pulsed.

Once.

Then it stayed still.

Not urging.

Waiting.

Haruto swallowed and opened it, not with curiosity this time, but with grim, careful intent.

The world blinked.

He wasn’t in the void.

He wasn’t in the workshop.

He wasn’t in the World Core chamber.

He was… in a page.

Not metaphorically.

Literally.

He stood on a surface of text—cyan letters the size of floor tiles, lines running under his feet, paragraphs stretching into darkness like roads.

Above him, in huge symbols, was the header:

> **SECOND EQUINOX PROTOCOL**

Haruto’s skin prickled.

He recognized this.

It was Codex 06’s ending—pressure escalation, replacement, the world not obligated to keep him.

The text under his boots glowed faintly as he stepped, as if the page responded to presence.

Haruto moved carefully, scanning the lines for the faint Verdant annotation he’d seen last time.

He found it where he expected.

Tucked beneath the protocol like a note someone had tried to hide in the gutters.

Not cyan.

Faint green.

Verdant.

> **UNOFFICIAL NOTE (trace source unknown):**  
> Balance is not peace.  
> Balance is motion.  
> Motion requires two hands.

Haruto stared at the words.

He felt the stupid, dangerous impulse to believe them.

Then the codex did something new.

The Verdant text didn’t just sit there.

It pulsed.

Once.

And the pulse spread outward like ink in water, touching cyan letters without changing them, *tagging* them.

A second layer appeared—thin, almost invisible.

Like a watermark.

Like hidden markup.

Haruto’s HUD flickered as if trying to interpret it.

This time, it failed.

The codex did it for him.

> **TRACE ACCESS GRANTED:** Margin layer  
> Source: concealed stabilization policy  
> Warning: access will be audited

Haruto swallowed. “Audited by who?”

The page answered without a voice.

The cyan letters above him shifted slightly, and for a fraction of a second, Haruto saw the outline of the audit halo—the same gold-violet scanner ring from Codex 07, hovering somewhere beyond the page like a camera lens searching for focus.

Haruto’s heart pounded.

He was reading a loophole inside a system that hated loopholes.

He forced himself to move anyway.

The margin layer highlighted a line Haruto hadn’t noticed before because it had been written to be unnoticeable.

It wasn’t a poem.

It wasn’t a prophecy.

It was a design note.

> **If ignition is required, ignition may be shared.**  
> **If continuity is required, continuity may be anchored in two.**

Haruto’s throat tightened.

Shared.

Two.

His mind flashed to Elara’s hand on his wrist—how the pain had eased when she was near.

Organic continuity link.

Two hands.

The codex highlighted another phrase, lower on the page.

It was small.

Like someone had written it fast, hoping no one would see.

> **Host is temporary.**  
> **But hands are not always hosts.**

Haruto stared.

Hands.

Not hosts.

So the loophole wasn’t “the host survives.”

It was… the role changes.

The function changes.

He felt cold spread through his chest as understanding took shape.

The world needed an outsider to do the bridging because outsiders were unclaimed.

But if the outsider could become a *hand* rather than a host—

If the outsider could share continuity with someone the world already recognized as infrastructure—

Then maybe the system didn’t have to burn him as fuel.

Haruto swallowed hard.

And then the page *shifted*.

Not the text.

The space around it.

A shadow fell over the cyan letters like a cloud passing over sunlight.

The codex froze.

Everything went still.

Not quiet.

Still.

Haruto’s teeth ached with recognition.

Permission being revoked.

The Avatar’s voice slid into his mind like a knife sliding into a sheath.

“You’re reading the wrong part.”

Haruto’s pulse spiked.

He looked up.

He couldn’t see the Avatar.

But he could feel it hovering beyond the page, as if the protocol itself had an observer.

The Verdant margin layer flickered.

Haruto felt panic claw at his ribs.

If the audit halo saw this—

If the Correction Engine flagged it—

If the world defended itself against the loophole—

Then he’d lose it.

He’d lose the only line that sounded like hope.

Haruto did the only thing he could.

He copied it.

Not with hands.

With memory.

He repeated the margin note in his head like a mantra, carving it into his own mind where the world couldn’t delete it without deleting him.

*Motion requires two hands.*

The Avatar’s voice pressed harder.

“Loopholes are not mercy,” it said softly. “They are debt.”

Haruto’s throat tightened.

“Everything is debt,” he whispered back.

The page trembled.

The Verdant layer flared once—bright, defiant—and then collapsed inward like a candle flame pinched between fingers.

The codex text returned to normal cyan.

The margin was gone.

The hidden layer vanished so completely Haruto couldn’t even tell where it had been.

But the words remained in his head.

And before the vision snapped shut, the codex left him one final line—small, almost apologetic.

> **Trace note:**  
> The world cannot audit what it refuses to acknowledge.  
> Keep it quiet.  
> Keep it human.

Stone returned.

Breath returned.

Noise returned.

Haruto stood very still, staring at nothing, because he could still feel the pressure of the audit halo somewhere above his life like a sun that watched instead of warmed.

The cyan tab sat at the edge of his vision, quiet now.

Not pulsing.

Not urging.

As if the system had decided it couldn’t erase what had already been read.

Haruto swallowed.

He didn’t know how to tell Elara.

He didn’t know if telling her would make it real enough for the world to punish.

But he knew one thing with the clarity of a man holding a secret that could save him and doom him at the same time:

The Avatar wanted him to be a host.

The margin note wanted him to be a hand.

And if there was any chance of surviving Lumora without becoming ignition—

It would require two hands.
