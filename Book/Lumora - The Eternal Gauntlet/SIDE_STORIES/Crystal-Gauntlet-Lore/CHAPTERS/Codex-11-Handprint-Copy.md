## Codex 11 — Handprint Copy

The first time Haruto saw someone try to copy a miracle, it happened in a back room that smelled like ink and desperation.

The second time, it happened in the codex.

The cyan tab pulsed.

Haruto didn’t want to open it.

He did anyway, because the System had trained him into compliance with curiosity.

The world blinked.

He stood in a workshop again.

Not the Architect workshop.

Something newer.

Something that had learned to imitate ancient precision without understanding the cost.

The room was made of stone and brass scraps, stitched together like a shrine built by thieves. Lanterns burned low. Shadows gathered in corners where people stood very still.

Humans.

Elves.

Someone with a Radiant sigil hidden under their cloak like guilt.

Someone with Veil ink on their hands like pride.

And on the table between them lay a sheet of thin metal etched with lines that hurt Haruto’s eyes to look at too long.

A handprint pattern.

Not a human handprint.

The Architect overlay—those branching lines, the lattice palm, the split geometry of fingers.

Codex 02’s key.

Haruto’s wrist warmed like it recognized the shape and hated it.

The codex text appeared in cyan.

> **UNAUTHORIZED REPLICATION ATTEMPT**  
> Target: handprint key  
> Goal: access Gauntlet operations without host

Haruto swallowed hard.

So someone was trying to become a hand.

Not through continuity.

Through theft.

The scene played in slow, clinical clarity.

A woman stepped forward—hooded, calm, hands steady.

She placed her palm over the etched plate.

The plate pulsed cyan.

For a heartbeat, Haruto thought it worked.

Then the air changed.

Not with heat.

Not with sound.

With **attention**.

A gold-violet ring appeared overhead.

The audit halo.

The room’s lantern light dimmed as if embarrassed to be seen during an inspection.

The codex labeled it without mercy.

> **AUDIT TRIGGERED**

The woman’s eyes widened.

Her palm stayed on the plate, because removing it now would be admitting guilt to a system that didn’t care about admissions.

The plate pulsed again—brighter.

The etched lines crawled up her arm like cyan veins, copying themselves into her skin in a pattern that looked like circuitry pretending to be a tattoo.

Haruto’s stomach twisted.

Copying the pattern wasn’t just forging a key.

It was rewriting a body.

The codex displayed a note like a safety warning no one had bothered to print.

> **COPY EFFECT:** unauthorized hand formation  
> Side effect: audit visibility increased

The audit halo rotated.

Once.

Twice.

Then it stopped directly over the woman’s head.

A thin line of gold-violet light fell from it like a cursor selecting an object.

The room froze.

Not quiet.

Frozen.

The same stillness Haruto had felt when permissions changed.

Then—

the Correction Sentinel manifested.

Not from a portal.

From the fact that the world had decided a tool was required.

It unfolded in the center of the room like an equation resolving.

The people around the table flinched.

Some reached for weapons.

Some reached for prayers.

The Sentinel did not acknowledge them.

It looked at the woman’s arm.

At the copied cyan lines.

At the hand that had tried to become a key.

And it performed an operation.

**Destroy.**

The copied pattern didn’t burn.

It didn’t rip.

It simply… *vanished*.

Like a file deleted.

The woman’s arm went pale where cyan had been, as if the system had removed something and left the skin to remember it had never belonged.

The woman gasped and stumbled back.

The Sentinel turned its tool-arm again.

**Build.**

A new lattice formed in the air—gold-violet and thin as guilt.

It wrapped around the etched plate on the table.

Not as a cage.

As a seal.

The plate dimmed.

The copied key pattern became unreadable, like ink washed away.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

It didn’t punish the thieves.

It removed the possibility.

It didn’t teach a lesson.

It enforced a constraint.

The codex offered the conclusion with brutal simplicity.

> **COPYING DOES NOT CREATE AUTHORITY.**  
> It creates an audit trail.

The Sentinel turned its head slightly—no face, no eyes, but Haruto felt the attention pivot anyway.

For a heartbeat, Haruto thought it was looking at him through the codex.

The codex text flickered.

Then it added one more line, almost like a reluctant confession.

> **If the world becomes desperate, it may allow unauthorized hands.**  
> Desperation changes tolerances.

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

So the system that refused copies now…

could choose to accept them later.

Not because it wanted to.

Because it needed to.

The vision snapped shut.

Haruto’s breath returned in a rush.

His wrist was warm.

Not warning-hot.

Not Overdrive-hot.

Warm like a device reacting to the idea of someone else trying to touch its controls.

The cyan tab pulsed once, quiet as a threat.

And Haruto understood something that made his teeth ache:

If factions learned they could copy the handprint—

then “ownership” wouldn’t just be political.

It would be mechanical.

And the world would defend itself against them…

until the day it decided it couldn’t afford to.
