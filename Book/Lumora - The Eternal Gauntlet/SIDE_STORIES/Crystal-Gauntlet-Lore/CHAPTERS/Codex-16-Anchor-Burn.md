## Codex 16 — Anchor Burn

Haruto wanted to believe “two hands” meant safety.

That was the human mistake.

Humans heard *shared* and thought *less.*

Less pain.
Less cost.
Less sacrifice.

But Lumora didn’t do less.

Lumora did balance.

Balance meant the cost moved.

The cyan tab pulsed at the edge of his vision like a bruise remembering it had teeth.

Haruto waited until the world was quiet enough to pretend it wasn’t listening.

Then he opened the codex.

The world blinked.

He stood in a room made of thresholds.

Not a workshop.

Not the World Core chamber.

A smaller space—two circles of light on the floor, connected by a thin cyan thread like a line drawn between wrists.

Above them, the audit halo rotated slowly, its gold-violet scan line so faint it could almost be mistaken for moonlight.

Almost.

The codex text appeared in cyan with a faint Verdant undertone.

> **ANCHOR EVENT**  
> Condition: shared continuity engaged  
> Result: rewrite stabilized  
> Cost: anchor scar

Haruto’s throat tightened.

Anchor scar.

He stared at the two circles.

One circle pulsed with cyan heat—Overdrive pressure, the rewrite trying to happen.

The other circle pulsed with pale green steadiness—Verdant damping, continuity discipline, the hand that stayed.

He watched the two silhouettes in the circles.

Not him and Elara.

A prior pair.

The outsider stood in the cyan circle, trembling like a system under load.

The local stood in the green circle, palm lifted—not dramatic, not romantic.

Practical.

Ready.

The cyan thread between them brightened.

Overdrive surged.

The world around them trembled like brass under a hammer.

Haruto’s teeth ached with recognition.

Then the local’s hand closed gently around the outsider’s wrist.

The cyan surge… slowed.

The tremor became a vibration.

The vibration became a hum.

The hum became *held.*

The codex displayed a calm note as if describing a simple engineering solution.

> **STABILIZATION:** achieved via continuity sink

Sink.

Haruto’s stomach twisted.

A sink didn’t erase water.

It *takes it in.*

The local’s skin began to glow faintly.

Not gold.

Not cyan.

A bruised green with cyan inclusions—like Verdant glass cracked and filled with light.

The glow crawled up their arm in thin, branching lines.

Not a tattoo.

A stain.

The codex labeled it without sentiment.

> **ANCHOR BURN**

Haruto’s breath caught.

The local didn’t scream.

They didn’t flinch.

But their eyes widened as if something inside their head had just shifted a half-step out of alignment.

The outsider’s Overdrive pressure eased.

The world steadied.

And the anchor burned anyway.

Haruto whispered, “So it’s not free.”

The codex answered, cruelly honest.

> **Shared ignition is not less ignition.**  
> It is redistributed ignition.

The scene shifted.

Haruto watched the anchor stumble after the event ended, not from physical injury—

but from *memory bleed.*

Their gaze flicked left, then right, as if searching for a UI that wasn’t there.

They muttered a phrase that didn’t belong in their mouth.

Something like “quest update.”

Something like “tip.”

Haruto’s throat tightened.

Identity drift.

Host content leaking into the anchor.

The codex labeled it:

> **SIDE EFFECT:** temporary role contamination  
> Severity: variable  
> Note: worsens under audit pressure

The audit halo overhead rotated faster, as if pleased to have something new to measure.

Haruto felt cold settle in his chest.

Because the cost wasn’t “Haruto lives.”

The cost was “someone else carries the scorch marks.”

And Haruto didn’t know which was worse:

Being used as fuel—

or being the reason someone else got burned.

The codex displayed its final line in small cyan text, like a warning label tucked where you’d only see it after you’d already bought the product.

> **Anchor burn is survivable.**  
> It is not harmless.  
> Do not call it romance.

The vision snapped shut.

Haruto blinked into the real world, palms damp.

His wrist was warm.

And for the first time since he’d read “two hands,” Haruto felt something colder than fear:

Responsibility, sharpened into a blade.
