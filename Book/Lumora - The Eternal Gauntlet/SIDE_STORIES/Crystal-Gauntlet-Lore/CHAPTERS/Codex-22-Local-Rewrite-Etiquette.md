## Codex 22 — Local Rewrite Etiquette

The System didn’t teach Haruto morality.

It taught him *formatting*.

It taught him which kinds of suffering were acceptable as long as they were clean, reversible, and didn’t make the world’s audit halo rotate too fast.

Haruto learned that when the cyan tab pulsed like a polite reminder.

Not urgent.

Not violent.

Administrative.

He opened it.

The world blinked.

He stood in a room that looked like a tutorial level built by a bureaucrat.

White stone.

Neat lines.

A single lever labeled in gold:

> **LOCAL REWRITE**

Haruto’s stomach tightened.

The lever wasn’t attached to a machine.

It was attached to *meaning*.

Every time the world had “agreed” with him violently—Overdrive—Haruto had felt it as chaos.

Here, the codex reframed it as a tool with instructions.

The cyan text appeared in front of him.

> **LOCAL REWRITE ETIQUETTE**  
> Goal: stabilize without spectacle  
> Rule: edits must look like optimization

Haruto swallowed. “Optimization.”

The codex didn’t respond like a friend.

It responded like a manual.

The room split into three lanes, each with a simple scenario playing on repeat like a training montage you didn’t want to earn.

### Lane 1: **The Clean Edit**

Haruto watched a scene in a city street:

Two factions nearly collided.

A Radiant patrol turned a corner.

A Veil courier froze.

Tension spiked.

Then the world performed a tiny rewrite.

Not dramatic.

Not loud.

The courier’s foot slipped on a wet stone that “had always been wet.”

The patrol didn’t see them.

No confrontation.

No fight.

No unity.

No growth.

Just… avoidance.

The codex stamped it:

> **EDIT TYPE:** acceptable  
> Outcome: reduced variance  
> Audit risk: low

Haruto’s throat tightened.

So the world liked edits that prevented conflict…

as long as they also prevented connection.

### Lane 2: **The Useful Lie**

Haruto watched a tribunal chamber replay.

A councilor said, “This is for your protection.”

Haruto refused.

The room tensed.

Witness keys flared.

Then the world rewrote something small.

A guard coughed—loud enough to break the moment.

The councilor softened their tone.

The outcome shifted from “cage” to “compromise.”

Haruto still got leashed.

But the leash was less obvious.

The codex labeled it:

> **EDIT TYPE:** recommended  
> Outcome: compliance preserved  
> Audit risk: minimal

Haruto’s stomach twisted.

Recommended.

The world was teaching him how to make his own cage look like a choice.

### Lane 3: **The Forbidden Proof**

Haruto watched a mixed group laugh together.

Unity held.

Not avoidance.

Not compromise.

Real connection.

Then someone said something sincere out loud—something that could be called proof.

The audit halo sharpened.

The Correction Engine preloaded.

The world prepared to punish the spike.

The codex stamped the lane with a warning.

> **EDIT TYPE:** discouraged  
> Outcome: coherence persists  
> Audit risk: high

Haruto’s chest went tight with anger.

The System wasn’t just saying “don’t be dramatic.”

It was saying:

Don’t be *real* in a way witnesses can verify.

Haruto whispered, “So what do you want me to do?”

The codex answered with one sentence that made his teeth ache.

> **Be kind in ways that do not change the system.**

Haruto’s cuff warmed.

The lever labeled LOCAL REWRITE glowed faintly, inviting.

Haruto stepped closer and saw the small print carved into the stone beneath it.

Not poetic.

Legal.

> **By using Local Rewrite, Host acknowledges responsibility for outcomes.**

Haruto swallowed.

Responsibility.

The world loved that word.

It meant: if you touch the lever, the blame becomes yours.

He backed away.

The codex didn’t accuse him.

It simply displayed a calm conclusion.

> **Local rewrite is tolerated when it reduces variance.**  
> **Local rewrite is punished when it creates new rules.**

The vision snapped shut.

Haruto returned to the real world with a cold understanding that made his palms damp:

The world wasn’t afraid of rewriting.

It was afraid of rewriting that made people freer.
