## Codex 28 — Witness Hunger

Haruto thought the safest place was the place with the fewest eyes.

That was an Earth instinct.

On Earth, privacy meant less trouble.

In Lumora, privacy meant the world couldn’t *verify* you.

And the world hated uncertainty more than it hated violence.

The cyan tab didn’t pulse.

It appeared already awake—steady, present, like an icon that had stopped asking permission.

Haruto stared at it and felt his throat tighten.

He had been trying, lately, to be quiet.

To keep his party’s unity small and steady.

To stop feeding the correction layer with proof.

He had been trying to starve the witnesses.

The codex opened anyway.

The world blinked.

He stood in a street that didn’t belong to any faction.

Not Radiant-bright.

Not Veil-dim.

Ordinary.

Which, in Lumora, meant suspicious.

People moved past him.

Not staring.

Not whispering.

Not laughing.

Just living.

Haruto’s chest eased for half a heartbeat.

Then the codex text appeared in cyan.

> **WITNESS HUNGER**  
> When witness keys decrease, audit demand increases.  
> The system will manufacture visibility.

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

Manufacture.

The street shifted into the system’s view.

Haruto saw tiny icons flicker at the corners of ordinary faces—sun-seals that weren’t pinned to people so much as pinned to *proximity.*

Most were dim.

Some were missing.

And in the places where they were missing, the air itself began to change.

Not with sound.

With behavior.

A vendor turned at the exact wrong time and looked directly at Haruto’s wrist.

Not curious.

Compelled.

Haruto felt his cuff warm, the smallest pulse of “noticed.”

The vendor blinked, frowning, as if waking from a thought they didn’t remember choosing.

Then the vendor laughed too loudly and called out to no one in particular:

“Hey! Did you hear? The Player’s around!”

Haruto froze.

The street’s attention swiveled like a flock changing direction.

Witness keys flared—sun-seals blinking into existence where there had been none.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

The system hadn’t waited for eyes.

It had *made* them.

The codex highlighted the vendor’s icon and labeled it with brutal calm.

> **SEEDED WITNESS:** activated  
> Trigger: visibility deficit  
> Method: social provocation

Haruto’s stomach twisted.

So the system didn’t just record rumors.

It planted rumor carriers when the street went quiet.

The scene advanced.

Haruto tried to step away.

Quietly.

Non-dramatically.

He tried to be boring.

The system punished boredom by making it loud.

A child bumped into him—hard enough to jostle his sleeve back.

Haruto’s cuff flashed once.

Just warm.

Just enough.

The child gasped, eyes wide, then shouted:

“It’s glowing!”

Haruto’s chest went cold.

Witness keys flared again.

The codex’s note appeared like a cruel tooltip.

> **NOTE:**  
> Hiding reduces witness clarity.  
> Reduced clarity increases witness creation.

Haruto swallowed hard.

So starving witnesses wasn’t a solution.

It was a signal.

It told the system:

*Something important is happening off-camera.*

And the system responded the way any nervous machine responded:

By turning the cameras back on.

The audit halo appeared overhead—faint at first, then sharper as the crowd’s attention synchronized.

Haruto felt the pressure of it like a hand hovering over his spine.

Not deployed.

Not yet.

But preloading.

The codex displayed its conclusion in small cyan text.

> **When you deny the world witnesses, it becomes hungry.**  
> Hunger creates eyes.  
> Eyes create audits.

The vision snapped shut.

Haruto blinked into the real street again.

People were staring.

Rumors were already moving mouth to mouth like sparks in dry grass.

And Haruto realized the worst part:

He hadn’t spiked.

He hadn’t proved anything.

He had simply tried to be quiet.

And the world, unable to tolerate quiet uncertainty, had made noise for him.
