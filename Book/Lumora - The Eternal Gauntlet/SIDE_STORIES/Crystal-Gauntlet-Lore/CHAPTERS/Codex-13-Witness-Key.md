## Codex 13 — Witness Key

Haruto used to think being watched was a feeling.

Then he came to Lumora and learned it was an input.

He learned it the way you learn a stove is hot:

not from being told,

but from touching it once and remembering the burn forever.

After the hearing.
After the contract.
After the betting board.

Haruto started noticing a new kind of attention.

Not the loud attention—the laughter, the rumors, the “did you hear?”

A quieter kind.

The kind that sat in the corner of his vision like a speck of dust he couldn’t blink away.

Sometimes it looked like a sun-seal.

Sometimes it looked like nothing at all.

Sometimes it felt like the air itself was holding a pen.

The cyan tab pulsed.

Haruto stared at it and whispered, “If this is another warning, I’m going to uninstall my eyeballs.”

The tab pulsed again.

The world blinked.

He stood in a tribunal chamber again—marble, gold, sunlight like judgment.

But this wasn’t the hearing.

This was the *system’s view* of it.

The room was layered.

Haruto saw the same people—councilors, guards, witnesses in the gallery—

but behind them floated faint shapes, translucent and geometric, like icons that had forgotten to fully hide.

The codex text appeared in cyan.

> **WITNESS KEY**  
> Witnesses are not always people.  
> Some witnesses are mechanisms.

Haruto’s stomach tightened.

Mechanisms disguised as eyes.

The codex highlighted a point in the gallery: a woman in a plain robe, hands folded, face neutral.

She looked ordinary.

Which, in Lumora, was suspicious.

Above her head, a tiny symbol flickered.

Not visible to the crowd.

Visible to the codex.

A sun-seal.

Small.

Unobtrusive.

Perfectly placed to be ignored.

The codex labeled it.

> **AUDIT BEACON**  
> Disguise: civilian witness  
> Function: record noncompliance  
> Output: correction layer feed

Haruto’s throat tightened.

So “witness factor” wasn’t just social.

It was technical.

The codex zoomed out.

Haruto saw more.

Another witness—this one a laughing man in a tavern—had a faint violet thread at his collar like a tie.

Above him, a tiny icon blinked: a folded paper symbol, pinned.

Haruto recognized it with nausea.

The Veil contract.

Refused.

Pinned anyway.

The codex displayed the line like a forensic note.

> **WITNESS NETWORK:** rumor carriers  
> Some are organic.  
> Some are seeded.

Seeded.

Haruto’s pulse spiked.

So the world didn’t just *react* to rumors.

It planted them.

Not with speeches.

With nodes.

With carriers.

With witnesses that weren’t there to judge him—

but to **route him**.

The codex highlighted another layer, faint gold-violet above the room like a ceiling fan of light.

The audit halo.

But smaller.

Distributed.

Not a single eye.

A swarm of tiny, invisible cursors that moved when the crowd’s attention moved.

Haruto felt sick.

The world didn’t have one watcher.

It had a concept called “being seen,” and it had made that concept into infrastructure.

The codex offered a single, brutal instruction.

> **Rule:**  
> If you want a loophole, reduce witness keys.  
> If you want unity, accept witnesses and pay the cost.

Haruto’s wrist warmed.

He saw a cyan thread extend from his cuff into the room, and wherever it touched, the witness icons brightened—like his existence increased their sensitivity.

He wanted to tear the thread out.

The codex shifted again.

Now he saw a street.

A normal street.

A vendor laughing.

A child pointing.

Two guards talking.

And in the corner of Haruto’s vision—tiny, almost nothing—

a sun-seal.

The same speck he’d been noticing in real life.

The codex labeled it with merciless calm.

> **WITNESS KEY (ACTIVE):**  
> You are in view.  
> You are being remembered.

Haruto swallowed hard.

He tried to look away.

The seal stayed.

It wasn’t on a wall.

It wasn’t on a person.

It was on *him*.

Like an overlay.

Like a cursor that had selected the Player and refused to deselect.

The codex’s final line appeared, small and sharp.

> **Some keys open doors.**  
> **Witness keys open audits.**

The vision snapped shut.

Haruto blinked into the real world and immediately checked the corner of his vision.

Nothing.

Then—

just for a heartbeat—

a tiny sun-seal flickered and vanished.

Haruto’s breath caught.

He didn’t know who had seen him.

He didn’t know what had been recorded.

But he knew what he’d been taught:

In Lumora, “being watched” wasn’t paranoia.

It was a mechanic.
