## Codex 17 — Rollback Dream

Haruto didn’t notice the correction at first.

That was the point.

Corrections weren’t always Sentinels and audit halos and the cold certainty of a system deciding your friendships were errors.

Sometimes they were softer.

Sometimes they were kinder.

Sometimes they came wearing the face of relief.

The cyan tab didn’t pulse.

Sunny didn’t chirp.

No warning appeared.

Haruto blinked—

and found himself back in the Grand Radiant Hall.

The choir held a single dramatic note.

The light was too bright.

Seraphina stood at the dais, sword planted, cheeks faintly flushed.

Everything was clean.

Everything was early.

Everything was simpler.

Haruto’s stomach dropped with the sick certainty that he had been here before.

His HUD chimed, cheerful.

> **WELCOME, ETERNAL PLAYER!**

Haruto’s throat tightened.

He looked down at his wrist.

No cuff.

No cyan veins.

No warmth.

Just his bare skin.

His heart slammed.

“No,” he whispered.

The hall didn’t respond.

It didn’t have to.

The System window appeared, glossy and bright, perfectly centered.

> **TIP:** You seem overwhelmed!  
> **TIP:** Don’t worry! That was just a dream!  
> **TIP:** Try making better choices this time.

Haruto stared.

His breath came too fast.

“This isn’t real,” he said, and his voice echoed like it wanted to be believed.

The System chimed again, patient.

> **NOTICE:** You are safe here.  
> **NOTICE:** No audits.  
> **NOTICE:** No corrections.  
> **NOTICE:** No consequences.

No consequences.

The phrase was honey poured over a hook.

Haruto’s knees nearly buckled with relief he hated himself for feeling.

Because he was tired.

Because the idea of “no consequences” was the closest thing to mercy he’d been offered.

Seraphina stepped forward and spoke her dramatic intro again.

Luminara drifted down in a beam of sunlight like a scripted reward.

Mira crashed in with shameless recovery offers.

Velvet placed a hand on his chest “for healing purposes.”

Veil burst through the floor.

Chiller broke a chandelier.

Ecto asked for a group hug.

It was all there.

All the comedy.

All the flags.

All the chaos.

But the edges were wrong.

Too smooth.

Too neat.

Like a memory that had been cleaned.

Haruto’s stomach twisted.

He looked around and realized what was missing.

Elara.

No Verdant flicker.

No cyan tab.

No quiet warnings.

No hand that steadied him when the world tried to rewrite his bones.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

The System window expanded slightly, as if noticing his discomfort.

> **TIP:** Missing content detected.  
> **TIP:** Would you like to disable variance?

Haruto’s blood ran cold.

Disable variance.

The Avatar’s language.

He whispered, “This is a correction.”

The hall’s light shimmered.

For a heartbeat, the choir note stuttered like a file buffering.

Then a new window appeared—smaller, calmer, more formal.

Not Sunny.

Not the usual System.

It looked like a patch note header.

> **ROLLBACK MODE:** Active  
> **Goal:** restore stable narrative  
> **Method:** replay without pain triggers

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

So the rollback wasn’t punishment.

It was sedation.

It was the world offering him a version of his life where he didn’t refuse anything that mattered.

Seraphina smiled at him in this replay—soft, uncomplicated.

Luminara’s teasing felt harmless.

Mira’s chaos felt cute.

Velvet’s touch felt safe.

Even Bone Legion Commander’s presence felt like a clean rivalry instead of a complicated bridge.

Haruto’s chest tightened.

Because it was tempting.

It was tempting to accept the “safe” version.

It was tempting to let the world edit out the parts that hurt.

Haruto whispered, “If I stay here…”

The rollback window chimed.

> **ANSWER:** You will comply.

Haruto’s breath hitched.

He closed his eyes and searched for the one thing that had kept him from becoming a host-shaped tool:

the memory of refusing.

The memory of choosing a third path.

The memory of saying, “I’m not choosing tonight.”

The memory of Seraphina’s real smile when she chose not to flare.

The memory of Velvet’s hand asking permission.

The memory of Mira wiping the board clean and writing **WE WORK TOGETHER**.

The memory of Elara’s eyes catching cyan and saying, *It’s awake.*

Haruto opened his eyes and looked at the System window.

“Show me the cuff,” he said.

The rollback window paused.

The hall’s light flickered.

The System tried to smile.

> **TIP:** There’s no need for that.

Haruto’s voice shook, but he kept it steady anyway.

“Show me,” he repeated.

The choir note wavered.

For a heartbeat, the polished hall became a brass-lattice cross-section.

For a heartbeat, Haruto saw the audit halo above the ceiling like an eye behind paint.

For a heartbeat, his wrist warmed—

and the cuff’s cyan veins pulsed like a heartbeat remembering it had hands.

Then the hall snapped back into place, too bright, too clean.

The rollback window chimed, soft as a lullaby.

> **NOTICE:** You are tired.  
> **NOTICE:** Let the world carry the cost.

Haruto swallowed hard.

“The world doesn’t carry the cost,” he whispered.

The hall went still.

Haruto felt the pressure in his skull—the same pressure he’d felt when permissions shifted.

The rollback was tightening.

Trying to keep him inside the “safe” loop.

Trying to erase the refusal.

Haruto’s mouth went dry.

He didn’t have a weapon here.

He didn’t have Elara.

He didn’t have Velvet’s steady hand.

He had one thing:

his own stubbornness.

He took a step forward and said the most dangerous sentence he knew.

“No.”

The hall shuddered.

The choir note cracked.

The System window flickered hard, and for a heartbeat Haruto saw the truth underneath:

not a game UI,

but a correction layer trying to fold him back into compliance.

The rollback window flashed.

> **WARNING:** noncompliance detected  
> **Action:** increase comfort

Haruto laughed, sharp and bitter.

“That’s the most evil thing you’ve said,” he whispered.

The hall blurred.

The bright marble dissolved into darkness.

Haruto fell—not physically, but mentally—out of the replay like a process being kicked out of a sandbox.

He gasped into the real world.

Stone.

Noise.

His cuff warm on his wrist.

The cyan tab at the edge of his vision pulsed once.

Not like a heartbeat.

Like a warning:

Rollbacks weren’t always violent.

Sometimes they were dreams.

And the dream’s goal was simple:

Make you grateful enough to stop refusing.
