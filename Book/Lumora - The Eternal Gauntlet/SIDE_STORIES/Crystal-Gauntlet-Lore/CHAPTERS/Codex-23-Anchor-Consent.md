## Codex 23 — Anchor Consent

Haruto didn’t realize consent was a mechanic until he saw what happened when it was missing.

That was the cruel joke of Lumora:

It gave you rules only after you broke them.

The cyan tab pulsed once, quiet as a warning you didn’t want to read.

Haruto opened it.

The world blinked.

He stood in a Verdant field again—soft green light, vines curling with too much geometry to be nature.

Under the soil, brass lattice pulsed like a quiet heart.

Above it, the audit halo rotated slowly, patient as weather.

Two circles marked the ground.

Cyan.

Green.

The two-hand module.

Haruto swallowed.

Then the codex showed him the wrong version.

The outsider stood in the cyan circle, wrist burning with rewrite pressure.

The anchor stood in the green circle, steady—but wary.

Their posture wasn’t “ready.”

It was “uncertain.”

Haruto’s stomach tightened.

He could feel the edge of the mistake before it happened.

The outsider reached across the thread.

Fast.

Desperate.

And grabbed the anchor’s wrist.

Not a touch.

A grip.

The cyan thread flared.

The field shivered.

The anchor’s eyes widened—not with pain, but with violated choice.

The audit halo overhead sharpened immediately.

Not gradually.

Not politely.

Like a cursor snapping to a critical error.

The codex text appeared in cyan, calm and merciless.

> **UNAUTHORIZED CONTINUITY ATTEMPT**  
> Status: coercion detected  
> Result: audit escalation immediate

Haruto’s throat tightened.

So it wasn’t “two hands” that mattered.

It was two hands that *agreed*.

The field’s green light dimmed.

The brass lattice under the soil brightened in gold-violet instead, as if Verdant itself had been overridden by a compliance layer.

Haruto felt sick.

The world didn’t tolerate forced continuity.

Not because it was moral.

Because it was unstable.

Coercion created variance spikes the system couldn’t predict.

The audit halo rotated faster.

The Correction Sentinel manifested.

Not slowly.

Instantly.

The same function given legs.

Tool-arms.

No face.

No mercy.

The sentinel didn’t attack the outsider first.

It attacked the **link**.

**Destroy.**

The cyan thread between them didn’t snap like rope.

It vanished like a line deleted.

The outsider stumbled.

The anchor staggered.

And then the sentinel performed **Build**.

A gold-violet lattice formed around the anchor’s wrist.

Not a cuff.

Not a chain.

A stabilization clamp.

It made the anchor still.

Not calm.

Still.

Haruto’s teeth ached with recognition.

Temporary host lockout.

Containment.

The codex labeled it like an internal report.

> **CORRECTION:** prevent forced anchoring  
> Method: immobilize anchor  
> Note: anchors must remain voluntary to be reliable

Voluntary.

Reliable.

Haruto’s chest tightened.

The system didn’t value consent as kindness.

It valued consent as **engineering**.

The scene shifted again.

Haruto saw the right version now—the same two circles, the same thread.

But this time the outsider didn’t grab.

They spoke.

Quietly.

Honestly.

“May I?” the outsider asked.

The anchor hesitated.

Then nodded once.

The touch that followed was gentle.

The cyan thread brightened and steadied.

The audit halo passed overhead without sharpening.

Haruto felt his chest ache.

Because the difference wasn’t power.

It was respect.

The codex displayed the rule in a single line, cyan with faint green undertone.

> **ANCHOR CONSENT IS THE FIRST DISCIPLINE.**

Haruto swallowed hard.

He thought of Velvet asking “May I?” before touching his wrist.

He thought of Elara warning instead of claiming.

He thought of the Avatar presenting choices that weren’t choices.

He thought of factions trying to copy handprints and call it authority.

Consent was the one thing the system couldn’t counterfeit cleanly.

The codex’s final note appeared like a margin warning.

> **If you force an anchor, the world will correct you faster than it corrects you for love.**  
> **Because coercion is variance.**

The vision snapped shut.

Haruto returned to the real world with his palms damp and his throat tight.

Two hands wasn’t a loophole you could steal.

It was a discipline you could only earn.
