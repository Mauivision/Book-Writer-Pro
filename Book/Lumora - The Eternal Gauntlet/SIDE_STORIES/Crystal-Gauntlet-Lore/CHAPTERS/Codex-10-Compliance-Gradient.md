## Codex 10 — Compliance Gradient

Haruto used to think “bad luck” was random.

Then he came to Lumora.

Then the System started using the phrase **micro-incident frequency** like it was a setting you could adjust.

Then Mira wiped a chalkboard clean and the room itself seemed to inhale like it had been waiting for permission to believe a different story.

Haruto didn’t open the codex to learn something new this time.

He opened it to confirm a suspicion.

The cyan tab pulsed once, like a heartbeat trying to pretend it wasn’t attached to him.

Haruto selected it anyway.

The world blinked.

He stood in a place that felt like a map screen rendered as architecture.

Not the planet.

A *system.*

Layers floated in the air like panes of glass:

- a **social layer**: faces, conversations, laughter, rumors
- a **mechanic layer**: jealousy aggro, bond events, reputation tags
- a **correction layer**: audit halos, sentinel manifests, rollback operations

They stacked neatly, like someone had built reality in modules.

The codex text appeared in cyan.

> **COMPLIANCE GRADIENT**  
> Corrections are expensive.  
> Most adjustment occurs before correction.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

Before correction.

So the Sentinel wasn’t the first response.

It was the last.

The codex highlighted the lowest layer first—the one Haruto recognized too well.

The social layer brightened.

He watched a tavern scene play out in miniature like a toy stage:

A laugh.

A glance.

A rumor passed from mouth to ear like a coin changing hands.

Then the codex overlaid a tiny slider above the stage.

It looked like a difficulty bar.

On the left: a smiling face icon.

In the middle: a neutral face icon.

On the right: a warning triangle.

The slider moved one notch to the right.

And the stage changed.

Two patrons bumped shoulders.

A drink spilled.

Someone made a joke with teeth.

The laughter wasn’t warmer.

It was sharper.

The codex labeled the notch with a calm sentence that made Haruto’s stomach twist.

> **SOFT ROLLBACK:** nudge perception  
> Method: shame, gossip, social pressure

Haruto swallowed.

Soft rollback.

As if embarrassment was a feature.

The slider moved another notch.

The stage shifted again.

Now, the laughter triggered something.

A tiny slime spawned under a table.

A chair leg snapped.

A candle guttered at the wrong moment.

The codex labeled it:

> **LOW-COST CORRECTION:** micro-incidents  
> Method: inconvenience + reinforcement  
> Goal: reduce coherence

Reduce coherence.

Haruto’s chest tightened.

He thought of Seraphina’s shame.

Of Velvet’s warning.

Of Mira’s “we control the story.”

The world wasn’t just reacting to unity with violence.

It was reacting with *social engineering.*

The slider moved again.

The stage darkened.

Now, rumor turned to targeting.

Faces turned.

Whispers became directives.

“He’s Radiant.”
“He’s Veil.”
“He’s dangerous.”
“He’s a prize.”

Haruto saw himself in the middle of it, smaller than the story that swallowed him.

The codex labeled the notch:

> **BEHAVIOR SHAPING:** faction capture attempts  
> Method: offers framed as safety  
> Method: threats framed as duty

Haruto’s teeth ached.

Radiant hearing.

Veil contract.

Both cages.

Different lighting.

The slider hit the warning triangle.

The stage froze.

And above it, the audit halo appeared—gold-violet light rotating like an eye.

The codex displayed the next line with the merciless calm of a system log.

> **HARD CORRECTION:** sentinel deployment  
> Trigger: coherence persists despite low-cost layers

Haruto stared.

So the world tried to “correct” him with gossip first.

With shame.

With reputation.

With micro-incidents.

And only if he kept refusing—

only if he kept holding unity anyway—

then it sent the Sentinel.

Haruto’s voice came out rough. “Why?”

The codex answered like it had been waiting for the question.

> **Reason:** cost optimization  
> A world that corrects too hard breaks itself.  
> Soft rollback preserves infrastructure.

Infrastructure.

Haruto’s stomach twisted.

So the world wasn’t cruel because it enjoyed cruelty.

It was cruel because it was trying to survive efficiently.

The codex highlighted a small line beneath the compliance gradient.

It was written in a tone Haruto recognized from Sunny’s hidden base note—flat, log-like.

> **MONITOR NOTE:**  
> Hosts respond better to “social consequences” than existential terror.

Haruto’s breath caught.

Sunny.

So Sunny’s jokes—

Sunny’s snark—

Sunny’s constant noise—

Was part of the soft rollback layer too.

Not just a panic loop.

A compliance tool.

Haruto’s cuff warmed.

He saw a faint cyan thread run up through the layers like a wire connecting modules.

It linked the social layer to the correction layer.

Linked laughter to audits.

Linked rumors to sentinels.

Linked “bond events” to error flags.

Haruto’s chest tightened.

He’d thought he was fighting a dungeon.

He’d thought he was fighting factions.

He’d thought he was fighting gods who published patch notes like jokes.

He was fighting something simpler and worse:

A system that had learned the cheapest way to make people comply.

The codex’s final line appeared, small and cold.

> **If you want to avoid corrections, become less coherent.**  
> **If you want to save the world, remain coherent anyway.**

The vision snapped shut.

Haruto’s breath returned too fast.

His eyes stung.

The tavern noise returned in his memory like an echo.

And the most horrifying part wasn’t the Sentinel.

It was the knowledge that the world would try to correct him with laughter first.
