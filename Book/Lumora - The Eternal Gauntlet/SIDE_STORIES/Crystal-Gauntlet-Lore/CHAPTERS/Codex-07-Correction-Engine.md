## Codex 07 — Correction Engine

Haruto tried not to think about the word **correction**.

Thinking about it made it feel closer.

And Haruto had learned, the hard way, that Lumora treated attention like a beacon.

So he did what he always did when he was trying not to think:

He focused on the small things.

On the scrape of boots on stone.
On Mira’s grumbling about Sunny’s “chirp taxes.”
On Seraphina’s careful, furious silence.
On the way Elara’s eyes kept sliding upward as if the ceiling might open and laugh again.

On the warm weight of the cuff at his wrist, quiet as a sleeping animal that was still, somehow, listening.

It didn’t help.

The cyan tab pulsed anyway.

Once.

Then twice.

Then it held steady, like a notification that had decided it wasn’t optional content anymore.

Haruto whispered, “Not now.”

The tab did not care.

It pulsed again.

And the world blinked.

Haruto found himself standing in a corridor that wasn’t a corridor.

It was a cross-section of one.

A diagram made into space.

The walls were not stone.

They were layers of brass geometry and translucent light, stacked like a blueprint you could walk through. Under his feet, lines ran in neat grids—some gold, some violet, some cyan, and some a pale green that made his chest tighten with recognition.

Verdant.

There was no sky.

There was a ceiling of shifting symbols, moving slowly like a system log scrolling too large to read.

The codex text appeared in front of him in cyan, crisp and calm.

> **CORRECTION ENGINE**  
> Purpose: maintain equilibrium  
> Method: detect destabilizing edits  
> Method: rollback unauthorized unity

Haruto’s throat went dry.

Rollback.

That word belonged to software.

Not to people.

The corridor shimmered, and Haruto saw it—an overlay snapping into place across the layers.

A halo.

Not holy.

Not beautiful.

An **audit ring**: a thin band of gold-violet light that rotated slowly, casting lines over the structure like a scanner.

The codex labeled it with a single, brutal term.

> **AUDIT HALO**

Haruto felt his skin prickle as the halo swept over him.

It didn’t hurt.

It *evaluated*.

Haruto’s HUD flickered and tried to align itself with the codex view.

He hated that it succeeded.

A list appeared, floating beside him like an error report.

> **ANOMALY REPORT (LOCAL)**  
> - Cross-faction cohesion above baseline  
> - Bond-event stability exceeding tolerance  
> - Unauthorized bridging behavior detected  
> - Host refusal pattern: persistent

Haruto stared at the last line.

Host refusal pattern.

Like his backbone had become a bug.

The corridor changed, and suddenly Haruto was watching a scene from above.

Not a dungeon.

A city street—clean, bright, Radiant.

A mixed group stood together: a Radiant guard laughing with a Veil courier, a healer tending both, a tavern worker handing out bread.

It looked like nothing.

It looked like peace.

Then the audit halo passed overhead.

The light didn’t strike them like a beam.

It *brushed* them.

And the world reacted.

The laughter stuttered.

The bread slipped from hands.

The healer’s glow dimmed as if embarrassed.

Shadows under feet deepened into sharp lines, tethers snapping tight like a trap waking up.

Above the group, a small label appeared:

> **ERROR:** excessive coherence

Haruto’s stomach lurched.

Excessive coherence.

The phrase hit him like a slap because it meant the world had a threshold for kindness.

And then the Correction arrived.

It wasn’t a monster.

It didn’t roar.

It didn’t posture.

It simply *manifested*.

A figure unfolded from the audit halo’s light like a clause unfolding from a contract.

Tall.
Symmetrical.

Its surface was brass and shadow, but unlike the Avatar, it had no “face,” no attempt at humanity.

It was a function given legs.

Its arms ended in tools: not hands, but geometric implements that looked like they belonged in a workshop.

Build.
Destroy.
Move.

Haruto’s pulse spiked.

The codex displayed the name with the calm certainty of something that had never doubted its own necessity.

> **CORRECTION SENTINEL**  
> Role: rollback  
> Target: unity spikes  
> Behavior: remove bridges, restore separation

The Sentinel stepped toward the mixed group.

The Radiant guard lifted a sword.

The Veil courier lifted a knife.

The healer raised a hand.

The Sentinel did not react.

It raised one tool-arm and performed an operation.

**Destroy.**

The air between the Radiant guard and the Veil courier *emptied*.

Not physically.

Conceptually.

The invisible bridge of shared intent—laughter, trust, the willingness to stand together—was removed like a line deleted from a map editor.

The Radiant guard’s smile vanished.

The Veil courier’s shoulders tensed.

They took half a step apart, as if remembering they were supposed to be enemies.

The healer’s glow flickered uncertainly.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

The Sentinel raised its other arm.

**Move.**

The bread that had fallen returned to the tavern worker’s hands—but not as an act of kindness.

As an act of *reset.*

Like the world returning an object to its pre-incident position.

The scene shuddered.

The audit halo rotated faster.

The codex added the next line, a cold, factual note.

> **Corrections do not punish evil.**  
> Corrections punish *change.*

Haruto whispered, “That’s insane.”

The codex did not argue.

It simply showed him more.

The Sentinel turned its tool-arm toward the healer.

The healer’s glow was steady—supportive, practiced.

Velvet’s kind of light.

The Sentinel paused.

Then a small overlay appeared:

> **FLAG:** infrastructure-adjacent stabilization

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

Infrastructure.

The Sentinel didn’t attack the healer directly.

It didn’t need to.

It raised its arm and performed **Build**—adding a rule.

A thin gold-violet lattice formed in the air around the healer’s hands.

It looked like a blessing.

It felt like a cage.

The healer’s light kept trying to pour outward.

The lattice kept shaping it into narrower channels.

Healing became rationing.

Care became compliance.

Haruto’s chest went tight with fury.

He could practically hear Velvet’s calm voice: *healing is political.*

The codex pulled the scene away and brought Haruto back to the diagram corridor.

The audit halo hovered above him now.

He could feel it scanning his wrist.

He could feel it tasting the cyan.

And in the halo’s light, something flickered—brief, thin, and deeply wrong.

Not gold.

Not violet.

A seam of cyan so sharp it looked like a crack in glass.

The codex displayed a warning without drama.

> **NOTICE:** Host proximity increases correction frequency.

Haruto swallowed hard.

So the more he did what the world needed—

the more the world defended itself against him.

His cuff warmed.

His HUD tried to overlay a friendly tip and failed.

Instead, a single line appeared.

> **TIP:** Stop being noticeable.

Haruto’s laugh came out bitter.

“Sure,” he whispered. “Let me just stop existing.”

The audit halo rotated once more, and Haruto saw a final image before the codex released him.

A group like his.

Mixed.

Laughing.

Standing together.

And above them, the Correction Sentinel watching with patience that felt infinite.

Not hate.

Not malice.

The calm of a system waiting for the next spike.

The codex’s final line appeared, small and cold.

> **If the world can be edited, it can be defended.**  
> Unity is not only power.  
> Unity is a trigger.

The vision snapped shut.

Stone returned.

Breath returned.

Noise returned.

Haruto’s heart did not return to normal.

The cyan tab pulsed at the edge of his vision like a bruise that had learned how to blink.

And Haruto realized that “corrections” weren’t a rumor.

They were the world’s immune system.

And he—

He was the infection it had been designed to survive.
