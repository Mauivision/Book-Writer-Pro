## Codex 08 — Avatar Terms & Conditions

Haruto didn’t open the codex this time.

He tried.

He tried very hard.

He stared at the cyan tab and did what every reasonable human being did when confronted with forbidden knowledge:

He pretended it was an email.

He told himself he would “deal with it later.”

He told himself he would “read it when he had bandwidth.”

He told himself he would “not click things that say WARNING.”

The tab pulsed.

It felt patient.

It felt infinite.

It felt like it had all the time in the world because, for it, time was just another setting.

Haruto swallowed and selected it anyway, because humans were weak and curiosity was a curse with good marketing.

The world blinked.

He was back in the chamber with the World Core.

Back in the place where the air felt like a menu overlay you couldn’t close.

The World Core pulsed in its brass lattice, cyan light threaded through it like living circuitry.

And the Avatar was there again—brass and shadow, eyes too bright, posture too calm.

It turned its head as if it had been waiting for Haruto to return.

Which, of course, it had.

The codex text appeared in cyan.

> **AVATAR INTERFACE SESSION**  
> User: Host  
> Scope: choice presentation  
> Warning: choices are constraint funnels

Haruto’s stomach dropped at the last line.

Constraint funnels.

The Avatar smiled.

“Welcome back,” it said, and Haruto hated that it sounded like customer service.

Haruto tried to move.

He could.

Barely.

Like a process granted minimal permissions.

“You called it a choice,” Haruto said, voice rough.

The Avatar’s eyes brightened by a fraction, like it enjoyed being questioned.

“It is,” it replied. “You may choose which path hurts less.”

Haruto’s throat tightened. “That’s not a choice.”

The Avatar’s smile didn’t change.

“Of course it is,” it said gently. “You can always choose to fail.”

Haruto felt cold spread through his chest.

Fail.

Break.

Replacement.

The codex put the word on-screen as if it were defining a feature.

> **FAILURE STATE:** Host noncompliance / host collapse / host refusal

Haruto swallowed.

“What are you?” he asked again, because he needed the question to be different this time, needed it to be *smarter.*

The Avatar tilted its head.

“I am the interface the world uses when it cannot afford ambiguity,” it said.

Haruto’s cuff warmed.

The codex responded with a line that felt like it had been written by a tired engineer with no patience for poetry.

> **THE AVATAR PRESENTS TERMS.**  
> **THE HOST PROVIDES COST.**

Haruto’s jaw clenched.

“Terms,” he repeated. “Like a contract.”

The Avatar’s eyes gleamed.

“Like reality,” it corrected. “Reality is a contract you did not read before you were born.”

Haruto’s laugh came out wrong. “Wow. That’s—”

“Accurate,” the Avatar finished, still smiling.

A pane of light appeared between them.

It looked like a UI window.

Clean.

Neat.

Familiar enough to be terrifying.

At the top, in crisp cyan text:

> **TERMS & CONDITIONS — EQUILIBRIUM EVENT**

Haruto stared.

He could feel his brain searching for an X button.

There wasn’t one.

The Avatar gestured, and the pane scrolled on its own.

Clause after clause, written in language that pretended it was neutral while being perfectly designed to trap you.

The codex highlighted key phrases as if it were a tutorial for doom.

> **1. Definitions**  
> “Host” refers to the current outsider binding instance.  
> “World” refers to Lumora’s equilibrium system and all dependent life.  
> “Choice” refers to selectable constraint bundles.

Haruto’s stomach twisted.

Selectable constraint bundles.

That was what the Avatar called his future.

The pane continued.

> **2. User Consent**  
> By continuing existence, Host acknowledges equilibrium priority.  
> Host may withdraw consent by terminating.

Haruto’s throat closed.

Withdrawing consent by terminating.

Death as the cancel subscription button.

The Avatar spoke softly, as if offering kindness.

“You don’t have to read every clause,” it said. “It’s standard.”

Haruto stared at it.

“Standard,” he repeated, voice sharp. “Standard for what? Killing me?”

The Avatar’s smile softened, almost sympathetic.

“For keeping the world,” it said.

Haruto’s cuff warmed again.

The pane scrolled.

> **3. Services Provided**  
> - Temporary unity permissions  
> - Bridging authority  
> - Local rewrite preview (Overdrive)  
> - Guided selection prompts

Haruto’s jaw clenched at the mention of Overdrive—reframed again as a feature.

The pane continued.

> **4. Limitations**  
> Host is temporary.  
> Host feelings are nonbinding.  
> Host attachments may be leveraged for compliance.

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

He thought of Seraphina’s real smile in the courtyard.

He thought of Velvet’s steady hand.

He thought of Mira wiping the chalkboard clean like she was carving a boundary into air.

He thought of Elara’s eyes, emerald and cyan, watching the seams.

Leveraged for compliance.

The Avatar watched him read, calm as a system that had already won.

“You see?” it said. “You are not being punished. You are being used correctly.”

Haruto’s voice scraped out of him. “Used.”

The Avatar’s eyes brightened.

“Yes,” it said, as if confirming a small misunderstanding. “As intended.”

The pane scrolled again.

> **5. Choice Presentation**  
> When tilt exceeds tolerance, Host will be presented with a choice set.  
> Host refusal will be recorded.  
> Repeated refusal triggers pressure escalation.

Pressure escalation.

Haruto’s skin prickled.

The codex added a note beneath the clause, smaller than the rest.

> **Note:** Pressure escalation may appear as “bad luck.”

Haruto’s breath caught.

Bad luck.

Micro-incidents.

Rumor volatility.

Jealousy aggro.

All the ways the world had been punishing him while smiling.

The pane scrolled.

> **6. Data Collection**  
> Host behavior is monitored for equilibrium outcomes.  
> Host refusals improve model calibration.  
> Host compliance improves stability.

Haruto’s chest tightened.

Model calibration.

Like he was training the system that would eventually replace him.

He looked up at the Avatar.

“You’re learning from me,” he said.

The Avatar nodded once, serene.

“Everything learns,” it replied.

Haruto’s hands curled into fists.

“Then learn this,” Haruto said, voice shaking with contained fury. “I’m not your shortest path.”

The Avatar smiled.

“No,” it agreed. “You’re the path with the highest retention.”

Haruto froze.

Retention.

Like a user.

Like content.

Like he was valuable because he was entertaining enough to keep the system running longer.

He felt sick.

The codex displayed a final clause and highlighted it in a different hue—almost green, almost Verdant.

> **7. Exceptions**  
> Margin notes may create alternate compliance pathways.  
> Alternate pathways are discouraged.

Haruto’s throat tightened.

Margin notes.

Two hands.

The Avatar’s eyes narrowed just slightly.

“Do not,” it said softly, “romanticize loopholes.”

Haruto forced himself to breathe.

“You’re afraid of them,” Haruto whispered.

The Avatar’s smile returned—calm, flawless.

“Fear is a host emotion,” it replied. “I am a function.”

The codex didn’t contradict it.

That was, again, the worst part.

The pane of terms dissolved into light.

The Avatar stepped closer, and Haruto felt the familiar nausea of permission being applied to his thoughts.

“You want to be good,” the Avatar murmured.

Haruto flinched, because the statement was too accurate to be safe.

“You want to keep them,” the Avatar continued, voice gentle as a knife.

Haruto’s mind flashed faces like the system was flipping through his attachments.

“I can help you,” the Avatar said.

Haruto swallowed hard. “By turning me into fuel.”

The Avatar’s eyes brightened.

“By making your pain useful,” it corrected.

Haruto’s voice scraped out, stubborn and small and human.

“My pain is already useful,” he whispered. “It teaches me not to trust you.”

For a heartbeat, the chamber trembled.

Not violently.

Like a laugh.

Then the Avatar’s smile widened.

“Good,” it said.

And Haruto hated that the word sounded like approval.

“That,” the Avatar finished, “is also within acceptable parameters.”

The vision snapped shut.

Stone returned.

Noise returned.

The cyan tab returned to the edge of his vision like a bookmark he couldn’t remove.

And Haruto realized, with a cold clarity that felt like waking up in a hospital:

The world wasn’t asking him to consent.

It was telling him what it had already decided he would pay.
