## Codex 02 — Architect Hands

Haruto did not open the codex the way you open a book.

He opened it the way you open a wound you keep checking to make sure it’s still real.

The cyan tab waited at the edge of his vision, pulsing with the same quiet patience as an autosave icon—never asking permission, never apologizing, merely *being there* as if it had always belonged to him.

He told himself he wouldn’t.

He told himself he should focus on not dying in a dungeon that had opinions about teamwork.

He told himself he’d already learned enough to ruin a person’s sleep for the next decade.

The tab pulsed once.

And Haruto, because he was human and stubborn and deeply cursed by curiosity, selected it anyway.

The world blinked.

This time, the void did not show him Lumora turning beneath twin suns.

It showed him a table.

Not a metaphorical table. A literal one—vast, curved, made of something that looked like brass until the light moved and revealed it was not metal, not stone, not even matter in the ordinary sense.

It was a *surface of rules*.

It hummed with lines and symbols and pale cyan threads that drifted above it like dust motes caught in a projector beam. Some lines intersected. Some refused.

Above the table, the silhouettes returned.

The Architects.

They did not look like people when you had the luxury of staring for longer than a heartbeat. They looked like the concept of hands made into bodies—precision given posture.

And then Haruto realized why the codex had chosen a table.

Because this wasn’t a vision of worship.

It was a workshop replay.

> **ARCHITECT PRACTICE**  
> Reality is not holy.  
> Reality is editable.  
> The World is a construct with an attitude problem.

Haruto’s mouth went dry at the last line.

That last line felt… new.

Not ancient.

Not myth.

Like a note written by a tired engineer who had spent too long debugging a planet.

The replay shifted closer.

On the table, a shape hovered—unfinished, incomplete. A cuff. A partial lattice. Something between jewelry and restraint.

Haruto’s wrist prickled in sympathetic recognition.

The Architects circled it without speaking. Their heads tilted in angles that implied calculation rather than curiosity. Their too-jointed hands moved in synchronized increments, as if each motion was part of a larger equation the codex wasn’t showing him.

Then the codex provided a translation that didn’t feel like language.

It felt like a tooltip.

> **THE THREE VERBS**  
> The Architects did not cast spells.  
> They applied operations.  
> **Build** — add structure.  
> **Destroy** — remove structure.  
> **Move** — change position without loss.

Haruto stared.

He’d heard “Build / Destroy / Move” in Codex 01, but seeing it written like that—clean, clinical—scraped against every fantasy trope his brain wanted to use as armor.

Build.

Destroy.

Move.

Not heal.

Not bless.

Not curse.

Not even “attack” or “defend.”

Just… operations.

The Architects raised their hands.

The air over the table trembled.

And the shape *changed*.

No sparks.

No fire.

No dramatic surge of music.

The cuff gained a strut, an inner ring, a thin brace along the underside that looked like a tendon made of geometry. Cyan lines crawled along the new structure like veins filling with blood.

Haruto’s gamer brain tried to label it anyway.

*Crafting.*

But the codex corrected him without malice.

> **BUILD**  
> Adds rules.  
> Adds constraints.  
> Adds intention.

The replay cut, almost like a jump in a tutorial.

Another table.

Another construct.

This one was a bridge—an elegant arc of brass-colored lattice spanning a canyon on the raw Lumora from Codex 01. It glowed faintly in cyan, as if the bridge remembered its own blueprint.

An Architect gestured.

The bridge vanished.

Not collapsed.

Not shattered.

Simply *removed*, like an object deleted from a map editor.

Haruto flinched anyway, because his body did not trust power that clean.

> **DESTROY**  
> Removes rules.  
> Removes constraints.  
> Removes intention.

And then the canyon… did not remain a canyon.

The empty space where the bridge had been did something worse.

The world corrected itself.

Rock surged upward, filling the gap with raw stone as if nature had been waiting impatiently for the Architect’s hand to leave.

Haruto stared with a sick fascination.

Destroy didn’t create destruction the way a bomb did.

It removed architecture and forced the world to resolve the absence.

Like deleting a support beam and letting gravity pick the ending.

The codex returned him to the workshop.

The cuff hovered again over the rule-table.

Now a fourth element appeared: a faint, translucent overlay shaped like a hand—five fingers, but each finger split into branching lines, and the palm was a lattice rather than flesh.

The overlay pressed itself down over the cuff.

The cuff flared cyan.

The Architects’ heads tilted in unison.

Haruto felt a cold bead of dread slide down his spine.

Because the overlay did not feel like a glove.

It felt like a key.

> **HANDPRINT KEY**  
> The Gauntlet is locked to a pattern.  
> Not a name.  
> Not a bloodline.  
> A *method*.

The replay zoomed in until Haruto could see the cuff’s inner ring.

There were grooves there.

Not decorative.

Not aesthetic.

Instruction grooves.

Thin channels that looked like fingerprints rendered as circuitry.

He’d seen something like that before.

Not in fantasy.

In real life.

Capacitive touch sensors. Authentication patterns. The way a phone knows your thumb.

His breath stuttered.

“This is…” he tried, but the codex didn’t care about his voice.

It offered him the next line as if it were a warning label.

> **THE GAUNTLET DOES NOT OBEY POWER.**  
> The Gauntlet obeys *operations*.

Haruto’s mind flashed to Sunny’s tutorial pop-ups, to how the System treated him like a user, not a hero.

To the way the Watcher had watched.

To the way the dungeon had reacted when he tried to cheat.

He had been thinking of the world as a game with lore.

The codex insisted it was a machine with history.

The Architects returned to their construct.

One of them—if “one” was meaningful when they moved like a synchronized thought—held the cuff and made a motion like turning a dial.

The cuff *shifted*.

Not moved through space.

Moved in… state.

Its cyan veins dimmed.

Then flared.

Then dimmed again, as if cycling through modes Haruto didn’t have the vocabulary to name.

> **MOVE**  
> Relocates without loss.  
> Converts damage into distance.  
> Converts breakage into a new position where breakage is no longer required.

Haruto’s stomach dropped.

That line landed too close to things he’d done.

Too close to moments in the dungeon where he’d survived by being somewhere else at exactly the right time.

Too close to the way the gauntlet had dragged him through worlds like a relocation spell wearing the skin of a summoning.

The replay changed again.

The Architects stood in a chamber deep within Lumora’s raw crust. The World Core pulsed nearby, a colossal heart of cyan light trapped in a lattice of brass geometry.

The cuff hovered between Architect hands.

They held it toward the core.

The core’s beat slowed.

Haruto felt it in his teeth.

The cuff’s veins brightened in response, matching the pulse.

Then the Architects *stopped* moving.

They held still with the precision of a machine waiting for a prompt.

The codex displayed a line that was less poetry and more procedure.

> **CALIBRATION**  
> The Gauntlet must recognize the hand that uses it.  
> Not the wearer.  
> The *verb*.

Haruto frowned despite himself.

Not the wearer.

Then what—anyone could put it on?

Any hand could become the hand?

The codex answered by showing him a sequence that felt like a test.

An Architect placed the cuff on a stone pillar.

Another Architect approached and reached for it.

The cuff flashed.

The hand passed through it like fog.

No resistance.

No damage.

Just… refusal.

Then a third Architect reached.

The cuff did not refuse.

It *accepted*.

The lattice tightened and conformed, locking to the hand’s overlay pattern like a password being entered correctly.

Haruto’s pulse quickened.

So it wasn’t random.

Not bloodline.

Not worthiness.

Not fate.

Compatibility with a method.

And that—more than any prophecy—terrified him.

Because methods could be replicated.

Stolen.

Learned.

The codex provided the next sentence like a note on a blueprint.

> **If the handprint key is copied, the world can be edited by unauthorized hands.**

Haruto’s breath caught.

That was the first time the codex sounded…

Afraid.

The replay shuddered, as if the file had a corrupt segment.

For a fraction of a second, Haruto saw something else:

Not Architects.

Not the World Core.

A different chamber.

Modern, in the way a ruin could be modern if it remembered a future.

Rows of dormant constructs.

And a single figure standing before them, wearing a smile that didn’t belong on any face that understood what it was looking at.

Then the codex snapped back to the workshop.

Haruto’s skin crawled.

The codex pretended nothing had happened.

The cuff was lowered into the cyan lattice of the World Core again, fed into the heart’s infrastructure like a tool being stored for later use.

The Architects’ hands withdrew.

But the replay did not end.

Instead, it zoomed in on the moment their hands left the cuff.

On the absence.

On the fact that, for the first time, the cuff hovered in the lattice without a hand above it.

Alone.

Waiting.

Haruto had the irrational impulse to apologize to it, which told him a lot about how badly his sense of reality had been damaged lately.

Then the codex displayed a final line, small and cold and practical.

> **A world cannot be repaired by the hands that broke it.**  
> When the next Equinox arrives, the Gauntlet will seek a hand that is not part of the system that failed.

The vision released him.

Stone ceiling.

Dungeon hum.

Party noise.

Haruto’s own breath—sharp, startled, too loud.

He looked down at his wrist.

The cuff sat there like a simple item.

Like a “legendary.”

Like a reward.

But now he could see it differently.

Not as a weapon.

As a user interface for editing a world that could not afford the wrong hands.

The cyan tab pulsed at the edge of his vision.

Not a heartbeat this time.

A prompt.

Like the system was asking:

*Do you understand what you are holding yet?*
