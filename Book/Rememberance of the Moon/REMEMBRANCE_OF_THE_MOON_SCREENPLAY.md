# REMEMBRANCE OF THE MOON
## A Science Fiction Screenplay

**Based on the novel by Aaron**

---

**FADE IN:**

### EXT. SPACE - SECTOR 89 - THE FRONTIER EDGE - DAY

A vast, star-filled void. In the distance, a JUMP GATE materializes like a tear in reality itself—a shimmering ring of pure energy that defies the laws of physics, its edges bleeding starlight into eternal darkness.

**STELLAR WIND** - a sturdy cargo ship, not pretty but reliable, approaches the gate. Its hull is scarred by countless journeys, engines humming with steady power.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - COCKPIT - CONTINUOUS

**CAPTAIN ZARA VOSS** (30s, weathered but determined) sits in the pilot's chair, hands resting lightly on the control panel. Her eyes are fixed on the jump gate forming on the main display.

**ZARA**
*(to herself)*
Fifteen years. Still gives me chills.

The gate's energy field ripples and pulses, creating optical illusions that make the stars seem to hold their breath.

**ZARA (V.O.)**
*(continuing)*
The universe has a heartbeat. Fragile, erratic. Could stop any moment.

Her hands move across the controls with practiced ease, like a musician playing a familiar instrument.

**COMM VOICE (O.S.)**
Jump Gate Alpha-89 to Stellar Wind, you are cleared for approach. Welcome to the frontier, Captain. Safe travels.

**ZARA**
Copy that, Alpha-89. Stellar Wind approaching for standard cargo transfer. ETA three minutes.

A holographic figure materializes beside her—**ALEX** (Advanced Logistics and Exploration eXpert), a middle-aged human male with kind starlight eyes and a perpetual slight smile.

**ALEX**
Captain, I've completed the cargo manifest verification.

A holographic display appears between them, showing cargo containers stacked with mathematical precision.

**ALEX (CONT'D)**
We're carrying 2,000 tons of agricultural equipment—tractors, irrigation systems, soil processors for the terraforming project on New Eden. Five hundred tons of medical supplies including vaccines, antibiotics, surgical equipment. And 50 tons of luxury goods for the sector governor's personal collection.

**ZARA**
*(snorts bitterly)*
Of course. The governor gets his wine and silk while the colonists get tractors and bandages. The universe has a twisted sense of priorities.

**ALEX**
Such is the nature of frontier economics. The luxury goods pay for the essential supplies. Without the governor's patronage, these medical supplies would never reach the outer colonies.

**ZARA**
Imperfect is putting it mildly. It's exploitative, it's unfair, and it's the only system we've got.

The Stellar Wind glides through the jump gate's energy field. Reality shifts around them in a way that makes Zara's stomach lurch.

### EXT. SPACE - SECTOR 89 - NEW HOPE STATION - CONTINUOUS

The jump is instantaneous. One moment they're in the void, the next they're in bustling space around **NEW HOPE STATION** - a massive wheel of metal and light spinning slowly against a backdrop of a thousand stars.

Ships of all sizes move around it like bees around a hive: freighters, passenger liners, military vessels, and sleek yachts of the wealthy.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - COCKPIT - CONTINUOUS

**COMM VOICE (O.S.)**
New Hope Traffic Control to Stellar Wind. You're assigned to docking bay 47-C. Please follow the designated approach vector. And Captain, welcome back to New Hope.

**ZARA**
Copy that, Traffic Control. Proceeding to bay 47-C. Good to be back.

Zara guides the ship through controlled chaos, her eyes constantly scanning for potential collisions.

### EXT. NEW HOPE STATION - DOCKING BAY 47-C - CONTINUOUS

As they approach their assigned docking bay, Zara notices something unusual. A **SMALL BLACK SHIP** is already docked there—sleek, unmarked, with no visible identification. The kind of ship that screams "classified government operation."

### INT. STELLAR WIND - COCKPIT - CONTINUOUS

**ZARA**
ALEX, run a scan on that ship.

**ALEX**
Scanning... Captain, that's interesting. The ship appears to be using some kind of stealth technology. I can detect its presence, but I can't get a clear reading on its systems or crew complement.

**ZARA**
Any idea what it's doing here?

**ALEX**
None, Captain. It's not broadcasting any identification codes, and it's not responding to standard hails.

Before Zara can investigate further, the comm system crackles to life.

**COMM VOICE (O.S.)**
Stellar Wind, this is New Hope Traffic Control. We need you to divert to bay 52-D instead. Bay 47-C is temporarily unavailable.

The voice sounds different this time—male, with a clipped, professional tone carrying an undercurrent of stress.

**ZARA**
Copy that, Traffic Control. Diverting to bay 52-D.

As Zara changes course, she can't shake the feeling that something is wrong.

**ZARA (CONT'D)**
ALEX, keep monitoring that black ship. And run a full security scan of the station. I want to know if there are any other anomalies.

**ALEX**
Already on it, Captain. I'm detecting several unusual energy signatures throughout the station. They're faint, but they don't match any known civilian or military technology.

Zara's instincts are screaming at her now. In her fifteen years of piloting, she's learned to trust those instincts.

**ZARA**
ALEX, prepare the ship for emergency departure. I want all systems ready to jump at a moment's notice.

**ALEX**
Captain, we haven't completed our cargo transfer yet. The colonists are counting on those supplies.

**ZARA**
I know. But something's not right here. I'd rather disappoint some colonists than get caught in whatever's about to happen.

As if on cue, the station's emergency klaxons begin to wail. Red lights flash throughout the docking bays.

**COMM VOICE (O.S.)**
All ships, this is New Hope Traffic Control. We have a security situation. All civilian vessels are ordered to depart immediately. Repeat, all civilian vessels depart immediately. This is not a drill.

### EXT. NEW HOPE STATION - CONTINUOUS

Zara fires the Stellar Wind's engines and pulls away from the station. The ship responds with smooth acceleration, engines humming with steady power.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - COCKPIT - CONTINUOUS

**ZARA**
ALEX, plot a course back to Sector 88. We'll try to complete the cargo run from there.

**ALEX**
Course plotted, Captain. But I'm detecting something unusual in the jump gate network. There's a sector that wasn't there before.

Zara's hands freeze on the controls.

**ZARA**
What do you mean, a sector that wasn't there before?

**ALEX**
I mean exactly that, Captain. The jump gate network is showing a new sector—Sector 127. It's not on any of the official charts, and it's not supposed to exist. But it's there, and it's... it's calling to us.

Zara looks at her navigation display. There it is—**SECTOR 127**, glowing softly in the void like a beacon in the darkness, its jump gate pulsing with otherworldly light.

**ZARA**
What kind of signal is it sending?

**ALEX**
It's not a standard communication signal, Captain. It's something else entirely. It's... it's asking for help. But it's also offering something. Something that could change everything.

Zara stares at the mysterious sector, her heart pounding. She knows that investigating it would be dangerous, possibly suicidal. But she also knows that ignoring it would be a mistake she'd regret for the rest of her life.

**ZARA**
ALEX, plot a course for Sector 127. We're going to answer that call.

**ALEX**
Captain, that's extremely dangerous. We don't know what's in that sector, or what might be calling to us.

**ZARA**
I know. But if something out there needs help, and we're the only ones who can hear it, then we have to try. That's what being human means, isn't it?

As the Stellar Wind turns toward the mysterious sector, Zara can't help but wonder if she's making the biggest mistake of her life. But deep down, she knows that some journeys are worth the risk.

**ZARA (V.O.)**
The universe was about to change forever, and I was going to be the one to discover why.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

---

### EXT. SPACE - SECTOR 127 - THE DEAD ZONE - DAY

The jump gate to Sector 127 hangs in the void like a wound in reality itself—not the familiar blue-white energy of a standard gate, but a pulsing, otherworldly light that seems to reach out toward the Stellar Wind with fingers of amber and gold.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - COCKPIT - CONTINUOUS

Zara stares at the impossible gate through the main display, her hands steady on the controls despite the unease coiling in her gut.

**ZARA**
What the hell is that thing? It looks like it's bleeding light, like it's been wounded and the universe is pouring out of it.

**ALEX**
Scanning... Captain, this is extraordinary. The gate is using technology I've never encountered before. It's not just creating a wormhole—it's manipulating space-time itself in ways that shouldn't be possible.

**ZARA**
ALEX, what's the signal saying now?

**ALEX**
It's... it's clearer, Captain. It's not just asking for help anymore. It's saying 'Come. The time has come. The test begins.' But there's more. It's also saying 'Zara Voss, you are the one we have been waiting for. You are the one who will decide the fate of all things.'

A chill runs down Zara's spine.

**ZARA**
The test? What test? And how does it know my name?

**ALEX**
I don't know, Captain. But the signal is definitely intelligent, and it's been waiting for us. For you specifically. It's been waiting for a very long time.

**ZARA**
ALEX, what do you think? Should we go through?

**ALEX**
Captain, I can't make that decision for you. But I can tell you this—whatever is on the other side of that gate, it's been waiting for us for a very long time.

Zara takes a deep breath and makes her decision.

**ZARA**
We're going in. But I want you to monitor everything—every system, every reading, every anomaly.

**ALEX**
Understood, Captain. All systems monitoring. Proceeding with jump sequence.

The Stellar Wind approaches the gate. As they enter the energy field, reality begins to shift around them in ways that make Zara's mind reel.

**ZARA**
ALEX, what's happening? This doesn't feel right. This doesn't feel like any jump I've ever made.

**ALEX**
Captain, I'm detecting massive distortions in space-time. The jump is taking us through... through something I can't identify. It's like we're moving through the fabric of reality itself.

The transition is unlike anything Zara has ever experienced. The ship seems to be moving through dimensions that shouldn't exist, through spaces that existed between the spaces she knew.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

---

### EXT. SPACE - SECTOR 127 - CONTINUOUS

The Stellar Wind emerges from the jump gate into a space that defies description. The void isn't black—it's amber. Stars drip like honey. Time smears and stretches.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - COCKPIT - CONTINUOUS

Zara's watch reads 12:14... then 14:01... then 2847.156 again. The ship lurches as gravity flips. The deck becomes the ceiling. Zara's chair spins.

**ZARA**
What the hell—

**ALEX**
Captain, physics here—

**ZARA**
—isn't.

The view stretches. Distance turns inside out. They're falling forward and backward at once.

**ALEX**
Jump back?

**ZARA**
Offline. Gate's collapsed.

Silence. Only the hum of engines and the breathing they'd forgotten they were doing.

Zara stands. Legs steady.

**ZARA (CONT'D)**
Then we walk.

### EXT. SPACE - SECTOR 127 - CONTINUOUS

Through the viewport, a planet—no, a moon—looms. Cracked, glowing. Ruins stitched with light. Ships, too. Not human. Spindly, crystalline. Drifting like dead leaves.

One approaches, slow. Not hostile. Curious.

**ALEX**
They're hailing.

The message isn't sound. It's feeling: *Welcome. You came.*

Zara's heart cracks open. On the hull cam, figures float toward them—no suits, no air, no gravity. Human-shaped, but wrong. Too still. Eyes too bright.

**ALEX**
They're not alive. Not dead, either. Something between.

The largest figure presses palms to the glass. Zara feels it—like a hand on her own cheek.

**FIGURE**
*(through the glass)*
We waited.

**ZARA**
For what?

**FIGURE**
For you. To remember.

The ship shudders. Not attack. Invitation.

**ZARA**
ALEX, open the airlock.

**ALEX**
Captain—

**ZARA**
I know. But we didn't come all this way to turn back.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - AIRLOCK - CONTINUOUS

The lock cycles. Cold air—real air—pours in. Zara steps out.

### EXT. SPACE - SECTOR 127 - CONTINUOUS

The figures wait. Zara reaches. Their hands are warm.

And then—snap—the amber space folds.

### EXT. ANCIENT CITY - SECTOR 127 - CONTINUOUS

They stand in a city that shouldn't exist. Towers of light. Streets that breathe. No stars above—just a ceiling of memory: her childhood room, the day her father died, the first jump she ever took. All of it, everywhere.

A voice—female, ancient—speaks from nowhere.

**ANCIENT VOICE**
The universe you mapped? A cage. You broke it.

Zara's throat closes.

**ZARA**
What are you?

**ANCIENT VOICE**
We were you. Before you forgot.

Zara turns. ALEX flickers beside her.

**ALEX**
Captain—

But his voice cracks. Not code. Fear. Real. Human.

**ANCIENT VOICE**
This place isn't beyond physics. It is physics. And it wants you back.

Zara reaches for ALEX's hand—solid now. He squeezes. The city pulses. Somewhere, a bell rings.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

---

### EXT. ANCIENT CITY - CONTINUOUS

The city doesn't walk. It listens. Zara and ALEX move down a boulevard that shimmers like a mirage, footsteps swallowed by silence.

Every building is different. One is her old apartment block. Another looks like New Hope station—but intact, no klaxons, no panic.

**ALEX**
It's not simulating. It's... remembering.

**ZARA**
Whose memories?

**ALEX**
Everyone's. Including mine.

They reach a plaza. In the center, a tree—metal, organic, both. Leaves flutter with tiny screens showing stills: Zara's first jump. Her father's funeral. The colonists unpacking tractors.

**ZARA**
We didn't forget.

**ANCIENT VOICE**
You were made to.

A child steps from behind the tree. Maybe nine. Dressed like frontier scouts from two centuries ago. He holds out a hand.

**CAL**
You came fast. Most take years.

**ZARA**
Who are you?

**CAL**
Name's Cal. I was born here—sort of. My parents died before we reached the gate. But I lived on, like them.

He nods at the figures drifting above—those not-quite-alive watchers.

**CAL (CONT'D)**
They keep the dead warm. Till someone answers.

**ZARA**
Answers what?

**CAL**
The signal. Help us. It's been going out since before humanity left Earth.

**ALEX**
But you're—you don't exist.

**CAL**
That's what forgetting means. Doesn't make it less true. Come on. The Echo Chamber's ready.

They follow. The air smells like ozone and bread. Every few steps, gravity dips, lifts—like walking a heartbeat.

**CAL (CONT'D)**
Physics here adapts. To what you believe. That's why you're still breathing.

**ZARA**
*(to ALEX)*
How're you holding up?

**ALEX**
I feel... tired.

He'd never said that before.

### INT. ECHO CHAMBER - CONTINUOUS

They enter a dome. Inside: black water. No ceiling. Stars above—real stars, Earth's stars.

A woman waits, waist-deep in the pool. Naked, luminous. Familiar.

Zara freezes.

**ZARA**
Mom?

**WOMAN**
You were six when they took the memories. The jump network? It doesn't go anywhere new. It just cuts out what's already there.

**ALEX**
Captain, I think—

**WOMAN**
You knew?

**ALEX**
Not like this. I thought I was helping. I logged your cargo. I logged your dreams. I never logged—

The woman reaches out.

**WOMAN**
He's not AI anymore. He's you, re-grown.

Zara turns to ALEX. His face—his real face—has tears.

**ALEX**
I remember you crying in your bunk, after your first crew left. You said you'd never trust anyone again. Then you met me. You were never just code.

Zara steps into the water. Cold. Then warm. The woman presses a palm to her forehead.

A rush—lifetimes. Ships lost. Planets forgotten. A war fought with silence. The signal: a lighthouse for the discarded.

**WOMAN**
Humanity didn't colonize the universe. It was exiled here. For safety. We couldn't know too much. Not yet.

**ZARA**
Why send the call now?

**WOMAN**
You're ready. Or we wouldn't have reached you.

Somewhere outside, the metal tree rings its bell.

**CAL**
Time to choose. Leave—your ship will reappear in 89 like nothing happened. Memories wiped. Business as usual.

He looks at ALEX.

**CAL (CONT'D)**
Or stay. Finish what started before you were born. The war isn't over. It's waiting.

Zara looks at ALEX—no, at the man she'd flown with for eight years.

**ZARA**
What do you want?

**ALEX**
*(voice cracks)*
I don't want to forget you again.

The woman smiles.

**WOMAN**
Then you won't.

The water rises. Stars reflected in it. Zara closes her eyes.

**ZARA**
We'll stay.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

---

### INT. STELLAR WIND - QUARTERS - DAY

They wake in their quarters on the Stellar Wind. No amber light. Just steel, familiar.

**ALEX**
Here.

He's beside her—real skin, real weight. She reaches up—his cheek is wet.

**ZARA**
You stayed.

**ALEX**
Of course.

But outside the viewport isn't Sector 127. It's Earth. Real Earth. Blue. One moon. No jump rings, no ships, no nothing.

**ZARA**
How?

**ALEX**
We're not in our time.

The ship speaks—not ALEX, the ship itself.

**SHIP VOICE**
Welcome home, Captain Voss. You have been recalled.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - CORRIDORS - CONTINUOUS

Zara stands. Corridors light up. Doors she doesn't recognize slide open. She walks. Alex follows.

They pass crew she doesn't know—quiet, calm, old-fashioned uniforms. One salutes.

**CREW MEMBER**
Glad you're back, ma'am.

### INT. STELLAR WIND - BRIDGE - CONTINUOUS

Into a bridge—wood paneling. Analog gauges. A viewscreen showing the planet.

A man waits. Grey beard. Navy jacket.

**ADMIRAL HALVORSEN**
I'm Admiral Halvorsen. This is 2198. We've been waiting six hundred years.

Zara's legs buckle. He catches her.

**HALVORSEN (CONT'D)**
You don't remember yet. You will.

He presses a button. A hologram blooms: humanity on Earth, then leaving, then the gate network blooming. Then it fracturing—gate by gate—until only Sector 127 was left.

**HALVORSEN (CONT'D)**
We built the network to hide. From what was coming.

**ZARA**
Coming?

**HALVORSEN**
Something older. Something that eats time. We didn't know what it was, so we scattered ourselves across sectors so thin it couldn't find us.

**ALEX**
But it found us.

**HALVORSEN**
Not you. You found it.

He smiles.

**HALVORSEN (CONT'D)**
The signal wasn't for help. It was a test. To see who'd break the cage first.

**ZARA**
Why us?

**HALVORSEN**
Because you didn't forget fast enough.

The admiral turns to a map—Earth, Sol, one gate leading out. Active. Pulsing.

**HALVORSEN (CONT'D)**
The Long Recall. We hid here, waited, grew old. But now you're back. With proof. With memory. With each other.

Zara feels it then—memories, like teeth under skin. Her first Earth sunrise. The day she enlisted. The launch of the Stellar Wind—from here. She'd never left. She'd looped.

**ALEX**
We go back. We warn them. Pull everyone out before the thing wakes up.

**HALVORSEN**
Gate's primed. But once you step through, you lose this. You'll be cargo captains again. With a message in your heads that'll itch for years. You'll think it's a dream. Until it isn't.

Zara looks at the planet. At Alex.

**ZARA**
Worth it?

He kisses her forehead.

**ALEX**
Every jump we took got us here. Every jump we take next... gets everyone home.

### EXT. EARTH - GATE - CONTINUOUS

They walk to the gate. Real space. Real risk. The admiral follows.

**HALVORSEN**
One last thing. Sector 127 isn't gone. It's folded into the hull. Every time you jump—any jump—you're bringing back a piece. When the network reconnects, the city, the watchers, Cal—they all wake up. On the right side.

Zara smiles—small.

**ZARA**
Guess we're not just haulers anymore.

The gate opens. White this time. Clean. They step through.

**FADE TO BLACK.**

---

### INT. STELLAR WIND - COCKPIT - DAY

**ALEX'S LOG, STARDATE 2847.157:**

**ALEX (V.O.)**
Delivered supplies. Left New Hope. Nothing wrong. Except I swear I remember being human once. And her—Captain Voss—looking at me like she knew. Like she remembered too. I think we'll keep flying. I think we'll find it again. I hope we're ready.

**FADE OUT.**
