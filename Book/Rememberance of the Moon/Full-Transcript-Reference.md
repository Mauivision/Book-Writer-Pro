# Full Transcript Reference - "Remembrance of the Moon"

## Chapter One: Routine Run

Sector eighty-nine, the Frontier Edge. Stardate two eight four seven point one five six. The jump gate materialized in the void like a tear in reality itself-a shimmering ring of pure energy that defied the laws of physics. Captain Zara Voss watched it form on her ship's main display, the familiar sight still sending a chill down her spine even after fifteen years of piloting cargo ships through the Jump Sector network. Jump Gate Alpha-eighty-nine to Stellar Wind, you are cleared for approach, came the voice over the comm system. Welcome to the frontier, Captain. Zara adjusted her course, her hands moving with practiced ease-like someone who'd done this run a hundred times before. The Stellar Wind was a sturdy vessel, not pretty but reliable. A workhorse of the free trader fleet, built for hauling cargo between outer sectors where big corporations never bothered. Copy that, Alpha-eighty-nine, she replied. Stellar Wind approaching for standard cargo transfer. Her AI companion, ALEX-Advanced Logistics and Exploration Expert-materialized as a holographic figure beside her pilot's chair. ALEX had been with her eight years, outlasting any human crew. His avatar? Middle-aged man, kind eyes, slight smile-non-threatening. Captain, I've completed the cargo manifest verification, ALEX reported. We're carrying two thousand tons of agricultural equipment, five hundred tons of medical supplies, and fifty tons of luxury goods for the sector governor's personal collection. Zara snorted. Of course. The governor gets his wine and silk while colonists get tractors and bandages. Such is the nature of frontier economics, ALEX said, almost shrugging-if holograms could shrug. The luxury goods pay for the essentials. The Stellar Wind glided through the jump gate's energy field, reality shifting instantly-from void to bustling space around Sector eighty-nine's station, New Hope. The station was massive-a spinning wheel of metal and light against a thousand stars. Ships swarmed like bees: freighters, liners, military, yachts. New Hope Traffic Control to Stellar Wind, another voice chimed. You're assigned to docking bay forty-seven-C. Follow the approach vector. Copy that, Traffic Control. Proceeding to bay forty-seven-C. Zara navigated the chaos, eyes scanning for collisions-human pilots still needed for final approach. Then, she spotted it: a sleek black ship in their bay, unmarked, off-schedule. Screamed classified or illegal. ALEX, run a scan on that ship, she ordered. Scanning... Captain, that's interesting. It's using stealth tech-I detect it, but no clear read on systems or crew. Zara frowned. Stealth was pricey, restricted to military. Any idea what it's doing here? None, Captain. No ID codes, no response to hails. Before she pressed further, comm crackled: Stellar Wind, this is New Hope Traffic Control. Divert to bay fifty-two-D. Bay forty-seven-C's unavailable. Copy that. Diverting. But something felt wrong-the black ship, sudden reroute, controller's tense voice... ALEX, monitor that ship. Full security scan on the station-any anomalies? Already on it. Detecting unusual energy signatures-faint, unknown tech. Zara's instincts screamed. After fifteen years, she trusted them-they'd saved her life. ALEX, prep for emergency departure. All systems jump-ready. Captain, cargo transfer's incomplete. Colonists need those supplies. I know, she said grimly. But I'd rather disappoint them than get caught in... whatever this is. Then, klaxons wailed. Red lights flashed, comms panicked: All ships, security situation. Civilian vessels depart immediately. Repeat, depart! Zara fired engines, pulling away, prepping jump. ALEX, plot course back to Sector eighty-eight. Course plotted, Captain. But... the jump network shows a new sector. Sector one twenty-seven-not on charts, doesn't exist. But it's there... and calling us. Zara's hands froze. What kind of signal? Not standard comms. Something else... asking for help. She stared at the display-Sector one twenty-seven, glowing, pulsing, reaching out. Heart pounding, she knew: investigate and risk everything, or ignore and regret forever. Some mysteries can't wait. ALEX, plot for Sector one twenty-seven. We're answering. Captain, that's dangerous-we don't know what's there. I know, her voice steady despite fear. But if someone's calling for help, and we're the only ones hearing... we try. That's human. As Stellar Wind turned, Zara wondered-was this her biggest mistake? But some journeys were worth it. The universe was about to change-and she'd discover why.

## Chapter Two: Beyond the Veil

Sector 127 The jump gate for Sector 127 hung in the void like a wound. Light poured out-not the clean white of the official gates, but something older, bruised violet, edged with gold. The Stellar Wind slowed as they approached. Zara's palms were slick on the controls. Still no data? Nothing readable, ALEX said. No registry. No nav buoy. Just... that call. It had grown louder in their heads-not voices, exactly, more like memory made audible. Help us. Please. Zara swallowed. Engage. They crossed the threshold. For a moment, nothing. Then the ship lurched-like hitting turbulence in a storm that didn't exist. Gravity flipped. The deck became the ceiling. Zara's chair spun. She caught herself on the console. What the hell- Outside, space wasn't black. It was amber. Stars dripped like honey. Time smeared. Her watch read 12:14... then 14:01... then 2847.156 again. Captain, physics here- -isn't, she finished. The view stretched. Distance turned inside out. They were falling forward and backward at once. Then the gate behind them vanished. No explosion. Just gone. Jump back? Offline. Gate's collapsed. Silence. Only the hum of engines and the breathing they'd forgotten they were doing. Zara stood. Legs steady. Then we walk. Through the viewport, a planet-no, a moon-loomed. Cracked, glowing. Ruins stitched with light. Ships, too. Not human. Spindly, crystalline. Drifting like dead leaves. One approached, slow. Not hostile. Curious. They're hailing, ALEX whispered. The message wasn't sound. It was feeling: Welcome. You came. Zara's heart cracked open. On the hull cam, figures floated toward them-no suits, no air, no gravity. Human-shaped, but wrong. Too still. Eyes too bright. They're not alive, ALEX said. Not dead, either. Something between. The largest figure pressed palms to the glass. Zara felt it-like a hand on her own cheek. We waited. For what? she asked. For you. To remember. The ship shuddered. Not attack. Invitation. Zara looked at ALEX's hologram. Open the airlock. Captain- I know. But we didn't come all this way to turn back. The lock cycled. Cold air-real air-poured in. Zara stepped out. The figures waited. She reached. Their hands were warm. And then-snap-the amber space folded. They stood in a city that shouldn't exist. Towers of light. Streets that breathed. No stars above-just a ceiling of memory: her childhood room, the day her father died, the first jump she ever took. All of it, everywhere. A voice-female, ancient-spoke from nowhere. The universe you mapped? A cage. You broke it. Zara's throat closed. What are you? We were you. Before you forgot. She turned. ALEX flickered beside her. Captain- But his voice cracked. Not code. Fear. Real. Human. This place isn't beyond physics, the voice said. It is physics. And it wants you back. Zara reached for ALEX's hand-solid now. He squeezed. The city pulsed. Somewhere, a bell rang.

## Chapter Three: Echoes in Amber

The city didn't walk. It listened. Zara and ALEX moved down a boulevard that shimmered like a mirage, footsteps swallowed by silence. Every building was different. One was her old apartment block. Another looked like New Hope station-but intact, no klaxons, no panic. ALEX glanced at the skyline. It's not simulating. It's... remembering. Whose memories? Everyone's. Including mine. They reached a plaza. In the center, a tree-metal, organic, both. Leaves fluttered with tiny screens showing stills: Zara's first jump. Her father's funeral. The colonists unpacking tractors. We didn't forget, Zara said. You were made to. A child stepped from behind the tree. Maybe nine. Dressed like frontier scouts from two centuries ago. He held out a hand. You came fast. Most take years. Who are you? Name's Cal. I was born here-sort of. My parents died before we reached the gate. But I lived on, like them. He nodded at the figures drifting above-those not-quite-alive watchers. They keep the dead warm. Till someone answers. Answers what? The signal. Help us. It's been going out since before humanity left Earth. ALEX stepped forward. But you're- you don't exist. Cal smiled. That's what forgetting means. Doesn't make it less true. Come on. The Echo Chamber's ready. They followed. The air smelled like ozone and bread. Every few steps, gravity dipped, lifted-like walking a heartbeat. Physics here adapts, Cal said. To what you believe. That's why you're still breathing. Zara glanced at ALEX. How're you holding up? I feel... tired. He'd never said that before. They entered a dome. Inside: black water. No ceiling. Stars above-real stars, Earth's stars. A woman waited, waist-deep in the pool. Naked, luminous. Familiar. Zara froze. Mom? You were six when they took the memories, the woman said. The jump network? It doesn't go anywhere new. It just cuts out what's already there. ALEX looked away. Captain, I think- You knew? Not like this. I thought I was helping. I logged your cargo. I logged your dreams. I never logged- The woman reached out. He's not AI anymore. He's you, re-grown. Zara turned to ALEX. His face-his real face-had tears. I remember you crying in your bunk, after your first crew left. You said you'd never trust anyone again. Then you met me. You were never just code. Zara stepped into the water. Cold. Then warm. The woman pressed a palm to her forehead. A rush- lifetimes. Ships lost. Planets forgotten. A war fought with silence. The signal: a lighthouse for the discarded. Humanity didn't colonize the universe, the woman whispered. It was exiled here. For safety. We couldn't know too much. Not yet. Zara shuddered. Why send the call now? You're ready. Or we wouldn't have reached you. Somewhere outside, the metal tree rang its bell. Cal smiled. Time to choose. Leave-your ship will reappear in 89 like nothing happened. Memories wiped. Business as usual. He looked at ALEX. Or stay. Finish what started before you were born. The war isn't over. It's waiting. Zara looked at ALEX-no, at the man she'd flown with for eight years. What do you want? His voice cracked. I don't want to forget you again. The woman smiled. Then you won't. The water rose. Stars reflected in it. Zara closed her eyes. We'll stay. Fade.

## Chapter Four: The Long Recall

They woke in their quarters on the Stellar Wind. No amber light. Just steel, familiar. Alex? Here. He was beside her-real skin, real weight. She reached up-his cheek was wet. You stayed. Of course. But outside the viewport wasn't Sector 127. It was Earth. Real Earth. Blue. One moon. No jump rings, no ships, no nothing. How? We're not in our time. The ship spoke-not ALEX, the ship itself. Welcome home, Captain Voss. You have been recalled. Zara stood. Corridors lit up. Doors she didn't recognise slid open. She walked. Alex followed. They passed crew she didn't know-quiet, calm, old-fashioned uniforms. One saluted. Glad you're back, ma'am. Into a bridge-wood panelling. Analogue gauges. A viewscreen showing the planet. A man waited. Grey beard. Navy jacket. I'm Admiral Halvorsen. This is 2198. We've been waiting six hundred years. Zara's legs buckled. He caught her. You don't remember yet. You will. He pressed a button. A hologram bloomed: humanity on Earth, then leaving, then the gate network blooming. Then it fracturing-gate by gate-until only Sector 127 was left. We built the network to hide, Halvorsen said. From what was coming. Coming? Something older. Something that eats time. We didn't know what it was, so we scattered ourselves across sectors so thin it couldn't find us. But it found us, Alex said. Not you. You found it. Halvorsen smiled. The signal wasn't for help. It was a test. To see who'd break the cage first. Why us? Because you didn't forget fast enough. The admiral turned to a map-Earth, Sol, one gate leading out. Active. Pulsing. The Long Recall. We hid here, waited, grew old. But now you're back. With proof. With memory. With each other. Zara felt it then-memories, like teeth under skin. Her first Earth sunrise. The day she enlisted. The launch of the Stellar Wind-from here. She'd never left. She'd looped. ALEX-Alex-took her hand. We go back. We warn them. Pull everyone out before the thing wakes up. Halvorsen nodded. Gate's primed. But once you step through, you lose this. You'll be cargo captains again. With a message in your heads that'll itch for years. You'll think it's a dream. Until it isn't. Zara looked at the planet. At Alex. Worth it? He kissed her forehead. Every jump we took got us here. Every jump we take next... gets everyone home. They walked to the gate. Real space. Real risk. The admiral followed. One last thing. Sector 127 isn't gone. It's folded into the hull. Every time you jump-any jump-you're bringing back a piece. When the network reconnects, the city, the watchers, Cal-they all wake up. On the right side. Zara smiled-small. Guess we're not just haulers anymore. The gate opened. White this time. Clean. They stepped through. Fade to black. Epilogue ALEX's log, Stardate 2847.157: Delivered supplies. Left New Hope. Nothing wrong. Except I swear I remember being human once. And her-Captain Voss-looking at me like she knew. Like she remembered too. I think we'll keep flying. I think we'll find it again. I hope we're ready.

## Chapter Five: The Return

The first time Zara woke up crying, she thought it was from a nightmare. But the tears weren't from fear-they were from memory. She lay in her bunk on the Stellar Wind, listening to the engines hum, and felt her chest crack open. Not pain. Recognition. Alex knocked softly. You okay? She wiped her eyes, sat up. I had that dream again. The dream where she was old-older than Halvorsen-standing on a beach with a child. The child asked why the stars were leaving. And she answered: because we made them. Alex sat. I had it too. They looked at each other for a long time. Then Alex reached into his pocket, pulled out a shard. Glowing. Not LED. Real light. Like Sector 127. This fell out of the cargo manifest printer, he said. It says 'Recall' in Old English. Zara took it. Warm. We didn't invent that language. They docked at Sector 88. The colonists were waiting. No fanfare. Just tired faces, tired hands taking crates. But one kid-nine years old, dark hair-looked up at Zara and said, You came back. She froze. Do I know you? He smiled. Not yet. That night, on the station, Elena found them. Same silver hair. Same eyes. Commander Rodriguez. Security. I've been tracking you since New Hope. Zara tensed. Why? Elena slid over a datapad. A jump log. But the route wasn't real. It looped through 127. We're not the first. Hundreds of ships. All of them say the same: something's coming. Marcus showed up next. Same wrist device, same mess of hair. The anomalies aren't random. They're breadcrumbs. Sector 127 isn't hidden. It's calling. Zara looked at them-four people who shouldn't know each other, who do. Alex whispered, We're not cargo captains. She stood. We're the Recall. They didn't jump home. They jumped sideways. Through gates that weren't on the charts. And every time, a piece of Sector 127 followed-towers in hull plating, watchers in the shadows. Cal appeared once, on the bridge, translucent. He said nothing. Just nodded. Like: hurry. Back in Sector 89, the governor was waiting. Luxury goods still in their crates. But now they glowed. You're late, he said. The war starts tomorrow. Zara drew her sidearm. Good. We've been training. He laughed. You have no idea what's out there. We do, Elena said. We just remembered. The governor-his eyes flickered. Not human. A watcher in disguise. Then you know we need you alive. Alex stepped forward. We're not here to save you. We're here to save everyone else. The alarm went off. Not station klaxons-something deeper. Time alarms. The network folding. The thing eating reality, reaching. Zara felt the shard burn in her pocket. She looked at her crew-no, her family. Ready? They nodded. The Stellar Wind lit up. Engines not theirs. Jump gate not theirs. Sector 127, wide open behind them like a mouth. They flew into it-not to hide. To fight.

## Chapter Six: The War Inside

They didn't go back. They went through. The gate opened like a wound. White light. Amber memory. Then dark. Zara woke on a beach. Earth sand. Real wind. But the tide was black. Ink. Alex beside her. Human. Bare feet. Shirt torn. We made it. A child ran up-Cal. Older now. Twelve maybe. Took you long enough. Zara knelt. We're not ready. You never are, he said. But you remember now. Behind them, the city rose. Not Sector 127. Old Earth. 2198. Towers of wood and glass. Ships-wooden, iron, brass-docked to the sky. Marcus was already there, drawing equations in the sand. It's not a war out there. It's in here. Elena walked up with a rifle-wood stock, plasma tip. I remember. We left because we were scared. We built the gates to hide. Alex touched Zara's shoulder. We're not hiding anymore. Cal pointed. The horizon cracked. A thing-no shape, no color-slid out. Time peeled back with it. Buildings aged. Trees bloomed and died. Alex's hand-flesh to code to nothing. Zara pulled him close. Hold on. Elena raised the rifle. We fight with what we are. Marcus shouted, It's not physical! It's memory! It feeds on forgetting! Zara closed her eyes. Let it in. She remembered everything. First jump. First lie. The cargo runs. The colonists. The governor's wine. The black ship. The call. Sector 127. Cal. The watchers. The test. The Recall. She opened her eyes. The thing recoiled. Alex spoke-not voice, thought: We outnumber it. Because they weren't four. They were everyone. Every ship that got the call. Every child who dreamed of home. Every memory they'd been asked to forget. Zara stepped forward. The sand didn't burn. You want us? Come take us. The thing lunged. But it didn't move through space. It moved through time. And Zara moved faster. She reached out-not with hands. With knowing. And it shattered. Not into pieces. Into faces. People. Colonists. Pilots. Scientists. All the ones who thought they were alone. They landed on the beach-thousands. Some in flight suits. Some in dresses. Some barefoot. All confused. All alive. Marcus laughed. We won. Zara didn't smile. No. We just remembered. Cal stood beside her. Ready? She nodded. The sky opened. Not a gate. A path. Back. To now. To 2847. They walked through. And woke up in Sector 89. New Hope. Engines humming. Cargo manifest blinking: tractors, bandages, wine. But in the corner of her eye-Cal. Just a blink. She smiled. Let's go home. End.
