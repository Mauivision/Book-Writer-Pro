# Chapter 6 — Flame Letters

The first letter is to his father and it does not begin with Dear. He folds the paper once, twice, as if tightness could spare him. On the balcony the wind tastes like pennies and rain. The city below clicks its teeth. He holds the page to a match and watches the orange happen—how quickly black remembers everything it was asked to forget. Smoke rises like writing in the old languages.

He speaks aloud because the body trusts sound. “I wanted you to be gentler,” he says, and the words feel too soft for the man they are meant for. “I wanted you to be gentle with me.” The flame gnaws a corner and eats its way toward the sentence about the river and the day he left for school without saying I love you because it sounded theatrical strapped to a boy’s throat. He coughs, and the cough is not a refusal but a release.

By the time he writes to Mira, the rain has started. He writes I’m sorry for the way I made you carry my quiet and for the time I walked past the mural and said nothing and called it restraint. He writes I’m sorry for the nights I measured our love in cost and not in light. He writes nothing about being forgiven. The letter curls and brightens and licks his fingers with a quick kiss.
