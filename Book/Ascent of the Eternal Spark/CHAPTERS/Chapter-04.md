# Chapter 4 — Murals in the Alley

By day the alley is a seam; by evening it becomes a throat singing light. The mural spills across brick—nebula on nebula, a weather of color. The street artist works with fingers again. Paint moons his nails. He dots white into the blue as if baptizing the dark.

Elias leans against a dumpster that smells like the city’s stomach. “Do you ever plan it?” he asks.

The artist laughs. “Planning is what I do until the wall tells me I’m wrong.” He drags a rag of violet through a spiral and the shape changes its mind. “You build,” the artist says. “So you know. The good stuff arrives when you stop making it behave.”

Elias thinks of the alternative sketch in his folder. He thinks of the way the firm’s language straightens anything that curves. “Buildings can’t fall,” he says. “So we teach them to stand.”

“Standing isn’t the same as living,” the artist says, not unkindly. He rinses his hands with water from a bottle. The runoff makes a small galaxy at Elias’s feet.

Wind slides down the alley like a hand and the smell of turpentine becomes the smell of rain. A woman with a canvas bag stops, looks up, and her face rearranges itself into the expression of someone who has just remembered a song she loved as a child. Two teenagers take photos and then put their phones down, as if the phones had been a mistake.

“Why the space stuff?” Elias asks.

The artist crosses his arms, paint smearing like a constellation. “Because it’s the one subject where I can be both honest and wrong and the sky forgives me. Also because I like the way blue makes people quiet.” He glances at Elias. “You’ve got too much noise.”

“I know,” Elias says. He does not add that the noise is not only the city but the voice inside that filed wonder as expense, tenderness as risk.

When he walks away, a thread pulls from the mural and ties itself to his sternum. It does not hurt. It reminds.

In his apartment, he pulls the alternative sketch out like a contraband fruit. He reads the lines the way the artist reads brick. For the first time in months, the building in his mind has a pulse.
